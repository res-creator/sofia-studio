TIKTOK CAPTION
In 1932, Australia sent soldiers with two Lewis machine guns to fight emus eating the wheat in Western Australia 🪶 The gun jammed, the emus outran the truck, and the Major's own report came out at exactly 10 rounds per bird. What finally worked was a cash bounty paid to farmers. Incentives or firepower: which would you bet on?

PINNED COMMENT
Sources: the operation ran Nov 2 – Dec 10, 1932 near Campion, Western Australia, under Major G.P.W. Meredith (Royal Australian Artillery) with two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds (Australian Geographic; Murray Johnson, Journal of Australian Studies, 2006). Around 20,000 emus were reported in the area. At the dam on Nov 4, about a dozen birds were killed before the gun jammed. Meredith's report claimed 986 kills with 9,860 rounds; other accounts put the number much lower. Military help was refused again in 1934 and the bounty system (in place since 1923) continued: 57,034 bounty claims in six months of 1934. Numbers vary between sources; the figures above are as reported.

YOUTUBE SHORTS
Title: Australia sent machine guns to fight emus. Here's what actually worked.
Description: The Great Emu War, Western Australia, 1932. Wheat farmers, many of them WWI veterans hit by the Depression, faced around 20,000 emus. The army sent a small Royal Australian Artillery unit under Major G.P.W. Meredith with two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds. A gun jammed at the dam, the emus outran a gun mounted on a truck, and the Major reported 986 kills with 9,860 rounds (other accounts were lower). The government refused more troops in 1934 and kept paying farmers a bounty: 57,034 claims in six months.
