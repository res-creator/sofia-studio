PINNED COMMENT
Sources: CNN (Feb 2017), quoting UPS's Jack Levis: "We will make left hand turns, but not ones that are unnecessary", and UPS's routing software "can give an undesirable left turn a penalty that adds 20 seconds to the estimated route time" (it's a cost in the calculation, not a penalty for the driver). UPS, ORION announcement (Nov 2013): one mile less per driver, per day, is worth up to $50 million a year — that's about total miles, not only left turns. MythBusters tested a delivery truck in San Francisco: the right-turn-only route was longer but used less fuel (one test, not a universal rule). These are historical figures (2013–2017); UPS's routing tools have changed since. Mike and his route are an illustration.

YOUTUBE SHORTS
Title: Why UPS Trucks Almost Never Turn Left
Description: UPS drivers often take three right turns instead of one left. Why? UPS's route software treats an unnecessary left turn as a cost: waiting for a gap in oncoming traffic, with the engine running, costs time and fuel. In 2017 UPS said the software can add a 20-second penalty to the estimated route time for an undesirable left — a cost in the math, not a penalty for the driver. UPS doesn't ban left turns: when a left makes more sense, the system can choose it. MythBusters tested the idea with a delivery truck: the right-turn route was longer but used less fuel (one test). UPS has said that one mile less per driver, per day, is worth up to $50 million a year (2013 estimate, about total miles). Mike is a fictional example driver.
