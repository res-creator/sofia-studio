This is a country you think you know. But the moment you land, everything you expected starts to fall apart. Five a.m. The city is still asleep. But someone is already setting out tiny plastic stools on the pavement. Within an hour, half the street will be sitting on them, drinking coffee for about a dollar. Here's the first surprise. A train once left twenty seconds early, and the railway company formally apologized. Twenty seconds. In other places, that wouldn't even count as late. Now, the numbers. Nearly one and a half billion people. Seventy million motorbikes. Over twenty trillion kroner in a single oil fund. And yet, the average monthly wage is about three hundred and fifty dollars. How is that possible? Why does a country that lands on the Moon still struggle to give every child a desk at school? And why do so many people fall in love with it on the very first day? Let's start at the very beginning. The river is calm in the morning. Boats drift past the old stone steps, and the light turns everything gold. A woman washes a bright red sari. Two boys jump into the water, laughing. Somewhere, a bell rings. But look closer. Behind the beauty, there's a quieter story. Rent that takes half a salary. Families of four in a room the size of a parking space. Long hours, and almost no time off. That's what we came to see. Not the postcard. The real thing. So, is it really cheap? Is it really perfect? Or is it something much more complicated, and much more interesting? Stay with us until the end. The answer might surprise you. Thank you for flying with us. Fasten your seatbelts.
