DESCRIPTION
In 1902, people in Hanoi were cutting the tails off live rats... and letting them go. Because the city was paying for tails.
This video is about one strange rule: every time someone pays for a number, people find the cheapest way to make the number. We see it with rats in colonial Hanoi, with Hoover's free-flights promotion, with the "pudding guy" and his airline miles, with the Wells Fargo fake-accounts scandal, with Mexico City's "Hoy No Circula" driving ban — and once, with emus in Australia, where paying people actually worked.
Illustrated re-enactment with generic characters (not portraits of real people). Brand names are used only to tell the true story.
