PINNED COMMENT
Sources: JAMA (2019) on the slogan's origin — dietitian Lenna Cooper wrote in 1917 in Good Health, the magazine of John Harvey Kellogg's Battle Creek Sanitarium, that "in many ways the breakfast is the most important meal of the day." Kellogg co-invented corn flakes (HuffPost). The 1920s Beech-Nut Packing bacon campaign by Edward Bernays: his agency's doctor favored a heavy breakfast and wrote to 5,000 physicians; Bernays later said "we got about 4,500 answers — all of them concurred," newspapers reported that doctors urged hearty breakfasts, and he said Beech-Nut's bacon sales went up (Penn CHIBE, Barry Popik quoting his memoir, Today I Found Out). The survey numbers come from Bernays' own account; a 1922 newspaper credited a similar survey to a medical journal without naming him, so accounts differ. Not nutrition advice. All people in the video are generic drawings (re-enactment), not portraits; brand names appear as plain text only.

Title: Who Said Breakfast Is the Most Important Meal? (Not Doctors)
Description: The line "breakfast is the most important meal of the day" first appeared in 1917 in Good Health, a magazine published by the Battle Creek Sanitarium of John Harvey Kellogg, the man who co-invented corn flakes. In the 1920s, the meatpacker Beech-Nut Packing hired PR man Edward Bernays to sell more bacon. Instead of buying ads, he asked one doctor whether a light or a heavy breakfast was better ("heavy"), then that doctor wrote to 5,000 physicians — about 4,500 agreed, at least as Bernays told it. Newspapers ran the story, and the heartiest breakfast on the plate was bacon and eggs. The doctors never picked the menu; the bacon seller did — and, he said, his sales went up. Not nutrition advice.
