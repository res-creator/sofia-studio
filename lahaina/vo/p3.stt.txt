Just after 6:30 in the morning on August 8th, the wind brought down power lines near Lahainaluna Road on the upper edge of town. A resident named Shane True called 911. The lines had sparked the dry grass. Firefighters fought the blaze for hours in the wind. By around nine, it was declared contained. At about 2:18 in the afternoon, after nearly eight hours on scene, the crews left. The fire chief would later say it had been fully contained and extinguished. Thirty-four minutes later, it was burning again. Shortly before 3:00, a Hawaiian Electric crew working nearby saw a small fire and called 911. Federal investigators would call it a rekindle. Something in the morning burn area was still alive, a spark carried by the wind, smoldering material moved by heavy equipment, or a root burning slowly underground. Even after a year of work, investigators could not rule out any of the three.
