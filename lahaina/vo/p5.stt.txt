For many people, there was only one direction left. They ran toward the ocean. Some sheltered behind the seawall. Others went into the water to escape the heat and the smoke as the town burned behind them. Around five forty-five, the Coast Guard learned that people were in the ocean. Boat crews from Station Maui spent the night searching the smoke. They pulled seventeen people from the water and helped forty more survivors ashore. At seven nineteen that evening, Andaya told state officials he believed only one business had been lost. By then, much of Lahaina was gone. The fire would not be fully contained for twenty-six days.
