And then there was the water. As firefighters pulled hoses into the streets, the hydrants began to lose pressure. One Lahaina firefighter put it simply: there was just no water in the hydrants. It wasn't that Lahaina had run out of water. The system was bleeding. Inside every burning house, plastic pipes melted and broke open. Water poured out of burning homes all over town, and the pressure in the hydrants collapsed. From the air, help was impossible. The gusts were too strong for helicopters to drop water. On the ground, firefighters were fighting a firestorm with almost nothing
