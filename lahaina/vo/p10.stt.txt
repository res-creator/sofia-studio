The Lahaina fire is often described as a natural disaster, a freak wind, a perfect storm. But almost every part of it had been seen before. The dry grass was on the hills for years. The twenty eighteen fire burned right next door. The wind was forecast days in advance. The sirens were on their poles. The fire was declared out, and the crews went home. Lahaina did not burn because nobody could see the danger. It burned because so many small warnings were never put together. And on August eighth, eighty sirens stayed silent.
