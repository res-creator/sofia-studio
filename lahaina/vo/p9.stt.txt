One hundred and two people died in the Lahaina fire. The youngest was seven. The oldest was ninety-seven. Most were older. The median age was sixty-eight. At least sixty-five of them were trying to get out. Some were found in their homes, some in their cars, some outdoors in the streets they knew. Eighty-two of them died in central Lahaina. Seven died at a single senior housing complex. More than two thousand buildings were damaged or destroyed. Most of them were homes. At one point, three hundred and eighty-eight people were listed as missing. The last victim died in March 2024, seven months after the fire. Nearly eight thousand people spent months living in hotels. Three years later, Lahaina is still rebuilding. By August 2026, a few hundred homes had been rebuilt. On historic Front Street, rebuilding had barely begun. But in the old town square, the banyan tree is still standing. Half of its branches died in the fire. The other half is growing back.
