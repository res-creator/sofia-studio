# Lahaina Fire, August 8, 2023: verified fact sheet

Compiled 2026-11-02. Every bullet comes from a page I read. "P1", "P2" and "P3" are the Attorney General / FSRI Phase 1, 2 and 3 reports. P2 bullets were read in the full report PDF: https://d1gi3fvbl0xj2a.cloudfront.net/2024-09/FSRI_Lahaina_Report__Redactions_09102024.pdf (cited below as [P2 PDF]). **CONTESTED** marks points where sources disagree.

## 1. Background
- Kamehameha III lived on Mokuʻula, an island in Mokuhinia pond in central Lahaina, from 1837 to 1845. He proclaimed the first Hawaiian constitution at Lahaina in 1840 and moved the capital to Honolulu in 1845. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii]
- Mokuhinia was a 17-acre spring-fed wetland pond, and Mokuʻula was a royal burial site. Sugar cane operations diverted mountain stream water in the late 1800s, which dried up the pond's source. In 1919 the county filled the site in as a park with a baseball field. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokuʻula]
- 19th-century whaling stop for water and food. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii]
- Front Street was the heart of the Lahaina Historic District. The American Planning Association named it one of its "Top Ten Greatest Streets" in 2011. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii]
- The banyan tree was planted April 24, 1873, by William Owen Smith, to mark 50 years of Christian missionaries in Lahaina. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii] AP gives the same reason and says the tree came from India, is more than 60 ft tall and spans nearly an acre. [https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/nation/a-deadly-fire-charred-lahainas-historic-banyan-tree-a-year-later-its-been-nursed-back-to-life]
- "One of the driest places in Hawaii" (about 14.6 in of rain a year). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii]
- Grasses: guinea, molasses and buffel grass are African species brought in as livestock forage. Almost a quarter of Hawaii's land is now covered by invasive grasses and shrubs. Plantations declined from the 1990s, and the last sugar plantation closed in 2016. An estimated 85% of the land burned in the 2018 West Maui fire was nonnative shrubland. [https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/how-swaths-of-invasive-grass-made-mauis-fires-so-devastating-180982729/]
- Guinea grass grows about 6 in a day and burns with flames up to 20 ft (forester Michael Walker). [https://www.nbcnews.com/science/science-news/maui-wildfires-grass-overgrown-nonnative-flammable-hawaii-rcna99963]
- 2018 fire: the Kauaʻula Valley fire was reported at 12:45 a.m. on Aug 24, 2018, as Hurricane Lane approached. It burned 1,500 acres and damaged or destroyed 21 homes and about 27 vehicles. Deputy Chief Lionel Montalvo called the conditions "unprecedented in our department's history." [https://mauinow.com/2018/08/27/conditions-unprecedented-in-battling-west-maui-fires-during-hurricane-warning/]
- P2 finding: the Maui Fire Department (MFD) and Maui Police Department (MPD) never started an after-action report on the 2018 fire, and the Maui Emergency Management Agency (MEMA) never finished one. Before 2023, sirens had never been used to warn of a wildfire, including in 2018. [P2 PDF]
- The 2014 Western Maui Community Wildfire Protection Plan was signed June 5, 2014. [https://mauinow.com/2014/06/05/west-maui-community-wildfire-protection-plan-formalized/] NBC reports that a 2014 Hawaii Wildfire Management Organization report rated Lahaina's fire risk as extreme. [NBC link above] GovTech quotes the plan: fire safety zones in West Maui "are yet to be determined." [https://www.govtech.com/em/deadly-fire-exposes-mauis-shocking-lack-of-preparation] *I could not open the plan PDF itself (the DLNR link returns 404), so I have not seen the "extreme" rating on the original page.*
- Wikipedia says a 2021 state report rated wildfire risk "low." [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hawaii_wildfires] *I have not seen the 2021 report itself.*

## 2. The forecast
- Hurricane Dora was a Category 4 storm that stayed more than 700 miles from the islands. The pressure gradient between a strong high to the north and Dora to the south drove the winds. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dora_(2023)] AP/PBS put the distance at about 500 miles south. [https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/hawaiian-wildfires-cause-power-outages-and-evacuations-as-strong-winds-complicate-fight] **CONTESTED (distance).**
- **CONTESTED (how much Dora mattered):** National Hurricane Center specialist Philippe Papin said Dora had only a minor role, "slightly enhancing low-level flow over Maui at fire initiation time." [Dora Wikipedia link above]
- NWS warning text: the gradient "will drive very strong and gusty easterly winds across the area." It forecast NE winds of 25–45 mph with local gusts over 60 mph, and warned: "Damaging winds could blow down trees and power lines." The High Wind Watch was later upgraded to a warning. [https://mauinow.com/2023/08/06/high-wind-watch-to-bring-gusty-winds-as-dora-passes-safely-south-of-hawaiʻi/]
- The P2 forecast-verification table lists these products and release times. Red Flag Warning products: Aug 7 at 3:15 a.m. (valid 8/7 6 a.m. to 8/9 6 a.m., east winds 30–45 mph with gusts over 65 mph), 3:30 a.m. and 4:42 a.m. High Wind Warning (valid through 8/9 6 a.m., "damaging winds may down trees/powerlines"): Aug 8 at 3:18 a.m. [P2 PDF] Wikipedia instead says the Red Flag Warning was issued at 5:00 a.m. Aug 7. [2023 Hawaii wildfires Wikipedia link] **CONTESTED (exact issue times).**
- Measured winds: P2 says winds on Aug 8 "ranged between 60-80 mph" and verified gusts of up to 80 mph. [P2 PDF] The MPD report describes 60–80 mph winds. [https://www.civilbeat.org/2024/02/maui-police-report-reveals-locations-of-lahaina-fire-victims-and-lessons-learned/]

## 3. Timeline of August 8 (Hawaii Standard Time)
- **Ignition, CONTESTED by a few minutes.** The MFD/ATF report says "a single fire ignited by re-energized, broken utility lines at 6:34 a.m. near utility pole No. 25." [https://mauinow.com/2024/10/02/lahaina-wildfire-ruled-accidental-caused-by-downed-power-line-that-ignited-dry-vegetation/] P1 news coverage gives 6:35 a.m., near Kuialua Street and Hookahua Place. [https://www.wcax.com/2024/04/18/first-park-hawaiis-report-deadly-lahaina-wildfire-highlights-communications-failures-including-lack-emergency-alerts/] AP gives 6:37 a.m., when resident Shane Treu called 911. [https://www.kpbs.org/news/environment/2023/08/28/hawaii-power-utility-takes-responsibility-for-first-fire-on-maui-but-faults-county-firefighters]
- **"Contained," CONTESTED:** NBC and ABC say "100% contained" shortly before 9 a.m. [https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/maui-wildfires-timeline-fires-created-chaos-rcna99967] AP says 9:55 a.m. [KPBS link above] A county situation report said 90% contained around 8:19 a.m. [Civil Beat MPD link]
- Crews left at about 2:18 p.m., after nearly eight hours on scene. Fire Chief Brad Ventura said the fire was then "fully contained and extinguished." [Maui Now Oct 2 2024 link]
- **Flare-up, CONTESTED:** the ATF puts the "rekindle event" at 2:52 p.m., in a gully next to the morning burn area. [Maui Now Oct 2 2024 link] P2 says the PM fire ignited at 14:55, jumped the Lahaina Bypass at about 15:21, and the first homes on Lahainaluna Road caught fire at 15:30. [P2 PDF] Hawaiian Electric says its crew saw a small fire "shortly before 3 p.m." and called 911. [KPBS link] Wikipedia gives 3:30 p.m. [Wikipedia]
- Roads, from radio and text logs: at 3:23 p.m. an officer reported, "I'm blocking off the Bypass headed into Lahaina right at Lahainaluna." At 3:30 p.m. Andaya texted, "Closing Lahaina Bypass road. Fire jump over to the roadway." At 3:52 p.m. an officer reported, "Front Street at a standstill." [https://mauinow.com/2024/04/22/critical-minutes-timeline-details-early-emergency-response-to-lahaina-wildfire/]
- Power: at 4:11 p.m. the MPD log reads "We need to shut power down." At 4:11:54 dispatch reported "MECO is saying it is shut off." [Maui Now critical-minutes link] Hawaiian Electric says its West Maui lines had been de-energized for more than six hours before the afternoon fire. [KPBS link] **CONTESTED:** a resident told AP their power came back around 2 p.m. [summary of AP via search, not verified on the page]
- Cell service: high winds knocked out cell towers early in the day. [https://www.wfae.org/united-states-world/2024-04-18/the-first-phase-of-the-lahaina-fire-report-details-timeline-of-disaster] The FCC reports 100% of cell sites in the area out of service Aug 9–12. 911 calls still got through on Aug 8, so the network was not completely down. The public safety radio system worked throughout. [P2 PDF]
- 4:24 p.m.: a civilian reported fire by the "Big Banyan Tree," 1.3 miles from the origin. [P1 PDF text] Officer 401 radioed, "And the fire jumped the road. Headed to Front Street." [Maui Now critical-minutes link] The first fatality was reported at 4:24 p.m. on Mill Street. [WCAX link]
- 4:43–4:46 p.m.: MPD body cameras show officers breaking through locked gates so residents could escape to the Bypass. [P2 PDF] Wikipedia says the fire crossed Hwy 30 into central Lahaina at 4:46 p.m. [Wikipedia]
- Gridlock and barricades: one utility pole blocked the northbound highway, and southbound traffic was being turned away from the fire. [Civil Beat MPD link] Survivors say utility crews and police cars blocked roads. Those who drove around the barricades survived. Maui County said the southern road out was clear at 5:20 p.m. [https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2023/08/23/in-deadly-lahaina-fires-many-in-vehicles-found-no-way-out-those-who-drove-around-barricades-survived/] Police Chief John Pelletier said officers did not close roads or stop people leaving. [https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2023/08/23/survivors-roads-blocked-raging-fire-swept-through-lahaina] **CONTESTED.**
- Shoreline: the Coast Guard was first notified at about 5:45 p.m. [ADN/AP link] Station Maui boat crews pulled 17 people from the water and helped 40 survivors ashore. [https://mauinow.com/2023/08/10/coast-guard-continues-response-to-west-maui-wildfire-search-and-rescue-continues/] (Some early AP coverage said 14. [PBS link])
- Night: at 7:19 p.m. Andaya told the state he believed only one business had been lost. [WCAX link] P1's review period ends at 08:30 on Aug 9. [https://fsri.org/research-update/lahaina-fire-comprehensive-timeline-report-released-attorney-general-hawaii] The fire was declared 100% contained on Sept 3, 2023. [https://mauinow.com/2023/09/03/26-days-later-the-lahaina-fire-is-now-100-contained/]

## 4. The sirens
- Hawaii's system has more than 400 sirens statewide, 80 of them on Maui. [search summary of CBS/AP, not opened] Civil Beat quotes the state's description: the "largest single integrated outdoor siren warning system for public safety in the world." [https://www.civilbeat.org/2023/08/maui-emergency-chief-defends-decision-not-to-activate-warning-sirens/]
- None were sounded. The county "provided no evidence of discussing siren activation." About 20% of sirens statewide are inoperable on any given day. Of the five sirens near Lahaina, only two (MA407 and MA412) were listed as operable, and they were "very likely inoperable" because cell service was intermittent. The P2 finding counts only one operable siren inside the burn perimeter. [P2 PDF]
- What was sent instead, by text, WEA and EAS (wireless and broadcast emergency alerts): 3:37 p.m. "ROAD CLOSURE: Lahainaluna Road is closed…"; 4:00 p.m. EAS/WEA evacuation for Kulalani (Upcountry); 4:10 p.m. EAS/WEA evacuation for Kelawea Mauka in Lahaina; 4:16 p.m. "WEA message: Evacuation for Kelawea Mauka." [Maui Now critical-minutes link] Of 71 residents P2 interviewed, 87% said they got no text notification. [P2 PDF]
- Andaya on Aug 16, 2023. Asked whether he regretted it: "I do not." "The public is trained to seek higher ground in the event that the siren is sounded." "Had we sounded the siren that night, we were afraid that people would have gone mauka… And if that's the case then they would have gone into the fire." [https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/national-international/maui-emergency-management-director-defends-decision-not-to-sound-sirens-in-fire/] Also: "We just don't use sirens for fires." [Civil Beat siren link]
- He resigned Aug 17, 2023, citing health reasons. [https://www.hppr.org/2023-08-17/the-embattled-leader-of-maui-countys-emergency-management-agency-has-resigned] (WCAX wrongly says "fired.")
- P2: it "seems unlikely" people would have known what sirens meant, but they "may have provided an additional cue." [P2 PDF]

## 5. Water and aircraft
- P2: "household plumbing (e.g., plastic and other piping) inside of the buildings failed, allowing unrestricted flow of water," which "left many of the hydrants with little to no pressure." [P2 PDF] County water director John Stufflebean: "The water was leaking out of the system." Firefighter Keahi Ho: "There was just no water in the hydrants." [https://www.irishtimes.com/world/us/2023/08/14/hawaii-wildfires-fighting-a-losing-battle-with-the-blaze-as-lahainas-water-system-collapses]
- Kaleo Manuel, deputy at the state water commission, held up West Maui Land Co.'s request (sent by Glenn Tremble) to fill its reservoirs. Civil Beat reported he "stalled for hours" while seeking the views of downstream taro farmers. He was reassigned Aug 16. The Department of Land and Natural Resources said: "This deployment does not suggest that First Deputy Manuel did anything wrong." [https://www.civilbeat.org/beat/state-water-official-who-delayed-water-release-has-been-reassigned/] **CONTESTED:** his defenders say the reservoirs do not feed Lahaina's hydrants. He was reinstated in October 2023 and later resigned. [https://mauinow.com/2023/10/09/hawaiʻiʻs-water-commission-reinstates-1st-deputy-kaleo-manuel-to-maui-post/]
- Helicopters: "because of the wind gusts, helicopters weren't able to dump water on the fires" (county spokesperson Mahina Martin, via AP). [PBS link]

## 6. Cause and responsibility
- Hawaiian Electric (Aug 27, 2023) said the morning fire "appears to have been caused by power lines that fell in high winds." It called the county's lawsuit "factually and legally irresponsible." [KPBS link] CEO Shelee Kimura told Congress on Sept 28, 2023, that preemptive shutoffs were "not an appropriate fit for Hawaii": "We had other protocols in place." [https://foxbusiness.com/markets/hawaiian-electric-ceo-power-lines] Its first shutoff program (PSPS) began July 1, 2024: "This is our last line of defense." [https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/hawaiian-electric-starts-public-safety-power-shutoff-program-on-july-1]
- The MFD/ATF origin-and-cause report was released Oct 2, 2024. It ruled the fire "accidental." It found re-energized broken lines near pole 25 started the morning fire, and the 2:52 p.m. fire was a "rekindle." Possible causes of the rekindle were a wind-blown firebrand, heavy equipment moving smoldering material, or a buried root. None could be ruled out. Firefighters were not at fault. [Maui Now Oct 2 2024 link; https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2024/10/03/federal-investigation-absolves-maui-firefighters-points-hawaiian-electric/; report: https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/149693/FI23-0012446-Lahaina-Origin-and-Cause-Report_Plus-Appendix-A-B-C-Redacted]
- P1, the Comprehensive Timeline, released Apr 17, 2024. It assigns no causes, and the attorney general said it is "not to place blame on anybody." [FSRI P1 link; WFAE link]
- P2, the Incident Analysis, released Sept 13, 2024 (revised Oct 2, 2024). It has 84 findings and 140 recommendations, and says "no single factor" caused the disaster. Its three focus areas are risk perception, infrastructure standards and incident management ("siloed command structure"). [https://ag.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/News-Release-2024-40.pdf]
- P3, the Forward-Looking Report, released Jan 14, 2025. Its top priority is staffing an Office of the State Fire Marshal. [https://mauinow.com/2025/01/14/hawai%ca%bbi-ag-releases-third-and-final-forward-looking-report-on-lahaina-wildfire/; https://usfa.fema.gov/blog/phase-3-report-on-lahaina-fire-released]
- Settlement: announced Aug 2, 2024 to settle about 450 lawsuits. The seven defendants are the State, Maui County, Hawaiian Electric, Kamehameha Schools, West Maui Land Co., Hawaiian Telcom and Spectrum/Charter. [https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/4-billion-settlement-reached-mauis-deadly-destructive-wildfires-rcna162751] The total is $4.037B. The Hawaiʻi Supreme Court approved it in February 2025. The state's share is about $807.5M over fiscal years 2026–27 (HB 1001). [https://mauinow.com/2025/04/25/800-million-maui-wildfire-settlement-closes-in-on-final-approval-at-legislature/] Hawaiian Electric's share is about $1.99B, with no admission of liability. [search summary of AP, not opened] *I did not find a confirmed date for the first payments.*

## 7. The human toll
- 102 deaths are confirmed and all are identified. The 102nd was Claudette Heermance, 68, who died March 28, 2024, after hospice care. Two people remained on the missing list, down from a peak of 388. [https://www.civilbeat.org/2024/06/lahaina-wildfire-death-toll-now-at-102/]
- Victims were aged 7 to 97, with an average age of 65 and a median of 68. At least 65 of the 102 (64%) were trying to evacuate. 82 (80%) died in central Lahaina, between Kahoma Stream and Dickenson Street. Seven died at the Hale Mahaolu Eono senior complex. [P2 PDF] Louise Abihai, 97, is named in police releases. [https://www.voanews.com/a/hawaii-officials-identify-last-of-100-known-victims-of-wildfire-that-destroyed-lahaina/7459614.html; search summary]
- Where victims were found (P2 table): inside home 28, outside home 9, inside vehicle 15, beside vehicle 3, outdoors 27, inside another building 14, ocean or seawall 2, hospital 4. [P2 PDF] (P2's text says "17" were in or beside vehicles, but its table adds up to 18.) An earlier MPD report gave 15 in vehicles and 1 in the water. Pelletier said: "Not one person died in a car on Front Street." [Civil Beat MPD link]
- 2,207 structures were damaged or destroyed, out of 2,719 exposed, and 2,170 acres burned. 86% of exposed buildings were residential. The Pacific Disaster Center put "capital exposure" at $5.52B. [https://mauinow.com/2023/08/12/fema-map-shows-2207-structures-damaged-or-destroyed-in-west-maui-wildfire-5-52-billion-price-to-to-rebuild/] **CONTESTED:** FEMA says that number is not a FEMA damage estimate. [search summary of CNN] Governor Green said about $6B. [Wikipedia]
- Displacement: the state's hotel sheltering program housed 7,796 people in 3,071 households before it ended (reported July 12, 2024). [https://mauinow.com/?p=460811]
- It was the deadliest US wildfire since the 1918 Cloquet fire, which killed 453. [Wikipedia] The AG called it "the most deadly fire in North America in 100 years." [AG NR 2024-40]

## 8. Aftermath (latest: Oct 2026)
- By Aug 1, 2026, 557 homes had been rebuilt (county "Maui Recovers" dashboard). Not one commercial rebuilding permit had yet been issued on Front Street. The first, for 612 Front Street, targets a summer 2027 opening. Debris removal took "a little over a year." [https://mauinow.com/2026/08/02/3-years-after-the-lahaina-fire-rebuilding-front-street-no-longer-on-the-backburner/]
- Oct 2026: construction is starting on 60 homes at 1034 Front Street. [https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/10/03/first-homes-rise-lahainas-historic-district-more-than-three-years-after-fire/]
- Banyan tree, as of Aug 2024: about half its branches died. The rest is regrowing and some branches are fruiting, with sensors tracking sap flow. [AP/PBS banyan link] *I found no 2025–26 update.*
- Memorial: the county and Maui Public Art Corps opened the "Lahaina Think Space" from June 3 to July 23, 2026, to gather input for a permanent memorial. [https://mauinow.com/2026/05/22/lahaina-think-space-opens-at-queen-ka%ca%bbahumanu-center/]

## 10. Open loops (tease, then answer)
1. **The fire was "extinguished." Why did it come back?** Answer: the ATF called the 2:52 p.m. fire a rekindle and could not settle the mechanism: a firebrand, a bulldozer, or a buried root. [Maui Now Oct 2 2024 link]
2. **Why didn't 80 sirens make a sound?** Answer: nobody discussed using them. They had never been used for a wildfire, most near Lahaina were broken, and they rely on cell links that were failing. [P2 PDF]
3. **Hydrants ran dry, but did Lahaina run out of water?** Answer: no. Melting plumbing in thousands of burning homes drained the pressure. [P2 PDF; Irish Times]
4. **Who blocked the roads out?** Answer: falling poles, utility crews and police control points, with conflicting accounts. Survivors who drove around barricades lived. [ADN/AP; Civil Beat MPD; HNN Aug 23]
5. **Where was the man who could sound the alarm?** Answer: Andaya was at a conference on Oʻahu. At 4:03 p.m. he texted "Should I come home?" At 7:19 p.m. he thought one business had burned. [Maui Now critical-minutes link; WCAX]
