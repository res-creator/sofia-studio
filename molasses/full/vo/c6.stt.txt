Then came the cleanup. Workers sprayed the streets with saltwater from a fireboat and spread sand to soak up the molasses. Several hundred people worked for weeks to clear the area. The molasses didn't stay in the North End. It was carried across the city on shoes and clothes. It turned up on subway platforms, on streetcar seats, on telephones, in homes. As one account put it, everything that a Bostonian touched was sticky. The water of Boston Harbor stayed brown until the summer.
