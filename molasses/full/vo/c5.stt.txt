The first help came from the water. A training ship of the Massachusetts Nautical School, the USS Nantucket, was docked nearby. One hundred and sixteen cadets, led by Lieutenant Commander H.J. Copeland, ran to the scene. They waded straight into the molasses and began pulling people out. Boston police arrived. So did the Red Cross and men from the Army and the Navy. Red Cross nurses waded into the molasses as well. A makeshift hospital was set up nearby. One of the most common problems doctors saw was coughing. People had breathed in the molasses. The search went on for four days. Many of the dead were so covered in molasses that they were hard to identify. Some victims had been swept into the harbor. They were not found for three to four months. Twenty-one people died. They were workers, drivers, firefighters, neighbors. The youngest were two ten-year-olds, Maria DiStasio and Pasquale Iantosca. Among them was a firefighter, George Layhe. Several horses died, too, and about a hundred and fifty people were injured.
