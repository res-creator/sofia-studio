That winter had been bitterly cold. In the days before the disaster, the temperature had been around two degrees Fahrenheit. Then the weather turned. By January 15th, it was above 40 degrees. After weeks of freezing weather, it felt almost like spring. The day before, a ship had delivered a fresh load of molasses. That molasses had been warmed so it would flow more easily. Warm molasses poured into a tank full of colder molasses on a day when the air was suddenly mild. Some researchers believe the expansion inside the tank added to the strain on the steel. Liquids take up more space when they warm. Not much more, but in a tank holding more than two million gallons, a little is a lot. And this was a tank that was already leaking, already groaning, built from steel that, as we'll see, was never strong enough.
