DESCRIPTION
On January 15, 1919, a steel tank on Boston's Commercial Street split open. More than two million gallons of molasses poured into the North End in a wave that killed 21 people and injured about 150.

It sounds like a strange story. It was a warning that nobody wanted to hear. The tank had leaked for years. It groaned. The company painted it brown.

This documentary follows the whole story: how the tank was built, why it failed, the rescue, the names of the dead, the court case that followed, and what modern researchers found.
