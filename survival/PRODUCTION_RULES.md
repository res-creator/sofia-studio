# Survival — production rules (approved by the owner, 2026-10-18)

The channel makes tense animated survival stories that people want to watch to the end — not zombie cartoons.
These rules apply to every new Survival episode. The finished comedy episode "First 5 Days" (Gary) stays as it is.

## 1. Story
1. **Core of every story:** danger, survival, the hero's decisions and one **central mystery** that develops through the whole episode.
2. **Cause and effect.** Every important event follows from the previous one (often from the hero's own action). No random events just to fill time.
3. **Something changes every 30–60 s:** new information, a hard choice, a new danger or a twist. It does not have to be a chase.
4. **Alternate modes:** danger → exploration → decision → tense waiting. Never one long chase.
5. **The ending answers the main question** of the episode. A new mystery may open, but the story is complete. **No "TO BE CONTINUED"** cards.
6. **Do not stretch.** If the story is 7 minutes, the video is 7 minutes.
7. **Humor is rare and dry** (~15%), mostly in the narrator's lines. No slapstick gags or comic sound-effect lettering in serious episodes.
8. Before writing a scene, write down: what the viewer learns, what the hero does, and why the viewer wants to keep watching.

## 2. The hero acts
- The hero interacts with the world in every scene: opens, carries, hides, counts, writes, listens, chooses. Never "stands while the narrator talks".
- When the narrator lists things (supplies, rules, clues), the hero is physically doing it on screen.
- Reactions are believable: fear is shown with posture, breathing, hesitation — not cartoon takes.

## 3. Camera works for the story
| Technique | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Snap zoom in | danger, a sudden discovery |
| Slow push in | building tension |
| Snap zoom out | revealing the scale of the situation |
| Pan left/right | searching for danger, exploring a location |
| Tilt up/down | slowly revealing an important detail |
| Hero POV | exploring corridors and rooms (sparingly — costly in 2D) |
| Fast cuts | escape, attack, a sudden event |
| Locked-off still frame | tense waiting |

Good editing is the right moment for movement, not constant movement. Do not change the angle every two seconds.

## 4. Sound
- Atmosphere first: room tone, ship creaks, distant sounds, breathing.
- **Silence is a tool.** Music is not a constant bed; drop it before and during the scariest moments.
- One clear sound in silence (a click, a scrape, a footstep) beats a loud mix.
- Voices always intelligible: narrator and characters balanced, music ducked under speech, long drones trimmed with fades.

## 5. Look (serious D2)
- D2 is the technical base (rig, ink, cel shading). **Proportions and designs of approved characters are not changed without the owner's approval.**
- Serious staging: low-key lighting with one motivated light source, cold palette (blue-green night, red emergency), shadows and silhouettes, infected shown first as shadows or parts.
- Restrained faces: fewer big grins and "melting" eyes; more squints, tension, side glances. Fewer face close-ups, more hands, doors and objects.
- No comic overlays (KNOCK KNOCK, SQUEAK!, stamps) in serious episodes. Text on screen only for day markers and story-relevant signs.

## 6. Animation
- No repeated identical scenes or loops; new actions are added only when the story needs them.
- Hands on grip points, planted feet don't slide, QA reach check must be clean (existing rig QA).

## 7. Production
- Order: rules → script with scene table → cause/effect check → asset list → voice → animation → sound → QA → publish kit.
- Reuse what already works (rig, sets, camera, mixer); simplify a complex scene only if quality does not visibly drop.
- No API keys in code; ElevenLabs via the session proxy; max 2 concurrent requests, ~3 s apart.
- English only on screen and in audio; hashtags lowercase; no burned-in subtitles (upload an .srt).
