=== PINNED COMMENT ===
📌 Fact check: the 7½¢ coin was only a proposal — it was never made. Economists Daniel Levy & Andrew Young ("The Real Thing," Journal of Money, Credit and Banking, 2004) write that by 1950 Coca-Cola had about 400,000 vending machines, most of them nickel-only, and that in 1953 Robert Woodruff asked for a 7½¢ coin. The Eisenhower part comes from secondary accounts (Kahn 1969, Allen 1994); no letter has turned up in the archives, so treat it as "reportedly." Prices weren't 5¢ everywhere until the end — by 1955 a Coke cost 5¢, 6¢, 7¢ or even 10¢ depending on the area, and the nickel Coke was gone by 1959.
