# Script review: ep27 "Pizza for 10,000 Bitcoins" + ep28 "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower"

Reviewer pass, 2026-11-09. Checked against `CHANNEL.md` (US TikTok playbook, rule 13) and each episode's `facts.md`.
Channel data the scripts have to beat: 45–56 % swipe in the first second, another 25–30 % leave at 0:04–0:08 (the "line 3 goes to background" drop). Viewers who stay watch 72–78 %. Pace: Chris at speed 0.95 ≈ 150 words/min, so 2.5 words ≈ 1 s.

## 1. Ep27 — Pizza for 10,000 Bitcoins (draft v1)

### Scores (1–10)

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (frame 0 + first line) | 8 | Absurd picture + "2 PIZZAS = 10,000 BTC" + a strange true fact. This is the channel formula. Only weakness: the "TEN THOUSAND bitcoins" punch comes at the end of a 9-word line. |
| 0:04–0:08 retention | 4 | s02 spends the biggest number at about 0:03. Then s03 (about 0:05–0:10) is exactly the pattern rule 13 bans: name + "posted the offer" = background. |
| Pacing / new info every 3 s | 6 | s04 "nobody took it" and s05 are fine, but s06 ($41) comes at about 0:20. It belongs near the top, where it creates the contrast. s07–s09 slow down. |
| Curiosity gap / open loop | 4 | Hook, value and "most expensive" all land in the first 4 s. Nothing is left to wait for. No "and then it got worse" beat. |
| Payoff / twist | 4 | s09 "most expensive pizzas in history" just repeats the hook. The strongest money twist in facts.md is missing: he kept making the offer, then wrote "I can't really afford to keep doing it." |
| Ending + comment question | 5 | "Would YOU have taken the deal?" is unclear: which side, the buyer or the seller? Most viewers answer "yes, obviously", which ends the thread. |
| Clarity with sound off | 7 | Good tags. But s07/s08 ("pizza tower", "shrug") carry no number on screen, so muted viewers lose the thread. |
| Factual safety vs facts.md | 6 | See problem 3: $700M understates the verified $747M. "Nobody took it" for four days is not in facts.md. "Proved bitcoin could buy something real" and "most expensive pizzas in history" have no source. |

### 3 biggest problems

1. **s02 + s03: the payoff is spent and then the script switches to background at the drop-off point.** The $700M lands at 0:03. At 0:05 the voice says "Programmer Laszlo Hanyecz posted the offer himself…". That is a name + backstory line right inside the 0:04–0:08 window where 25–30 % leave. Fix: put the $41 → $1.26B contrast at 0:03–0:07. Make the third beat a *surprise about the hero* (he says he wrote the first GPU miner, so he was not a clueless newbie). Show the forum post only on screen.
2. **s07–s09: no money twist at the end.** The draft ends by restating the hook ("most expensive pizzas"). facts.md has a much better reversal, fully VERIFIED: he made the same offer again (June 12, 2010), and on Aug 4, 2010 he wrote: "I can't really afford to keep doing it." That is the funniest and most ironic money line in the whole story, and it is unused. Also unused: the pizzas cost about $25 while the coins were worth about $41. The teenager came out ahead even in 2010.
3. **s02, s04, s09: factual drift.**
   - s02: "around seven hundred million" vs facts.md: $747M (CoinGecko, Nov 9, 2026 13:54 UTC). Say "about $747 million" on screen and recheck on publish day. The verified peak ($1.26B, Oct 6, 2025) is the stronger, stable number for the voice.
   - s04: "For four days… nobody took it." facts.md only shows the offer on May 18 and the deal done on May 22 (finalized over IRC). Use "Four days later…".
   - s09: "Proved bitcoin could buy something real" and "most expensive pizzas in history" are not in facts.md. Use verified lines instead ("Bitcoin Pizza Day, every May 22", or the quotes).

## 2. Ep28 — The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower (draft v1)

### Scores (1–10)

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (frame 0 + first line) | 7 | A strong, famous claim + "SOLD" stamp. But the line opens with a date ("In 1925, …"). The playbook puts dates in a tag. And it states a LEGEND as plain fact. |
| 0:04–0:08 retention | 5 | s02 "He didn't own it" is good. But s03 (about 0:05–0:10) is classic exposition: "Posing as a government official, Victor Lustig invited scrap-metal dealers…" (participle clause + name intro + setup). This is the drop-off pattern. |
| Pacing / new info every 3 s | 7 | Many short beats (paid → vanished → again → fled → Alcatraz). Good rhythm after 0:10. |
| Curiosity gap / open loop | 6 | "Didn't own it" and "tried to sell it again" open small loops. There is no promise of a bigger ending. |
| Payoff / twist | 3 | s10 "The money was never seen again" is flat, and it is **not in facts.md** (invented). The best verified twists are unused: the death certificate said "apprentice salesman", the judge's "rivalry with the United States Treasury" line, the bedsheet-rope escape. |
| Ending + comment question | 4 | "Would YOU have bought it?": everyone answers "no", so there is no debate. |
| Clarity with sound off | 6 | The archive photos are good, but several beats (s06 "empty chair", s08 "ship") need a big text line to read muted. |
| Factual safety vs facts.md | 4 | The narration never says "according to" for the con. The only hedge is a small source tag at the end. facts.md requires the hedge in the voice. Plus the invented s10 and the "Too embarrassed" motive. |

### 3 biggest problems

1. **s01–s08: the legend is told as fact.** facts.md (rows 3–4 and the narration rules): the whole con has no 1925 primary source. It comes from ex-Secret Service agent James F. Johnson's 1961 memoir, and the narration *must* say "according to…" / "the story goes". The draft has only "By one account" for the amount and a tiny tag in s10. Fix: put the hedge in the voice at line 2. Done right ("So says a U.S. Secret Service agent's memoir"), it *adds* authority for a US audience, and it's a hook in itself.
2. **s03 at 0:05–0:10 = background switch.** Name + job + setup in one long clause, exactly where 25–30 % leave. Fix: keep the action moving from the hook. The forged letter on screen *is* the scheme ("rusting… scrapped… secretly"). The name goes in a tag. The hotel/dealers beat becomes one short punch ("One bit.").
3. **s06 + s10: invented or overstated lines and a weak ending.** "Too embarrassed" is a guess about motive (facts row 2: say "he never went to the police"). "The money was never seen again" appears nowhere in facts.md. Replace the ending with the VERIFIED, ironic money twist: the man who sold a tower that wasn't his went on to print America's money, escaped jail on a bedsheet rope, landed on Alcatraz, and his death certificate listed his job as **"apprentice salesman."** That's the loop-worthy last line.

## 3. Which story has stronger viral potential?

**Pizza (ep27) — clearly stronger.**
- **One giant, fully VERIFIED contrast**: $41 → $1.26 billion, readable in one second, muted. Eiffel's central number ($70,000) is DISPUTED and has to carry a tag that weakens it.
- **Hot topic + comment fuel**: crypto splits people (holders vs skeptics), and "would you have held?" has two real sides. A comment war means more watch time and shares. Eiffel's "would you buy it?" has one obvious answer.
- **No hedging needed for the core**: the forum posts, the dates and his own quotes are primary sources. Eiffel must say "according to a memoir" in the first 5 s.
- **Saturation**: the Lustig story has been told many times on Shorts/TikTok, so many viewers know the hook already and swipe. The "can't afford to keep doing it" and "$25 vs $41" angles on pizza are much less used.
- **Rewatch/share trigger**: the deadpan "I don't regret it" over a billion-dollar loss is a meme-ready ending.

Eiffel can still perform well thanks to the "apprentice salesman" ending (strong share line) and broad non-crypto appeal. Publish it second, with the v2 hedge-as-authority opener.
