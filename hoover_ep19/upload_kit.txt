PINNED COMMENT
Sources: UPI, Mar 31 1993 — the offer began in August 1992 (spend £100 on Hoover products, two return tickets to Europe), upgraded in December to New York or Orlando; many buyers chose the cheapest qualifying product, a £119 vacuum; about 200,000 people had claimed tickets; Maytag removed Hoover Europe's president and two marketing executives and planned a $30m charge. UPI, Sep 29 1993 — a £20m ($30m) rescue package; 103,000 people flown so far; trading standards found no fraud but said Hoover "grossly miscalculated" the take-up. International Directory of Company Histories (vol. 40) — about $60m in pretax charges in 1993; Maytag sold Hoover Europe to Candy in 1995. The customer who went to Maytag's annual meeting in Newton, Iowa: Des Moines Register, Apr 7 1993 (via Wikipedia). Sources differ on the spend needed for the US flights (£100 or £250). All people in the video are generic drawings (re-enactment), not portraits.
Lesson: if a free gift is worth more than the product, read the small print — and expect a queue.

YOUTUBE SHORTS
Title: Hoover Gave Away Free Flights With Vacuums. It Went Very Wrong (UK 1992)
Description: In August 1992, Hoover offered two free return flights to anyone in Britain or Ireland who spent £100 on its products — first to Europe, then to New York and Orlando. The cheapest qualifying vacuum cost £119; the tickets were worth far more. By March 1993 about 200,000 people had claimed flights, travel agents couldn't keep up, and one angry customer went all the way to the owner Maytag's annual meeting in Iowa. Maytag paid for charter planes, removed three Hoover executives and took about $60 million in charges in 1993; two years later it sold Hoover Europe. Hoover thought it was giving away flights to sell vacuums — people were buying vacuums to get the flights. (Illustrated re-enactment; generic characters.)
