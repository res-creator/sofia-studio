PINNED COMMENT
Sources: CBS News on the Kytch / Taylor dispute (the daily pasteurizing cycle takes about four hours; if a step runs past its time limit the cycle fails and has to start over; the on-air example error "R BRL > 41F after SL"; Taylor says it doesn't make money from servicing its machines and repairs are handled by independent distributors). The machine heats the mix to about 151°F to kill bacteria, then cools it (reported by Inc. and Wired). On Oct. 28, 2024 a U.S. Copyright Office (Librarian of Congress) DMCA exemption took effect allowing owners to bypass software locks to diagnose and repair retail-level commercial food preparation equipment, including soft-serve machines. Limits: it doesn't allow selling bypass tools, and these exemptions are reviewed every 3 years. "Can take days" is typical reporting, not a fixed rule; some reports say weeks. The machine and the people in the video are generic drawings.

YOUTUBE SHORTS
Title: Why the McDonald's ice cream machine is ALWAYS broken
Description: It's usually not broken: it's cleaning itself. Every night the soft-serve machine heats the mix (to about 151°F) to kill bacteria, then cools it back down, which takes about four hours. If one step runs too long, the whole cycle fails, the screen shows a hard-to-read error code, and staff often have to wait for an authorized technician. In October 2024 the U.S. Copyright Office granted a DMCA exemption that makes it legal to bypass software locks to repair retail food-prep equipment like these machines. The problem wasn't the ice cream. It was who's allowed to fix the machine. (Illustrated; generic machine and characters.)
