YOUTUBE SHORTS
Title: "More Doctors Smoke Camels": The Rigged Doctors' Survey (1946)
Description: In 1946, R.J. Reynolds' ads told America that more doctors smoked Camels than any other cigarette. The proof, the ads claimed, was a survey of 113,597 doctors. But it wasn't run by scientists: it was run by the company's own ad agency, whose people went to medical conventions and doctors' offices, handed out free cartons, and then asked which brand the doctors smoked. Medical journals were taking cigarette ad money too — even JAMA, the doctors' own journal, only stopped in 1953. The doctors weren't selling cigarettes. The money was.
