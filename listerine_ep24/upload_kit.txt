The revenue jump ($115,000 → over $8 million in seven years) is reported by Freakonomics (cited by Wikipedia) — reported figures, not audited. Title: Listerine Was a Floor Cleaner. Then Ads Invented a "Condition"
Description: Listerine began in the 1880s as a surgical antiseptic and was also sold as a floor cleaner. As a mouthwash it barely sold — until, around 1921, Gerard Lambert (the boss's son) and his team picked up an old medical word: halitosis. The ads turned bad breath from a smell into a condition your friends would never mention, and gave us Edna — "often a bridesmaid, never a bride." Revenue reportedly jumped from $115,000 to over $8 million in seven years. They didn't sell a cure. They sold the fear. What "problem" do YOU think ads invented?
