# HARVEY · HOUSTON, 2017 — script and edit plan v1 (draft)

Status: draft for the owner's review. No narration recorded (ElevenLabs on the free tier), no render.
Working question: **"Catastrophic flooding was forecast days in advance. Why did so many people still end up trapped?"**
The answer must hold the real limits: what a forecast can and cannot say (scale, not streets); what officials decided and why (stated reasons, not blame); what people could actually do (water rose overnight, roads closed, 911 overwhelmed); what the reservoirs could do (hold water upstream or release it downstream).

Markers:
- **⟨V⟩** = number/quote under independent re-verification (`research/verification.md`); final wording after it.

- Labels say what the picture is and when it was filmed. Any rescue shown under a story it does not belong to is labelled as such.
- Sound: only the natural sound of each archive clip, interview voices, narration and a quiet music bed. No added effects.

## 1. The warning (Aug 23–25) ≈ 2:00

**Narrator:**
"Harvey had fallen apart once already, over the Caribbean. On Wednesday, August twenty-third, it came back to life in the Gulf of Mexico — and grew fast. ⟨V⟩
By Friday morning, the National Hurricane Center was using a word it rarely puts in writing: catastrophic. ⟨V: Advisory #21, 10 a.m. Aug 25⟩
That afternoon, forecasters put the whole Houston area under the highest flood risk they issue — the first time they had ever done so three days ahead. ⟨V: WPC Day-3 High Risk, 'unprecedented'⟩
What they could not say was where, exactly, the heaviest rain would fall. Their forecast put the centre of it more than a hundred miles south-west of where it eventually came down. ⟨V: ~110 mi⟩"

**Then the decision:**
"Houston is a city of more than two million people, in a county of four and a half million. ⟨V⟩ Coastal counties were ordered to leave. Houston was not.
The mayor, Sylvester Turner, asked people not to leave the city all at once. ⟨V: exact wording⟩ Harris County's top official, Ed Emmett, explained why: a hurricane's storm surge comes from the sea, and you know who lives in its path. Rain can flood any street — and nobody could say which ones. ⟨V⟩
They also remembered Hurricane Rita, twelve years earlier. Millions had tried to leave Houston at once. People died on the road before the storm ever arrived. ⟨V: Rita figures — 'more than' / '23 in one bus fire'⟩"

**Kiley's crew:** **Greene** (620838 0:01.9–0:12.6): "Things spun up super rapidly… flying our normal flight schedule Friday afternoon to canceling it… 'if we can get a crew on the road now…'"

## 8. The answer ≈ 1:00

**Narrator:**
"So why were so many people trapped, after a warning that came in time?
Because the forecast was right about the scale of the disaster, but could not say which streets would flood — and the rain fell in hours, in the dark, faster than most people could react.
Because the safest choice for a city of millions was not obvious — and leaving had killed people before.
And because some of the water had nowhere left to go: holding it flooded one neighbourhood, releasing it flooded another.
What filled the gap was people — in helicopters, in trucks and in their own boats."

**Kiley** (1:42.6–2:02.6, over a wide hoist shot — labelled): "…you can see on the horizon, you just see helicopters and guys getting hoisted down, like, everywhere. It was overpowering sometimes, just seeing the effort."
Picture: 546164 0:45–1:15 (hoist operator at the door over flooded trees) → 546526 0:57–1:00 (people walking away from the landing zone) → end card.
