# Harvey documentary — independent fact-check (verification.md)

Checked 2026-11-05 against primary documents. I did not rely on `chronology.md`. Where it agrees, that is noted only for convenience.

**Page convention.** "p. N" is the page number printed on the document. "pdf N" is the page in the PDF file, given when the two differ. Local copies are in `harvey/assets/raw/docs/`. Documents downloaded for this check (not saved in the repo) are listed with their URLs.

**Status key**
- **VERIFIED:** the primary source says it.
- **CORRECTED:** the source says something different, and the correct version is given.
- **PARTLY:** some parts are verified and others are corrected or unconfirmed.
- **NOT VERIFIABLE:** no primary source was found; what was searched is listed.

## Detail by claim

### 1. Hurricane Rita (2005)

**1b — Bus fire: VERIFIED**
- **Source:** NTSB, *Motorcoach Fire on Interstate 45 During Hurricane Rita Evacuation Near Wilmer, Texas, September 23, 2005*, HAR-07/01. Local copy: `NTSB_HAR0701_Wilmer_bus_fire.pdf`.
  - Abstract (pdf 4) and Executive Summary (p. vii): "The motorcoach, en route from Bellaire to Dallas, Texas, as part of the evacuation in anticipation of Hurricane Rita, was carrying 44 assisted living facility residents and nursing staff. The trip had begun about 3:00 p.m. on September 22. Fifteen hours later, about 6:00 a.m. on the following day … Twenty-three passengers were fatally injured."
  - p. 9, Table 1: Fatal — motorcoach passengers 23. "The 23 passengers who died in the fire ranged from 68 to 100 years old."
  - Probable cause (p. vii): "insufficient lubrication in the right-side tag axle wheel bearing assembly … which led to ignition of the tire and the catastrophic fire."
- **Timing:** Rita's center made landfall at 0740 UTC 24 September 2005 (NHC TCR Rita, AL182005, p. 3; downloaded from https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL182005_Rita.pdf). The fire (Sept 23) was therefore during the evacuation and before landfall.
- **Also from the NHC Rita TCR, p. 8:** "At least 55 'indirect' fatalities have been reported in Texas … A bus accident south of Dallas during the course of the evacuation killed more than 20 persons, mostly elderly evacuees from a nursing home. Other persons died during the evacuation due to heat exhaustion."

**1a — "73 of 139": secondary only**
- **Governor's Commission, *Eye of the Storm* (EYE), p. 22 (pdf 30):** "During Rita, 73 persons died in a chaotic evacuation before the hurricane even reached Texas. That toll accounted for more than half of the 139 deaths attributed to Rita, which veered away from Houston at the last minute…"
  - Its footnote 67 cites the *Texas Tribune* (Oct 13, 2017), not a state dataset.
- **Texas Tribune, "The Road From Rita" (Sept 16, 2015):** "Of the 139 deaths that the state linked to Hurricane Rita, 73 occurred before Rita reached Texas. Twenty-three people died in a bus fire. Ten others died from hyperthermia due to heat exposure." No document is named.
- **NWS Houston/Galveston Storm Data, Sept 2005 (local copy):**
  - "the massive evacuation resulted in at least 49 indirect fatalities, mostly due to excessive heat and the transportation of the elderly out of harm's way."
  - Harris County: "at least 34 indirect fatalities before, during and after Hurricane Rita. The majority of these fatalities occurred during the evacuation prior to Rita…"
  - This covers only the HGX forecast area. Wilmer is in Dallas County, so the bus fire is not part of the 49.
- **Other counts:** Zachria & Patel, *Chest* 2006;130(4):124S (meeting abstract), is cited by others as ~107 evacuation-related deaths; I did not open the abstract itself. Searched for a Texas DSHS Rita mortality report and found none online.
- **What counts:** the 73 are deaths "before Rita reached Texas", mostly in the evacuation (heat, transport of elderly people, the bus fire). Not every pre-landfall death was on the road.
- **Safest narration:** "When Rita threatened Houston in 2005, millions fled. Dozens of people died during that evacuation — twenty-three of them in a single bus fire." To use 73/139: "By the state's count, as reported by the Texas Tribune, more than half of Rita's 139 Texas deaths came before the storm even arrived."

### 2. Flooded buildings around Addicks and Barker
- **EYE p. 37 (pdf 45), "Focus on the Addicks and Barker Reservoirs":** "More than 9,000 homes and businesses built within the reservoirs' pools were flooded — about 5,000 upstream of Addicks and at least 4,000 upstream of Barker."
- **EYE p. 17 (pdf 25):**
  - "USACE opened the gates at both dams, releasing more than 15,000 cubic feet per second (cfs) into Buffalo Bayou — and flooding about 4,000 homes downstream. … In total, more than 10,000 homes in or near the reservoirs experienced some level of flooding during Harvey." (fn 36–37: Houston Flood Mitigation Consortium; HCFCD)
  - The same page wrongly dates flow around the north end of Addicks to "August 26".
- **EYE p. 20 (pdf 28):** "As noted above, these releases flooded about 4,000 homes downstream."
- **HCFCD, WEAT presentation, Oct 24, 2018 (Alan Black), slide 10, "Harvey House Flooding":** "Harvey 154,170 … 11% total structures in Harris County flooded … 105,340 or 68% outside 1% floodplain". Downloaded from https://www.weat.org/cms_assets/Files/2018-10-24-WEAT-Webinar.pdf.
- **HCFCD Immediate Report #1 (Sept 5, 2017), p. 4:** "suggests that 136,000 structures were flooded … HCFCD believes this is a conservative estimate".
- **NHC TCR p. 9:** "At least 160,000 structures were flooded in Harris and Galveston counties."
- **CFC 2019 (upstream) p. 19:** "Of the approximately one million homes in the Harris County, around 154,000 of them flooded from the impounded water." This puts the countywide total under the reservoir floods. Do not use it.
  - The same opinion (p. 13) says the Corps surveyed "over 10,000 structures in the potential impoundment area" in the early 2000s. That is not a count of flooded structures.
- **Safest narration:**
  - "The state's review later counted more than nine thousand homes and businesses flooded inside the reservoirs, and about four thousand homes downstream."
  - "Across Harris County, about 154,000 houses flooded."

### 3. "600,000 lives" and "zero" risk
- **Source:** CFC, *In re Downstream Addicks and Barker*, No. 17-9002, Opinion & Order filed Apr 22, 2026 (Senior Judge Loren A. Smith). Local copy `CFC_17-9002_Downstream_2026-04-22_via_ELR.pdf`.
- **p. 7:** "Defendant estimated that dam failure would have placed 600,000 lives at risk and caused approximately $21 billion in damages. Trial Tr. at 331:2–333:3."
- **p. 40:** "Defendant estimated that potential dam failure placed 600,000 lives at stake and around $21 billion in projected damages."
- **p. 41:**
  - "Despite pool levels at Addicks and Barker approaching their spillway design floods, risk of failure remained minimal."
  - "Defendant calculated that the spillway design flood created a 14 percent risk that the auxillary floodways would fail at Addicks. However, the risk of failure at pool elevations below 111.6 feet is zero. JX 42, at USACE066222. Even elevations slightly higher posed a risk of less than one in a million…"
- **p. 40:** "the Corps also feared that auxillary spillways would fail if water levels exceeded 112 feet at Addicks."
- **Who said what:**
  - 600,000 is the Corps' (defendant's) estimate of the consequences of a failure, given in trial testimony.
  - "Zero" is the court reading the Corps' own risk document, JX42.
  - The two figures do not conflict: one is consequence, the other probability.
- **Safest narration:** "The Corps estimated that if the dams failed, six hundred thousand lives would be at risk. But its own risk analysis, cited by the court years later, put the chance of failure at the levels reached during Harvey at essentially zero."

### 4. Court outcomes
- **(a) CFC 2019 (Lettow).** *In re Upstream Addicks and Barker (Texas) Flood-Control Reservoirs*, Sub-Master Docket No. 17-9001L, filed Dec 17, 2019. Local copy `CFC_17-9001…pdf`.
  - p. 46: "the government's actions relating to the Addicks and Barker Dams and the attendant flooding of plaintiffs' properties constituted a taking of a flowage easement under the Fifth Amendment. Thus, the court finds defendant liable."
  - p. 46, on necessity: "the flooding that occurred was the direct result of calculated planning."
- **(b) Federal Circuit 2025.** *Ablan v. United States*, No. 2023-1363 etc., decided Dec 22, 2025. Before Reyna, Taranto, Cunningham; opinion by Cunningham.
  - p. 4: "We affirm the decisions of the Court of Federal Claims as to liability and class certification."
  - On damages: affirmed for leasehold advantage, personal property and the FEMA offset; vacated for lost rent, displacement and the valuation of Ms. Popovici's easement.
  - p. 9: "We reject each of the government's arguments" (police power, no taking, necessity).
  - Citation 162 F.4th 1364.
- **(b, continued) Supreme Court.** SG application No. 25A1440 (local copy), p. 2:
  - "The court of appeals entered its judgment on December 22, 2025, and denied a petition for rehearing on April 9, 2026."
  - It asks for an extension "to and including September 4, 2026".
  - The docket (supremecourt.gov, checked 2026-11-05) shows: application Jun 24 → granted Jul 1 (to Aug 7); further application Jul 23 → granted Jul 24 (to Sept 4, 2026). No petition is listed.
  - ReduceFlooding (9/4/2026) and txlrs.org cite KPRC (Sept 7, 2026): counsel McGehee Chang Feiler announced that the government decided not to petition.
  - Status: no petition filed. The confirmation is secondary, but the docket agrees.
- **(c) Downstream.**
  - CFC dismissed: 147 Fed. Cl. 566 (2020), Feb 18, 2020, Senior Judge Loren A. Smith. Date from AP/Texas Tribune, Feb 19, 2020; the judge from the *Milton* caption.
  - *Milton v. United States*, Fed. Cir. No. 2021-1131, decided June 2, 2022 (Lourie, Chen, Cunningham). p. 5: "We hold that Appellants have alleged cognizable property interests in flowage easements"; p. 14: "REVERSED AND REMANDED."
  - On remand, Apr 22, 2026 (Smith), p. 48: "plaintiffs established that a temporary and a permanent taking occurred … JUDGMENT in favor of plaintiffs and shall be entered upon a determination of damages."
  - **Correction:** the 2026 judge is Smith, not Lettow. Its footnote 1 refers to "late Senior Judge Charles F. Lettow".
- **(e) Reasonable / professional.**
  - 2026 opinion p. 38: "Plaintiffs conceded that they 'have never taken the position that the Government's actions were unreasonable.' … Thus, the Court need not analyze whether the Induced Surcharges were 'actually necessary.'"
  - p. 40: "The Court does not intend to diminish or disrespect defendant's efforts. Corps members exhibited a high degree of professionalism amid Harvey's circumstances. Nonetheless, defendant failed to establish that conditions threatened the integrity of the Dams to create an actual emergency."
  - p. 40: "Again, plaintiffs do not question defendant's decision to open the floodgates."
  - No court held that the Corps "acted reasonably".
- **Safest narration:** "The courts did not say the engineers did anything wrong. They said the government had planned this flooding, so it must pay for it."

### 5. Corps releases and pools
- **CFC 2026 p. 6:** "At around midnight on August 28, 2017, defendant invoked Section 7-05(b) and began making surcharge releases 'due to the speed at which the pools have been rising.'"
- **CFC 2019 p. 18:** "On August 28, shortly after midnight, the Corps initiated releases of impounded water in both reservoirs… DX649 at 1."
- **CFC 2026 p. 7:** "By August 30, 2017, both Dams experienced peak flows, with Addicks releasing 7,500 cfs and Barker releasing 6,300 cfs of impounded water that totaled 13,800 cfs."
- **HCFCD Immediate Report #1, p. 5:**
  - "Addicks Reservoir peaked at a record elevation of 109.10 ft at 7:00 a.m. on August 30th"
  - "The pool reached an elevation of 108.0 ft on August 29th at 7:15 a.m. which resulted in uncontrolled flow over the natural ground at the end of the north spillway."
  - "Barker Reservoir reached a peak pool elevation of 101.56 ft on August 30th at 6:00 a.m."
- **CFC 2019 p. 18, quoting the Corps (PX25, USACE016691):** "Uncontrolled release[s] around the Addicks north[-]end emergency spillway w[ere] observed on August 29, 2017 when the reservoir reached [] 108 ft."
- **Conflicting dates:**
  - The HCFCD web page "Flooding Impacts in Connection with the Reservoirs" says "began spilling around the north end of the Addicks dam August 28". It was written during the event.
  - EYE p. 17 says "August 26".
  - Use **Aug 29**.
- **Primary statement:** the Corps' own press release is not in the files. The "midnight" timing rests on the trial exhibits as quoted by both courts (DX649; Def. Ex. 13), and HCFCD confirms Aug 28.
