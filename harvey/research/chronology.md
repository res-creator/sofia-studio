## 1. Forecast timeline, landfall, stall, rainfall, deaths

### 1.1 Storm life cycle (NHC best track) [CONFIRMED in primary source]
Source: TCR pp. 1–3 (Synoptic History) and Table 1 (pp. 15–16); UTC→CDT converted by me.

- **Aug 17, 0600 UTC (1 a.m. CDT):** tropical depression forms ~440 n mi east of Barbados; tropical storm 12 h later (Aug 17, 1800 UTC). TCR p. 2.
- **Aug 19, 1800 UTC:** degenerates to a tropical wave over the central Caribbean. TCR p. 2.
- **Aug 23, 1200 UTC (7 a.m. CDT):** regenerates as a tropical depression ~150 n mi west of Progreso, Mexico (Bay of Campeche). Tropical storm by 1800 UTC. TCR p. 2; Table 1.
- **Aug 24, 1800 UTC (1 p.m. CDT):** hurricane. **Aug 25 midday:** Category 3. **Aug 26, 0000 UTC (7 p.m. CDT Aug 25):** Category 4. TCR pp. 2–3; Table 1.
- **Landfall 1: Aug 26, 0300 UTC = ~10 p.m. CDT Fri Aug 25**, northern end of San Jose Island, ~5 n mi east of Rockport, TX; **115 kt (~130 mph), 937 mb, Category 4.** TCR p. 3.
- **Landfall 2: Aug 26, 0600 UTC = ~1 a.m. CDT Sat Aug 26**, northeast shore of Copano Bay west of Holiday Beach (SE of Refugio), 105 kt, 948 mb. TCR p. 3; Table 1.
- Weakened to tropical storm within ~12 h (Aug 26, 1800 UTC). "The storm then stalled, with its center over or near the Texas coast for four days" (TCR p. 1). Slow loop late Aug 26–27, then drifted E/SE. TCR p. 3.
- **Aug 28, ~0300 UTC (~10 p.m. CDT Aug 27):** center moves back offshore over Matagorda Bay. TCR p. 3.
- **Aug 30, 0800 UTC (3 a.m. CDT):** final landfall near Cameron, Louisiana, 40 kt. TCR p. 3.
- **Sept 1, 0600 UTC:** extratropical over the Tennessee Valley; dissipated over northern Kentucky late Sept 2. TCR p. 3.
- Why it rained so much over Houston: weak stationary front across the Houston metro Aug 26–27, rain bands "training"; "6.8 inches of rain in just one hour documented in southeastern Houston". TCR p. 7.

Discrepancies to be aware of (not real controversies, just errors in other documents):
- CSB report ¶1 says Harvey "made landfall in southeast Texas" on **August 24** — this conflicts with NHC (Aug 25, ~10 p.m. CDT). Use NHC. [CONTESTED → NHC is authoritative]
- HCFCD-IR1 p. 1 says landfall "near Port Aransas around 10:00 p.m. on August 25th"; NHC says San Jose Island ~5 n mi E of Rockport (a few miles NE of Port Aransas). Use NHC wording.
- CFC-UP p. 18 (Joint Stipulation): "near Rockport, Texas, around 10:00 p.m. on August 25, 2017, as a Category 4 hurricane (130 mile per hour winds)". Consistent with NHC.

### 1.2 Watches/warnings and first products [CONFIRMED in primary source]
- **Hurricane Watch** first issued **Aug 23, 1500 UTC (10 a.m. CDT)**, Port Mansfield–San Luis Pass; **Storm Surge Watch** same time, Port Mansfield–High Island. TCR p. 13–14; Tables 8–9.
- **Hurricane Warning** first issued **Aug 24, 0900 UTC (4 a.m. CDT)**, Port Mansfield–Matagorda; **Tropical Storm Warning** Matagorda–High Island (this covered the Houston/Galveston coast); **Storm Surge Warning** Port Mansfield–San Luis Pass. TCR Tables 8–9.
- These were the **first operational storm surge watches/warnings** issued by NWS. TCR p. 14.
- NHC began direct decision support to emergency managers on **Aug 22**. TCR p. 13.
- NHC Special Advisory at **1800 UTC Aug 24** (1 p.m. CDT), ~33 h before landfall, forecast the rapid intensification — "an outstanding forecast" (TCR p. 13).
- WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook: Slight Risk in Day 3 outlook Tue afternoon **Aug 22**; Day 1 "High Risk" each day **Aug 24–31**. SA p. 38 (PDF p. 47).
- **"In an unprecedented move, WPC depicted a High Risk in its Day 3 Excessive Rain Outlook issued Friday afternoon, August 25, valid for the period 12 UTC August 27 to 12 UTC August 28. This High Risk encompassed much of the greater Houston metropolitan area, where catastrophic flooding indeed occurred on August 27."** SA p. 38 (PDF p. 47).

The SA also notes a **dry bias** for the Houston–Lake Charles corridor: the "QPF centroid is displaced approximately 110 miles southwest of the center of heaviest rainfall". [CONFIRMED]

### 1.6 Assessment judgments about the forecast [CONFIRMED in primary source]
- "EMC provided the model guidance and warning for a catastrophic event a few days before the event, so it was not a surprise for the National Centers and WFOs." SA p. 40.

### 1.8 Deaths [CONFIRMED in primary source]
- "at least 68 direct deaths in the United States, all in Texas. Over half of the deaths (36) were in Harris County". "All but three ... from freshwater flooding"; none from storm surge. "About 35 additional deaths are ascribed to indirect causes". Deadliest hurricane in Texas since 1919. TCR p. 8.

- No tornado deaths (52 tornadoes, mostly EF-0/EF-1). TCR p. 8.

## 2. Evacuation decisions (presented as decisions with stated reasons)

### 2.1 Houston / Harris County: no mass evacuation order
**Context:** Texas hurricane evacuation zones are tied to storm surge (SA p. 24 notes NWS staff in non-surge zones did not qualify for evacuation pay because "mandatory evacuation zones are tied to storm surge"). Most of Harris County is outside surge zones.

**Before landfall — Fri Aug 25:**
- Mayor Sylvester Turner (Twitter, Friday afternoon Aug 25): "Please think twice before trying to leave Houston en masse," and "No evacuation orders have been issued." [SECONDARY only — TT-0825; ABC13-0825 also quotes "please think twice before trying to leave Houston en masse." Full tweet text not verified in an archive.]
- BuzzFeed News also reported a Turner tweet: "No official has issued evac order for Houston now" ... "Calm and care!" [SECONDARY only — https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/nidhiprakash/why-wasnt-houston-evacuated-before-hurricane-harvey]
- Harris County OEM spokesman Francisco Sanchez (tweet): "LOCAL LEADERS KNOW BEST." HCFCD/OEM meteorologist Jeff Lindner: "We do not need people inland evacuating the coast." and "We are not going to have that significant of a storm surge in Galveston Bay that you need to leave," [SECONDARY only — ABC13-0825]
- Judge Ed Emmett, Friday press conference — reported quotes: "If we wanted to call an evacuation, we wouldn't even know where to call it, 'cause we don't know where the rain's going to fall and which watersheds are going to be affected," and "When we say that, we mean storm surge, not rain." [SECONDARY only — seen only in search-result excerpts of news reports; not verified against a transcript → open question]

**After the flooding — Sun Aug 27:**
- Turner, Sunday news conference (two transcriptions):
  - TT-0827: "You cannot put, in the city of Houston, 2.3 million people on the road ... That is dangerous," and "If you think the situation right now is bad — you give an order to evacuate, you create a nightmare."
  - CNN-TR: "You cannot put in the city of Houston 2.3 million people on the road. That is dangerous." "When you combine Houston and Harris County, you literally cannot put 6.5 million people on the road." "If you think the situation right now is bad, you give an order to evacuate, you are creating a nightmare." "...you really are asking for a major calamity."
  - [SECONDARY only; minor wording differences between transcriptions — use video if possible: CNN clip "harvey-houston-mayor-no-regrets-no-evacuation-sot.kprc"]
- Emmett to CNN, Sunday Aug 27: "To suggest that we should have evacuated 2 million people is an outrageous statement," "When we have hurricanes, we know who to evacuate because you have a storm surge coming," "In this case, we have a rain event." "Unless you know where the rain is going to fall, we don't know who to evacuate." "We've had three major rain events in the past two years. This is now the fourth," (TT-0827). CNN-TR version: "Unless you know where the rain is going to fall, you don't know who to evacuate..." "If we had told those people you have to evacuate, they would have laughed..." "So to suggest that we should have evacuated two million people is just an outrageous statement." [SECONDARY only; the CNN transcript labels him "Director", he was County Judge]
- Emmett, Sunday Aug 27: "If you have a boat, we need your help." [CONFIRMED — quoted in SA p. 8, PDF p. 17]
- Harris County's own later position (HC-FACT, Nov 13 2017, Annex E): **"If we had to do it all over again, we would still not issue an evacuation order for Harris County based on flood threat"** [CONFIRMED in primary source — county document, copy via DocumentCloud]

**Stated reasons (summary, as the officials gave them):** (1) Harvey was a rain event, not a surge event for Houston; evacuation zones exist for surge. (2) Nobody could know in advance which watersheds would flood. (3) Putting 2.3 million (city) / 6.5 million (region) people on the road is itself dangerous — explicit reference to Hurricane Rita 2005. (4) Being on the road when bands hit is the most common way people die in floods ("Turn Around, Don't Drown").

### 2.2 Governor Abbott

## 3. Addicks and Barker reservoirs

### 3.1 What they are [CONFIRMED in primary source]
- Federal (USACE) dams on Buffalo Bayou ~17 miles west of downtown Houston (CFC-DOWN26 p. 4). Authorized after the 1929 and 1935 floods (Rivers and Harbors Act 1938, Flood Control Act 1939). **Barker built 1942–1945; Addicks 1946–1948.** Barker embankment ~13.6 miles; Addicks ~11.6 miles. Top of Barker 113.1 ft, Addicks 121 ft; ends of embankment 104 ft (Barker) / 112 ft (Addicks). Spillway design flood: Barker 108 ft, Addicks 115 ft. CFC-DOWN26 p. 3.
- "The reservoirs of the Addicks and Barker Dams are typically dry but fill with water when it rains". CAFC-UP p. 5. They have no other purpose (no water supply, navigation, power) (CFC-UP p. 16).
- Originally ungated conduits; gated progressively 1948–1963 as development grew. CFC-DOWN26 p. 3; CFC-UP pp. 9–10.
- Both are "classified as high hazard dams due to the probable loss of life and impacts ... in the event of a failure" (CFC-DOWN26 p. 4). EYE p. 37: USACE rated them "extremely high risk" in 2009.

### 3.2 Private land inside the flood pool [CONFIRMED in primary source]
- The Corps "acquired 12,460 acres behind Addicks and 23,060 acres behind Barker, or enough acreage to contain pools with elevations of 103.1 feet and 95 feet, respectively." (SCOTUS application p. 2, quoting CFC May 24 2018 opinion).
- Planning era: Corps considered buying all land that a 31.4-inch design storm would flood but found it "unnecessary"; the Corps recognized this "w[ould] eventually place the [g]overnment in the position of having to flood the area within the reservoir with the accompanying damages in order to protect downstream improvements in the event of a severe future storm." CAFC-UP pp. 5–6.
- Fort Bend County was told in the early 1990s "that the Barker Dam was designed and/or modified to contain 8.7 more feet of water than was purchased by the Corps" (~4,769 acres not under Corps jurisdiction). CFC-UP p. 14. HCFCD study: "more than 8,000 acres [are] within the reservoir 'fringe' areas". CFC-UP p. 14.
- Corps 1995 reconnaissance report considered buyouts, easements, warning plans etc. and decided to take no action upstream ("insufficient economic benefits"). CFC-UP p. 12–13.
- Corps 2009: "we know it can and probably will happen at some point in time." CFC-UP p. 17.
- First time water exceeded government land: April 2016 "Tax Day" storm (no structures flooded by the pool). CFC-UP p. 17.
- EYE p. 37: "Since 2000, nearly 30,000 structures have been built within the reservoirs' flood pools"; "The only official disclosure ... is found on subdivision maps of proposed developments within Fort Bend County; Houston and Harris County never required such disclosure." [CONFIRMED in state report]
- Warnings that existed: Fort Bend plat notes since early 1990s (e.g. "subject to extended controlled inundation under the management of the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers"); USGS quad maps since early 1970s; Key Maps; Corps "dozens and dozens" of public presentations, 2009/2010 public meetings. CFC-UP pp. 13–15. **But** the court found plaintiffs "neither knew, nor reasonably should have known, of the risk posed by the dams" (CFC-UP at 261, as summarized in CFC-DOWN26 p. 23).

### 3.3 Operating rules in force (2012 Water Control Manual) [CONFIRMED in primary source]
- Gates closed when 1 inch of rain falls below the reservoirs in 24 h or flooding is predicted downstream; "Keep the gates closed and under surveillance as long as necessary to prevent flooding below the dams." CFC-UP p. 16; CFC-DOWN26 p. 5.
- **Section 7-05(b) "Induced Surcharge"**: when pools reach **101 ft (Addicks) / 95.7 ft (Barker) NAVD88** and inflow keeps raising the pool, releases "will be made in accordance with the induced surcharge regulation schedules". Purpose (Corps): restore storage and "reduce risk of failure to the structures" / "protect the integrity of the dams". CFC-DOWN26 p. 5; CFC-UP p. 16.
- Downstream flooding of streets and homes begins when combined release exceeds ~**4,000 cfs** (HCFCD reservoir impacts page, https://www.hcfcd.org/About/Harris-Countys-Flooding-History/Countywide-Impacts/Flooding-Impacts-in-Connection-with-the-Reservoirs); historically the Corps limited combined releases to 2,000 cfs (1984) (CFC-DOWN26 p. 4).
- After the 2016 Tax Day flood the Corps publicly said: "[w]e will not open the dam to a point where it will cause flooding downstream." (CFC-DOWN26 p. 24, citing Pls.' 266-8 at A3331–32). Corps witnesses "could not recall if they ever publicly disclosed that they could flood downstream properties by Induced Surcharges." (ibid.)
- Harvey was the **first time** Section 7-05(b) was used to flood downstream properties (CFC-DOWN26 p. 39).

### 3.10 The trade-off, stated neutrally
- **Hold water (gates closed):** protects Buffalo Bayou neighborhoods, downtown and the Ship Channel; floods homes built on private land inside the pools — something the design anticipated since the 1940s (CAFC-UP pp. 5–6).
- **Release water (induced surcharge):** limits pool rise and flow around the dam ends (unprecedented at Addicks north end on Aug 29); adds thousands of cfs to an already flooded Buffalo Bayou, prolonging/deepening flooding for homes downstream (HCFCD: ~4,000 cfs combined already floods streets/homes).
- **Do nothing beyond the rule / let pools rise further:** more uncontrolled flow around embankment ends; Corps feared spillway damage above ~112 ft at Addicks; a failure scenario was estimated at 600,000 lives at risk / ~$21 bn (CFC-DOWN26 p. 7). Courts later found the actual failure risk at the pool levels reached to be very low (risk "zero" below 111.6 ft per Corps document JX42).
- Courts' framing: not that the Corps acted unreasonably, but that the burden fell on particular owners and the Constitution requires compensation ("forcing some people alone to bear public burdens which, in all fairness and justice, should be borne by the public as a whole" — Armstrong, quoted CAFC-UP p. 18).

## 6. Known in advance vs what happened vs what investigators/courts concluded

| Topic | Known / said in advance | What happened | What investigators / courts concluded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rain | From Aug 25 (≈36 h before Houston's worst night) NHC said 15–30 in, isolated 40 in, "catastrophic and life-threatening flooding"; WPC Day-3 High Risk over Houston | 36–48 in in Houston metro; 60.58 in record at Nederland; worst night Aug 26–27 | NWS SA: forecasts gave "extensive lead time" but WPC's QPF centroid was ~110 mi SW of the heaviest rain; residents "did not comprehend" what 60 in would mean (Finding 7) |
| Evacuation | Evacuation zones built for surge; officials said rain area couldn't be pinpointed; Rita 2005 evacuation deaths (73 of 139 before landfall; 23 in one bus fire) | No mass evacuation of Houston; targeted orders (coastal counties, levee areas, reservoir areas, Arkema zone) | NWS SA: asked for probabilistic rain info ≥3 days ahead to support evacuation decisions; Governor's Commission: far fewer road deaths than Rita; Harris County: would decide the same again |
| Addicks/Barker upstream | Since the 1940s the Corps knew big storms would flood private land in the pools; plats in Fort Bend warned; Houston/Harris never required disclosure | >9,000 homes/businesses in the pools flooded (state report); record pools Aug 30 | Courts (2019, affirmed 2025): a taking — government owes compensation; not an "emergency" defense because it was planned |
| Addicks/Barker downstream | Corps had said it would not open gates to flood downstream; 2012 Manual's induced-surcharge rule existed but was not publicly explained (court finding) | Releases from ~midnight Aug 28, peak 13,800 cfs Aug 30; ~4,000 downstream homes (state report), some for ~2 weeks | 2020 dismissal → 2022 reversed → **Apr 2026: liability found** (trial court); court found no imminent dam danger in legal sense, while not calling Corps actions unreasonable |
| Dam safety | Dams rated high hazard; Corps feared uncontrolled flow & spillway damage | First-ever flow around Addicks north end (Aug 29); dams performed "as intended", no seepage | Disputed: Corps (600,000 lives at risk if failure) vs court (failure risk ~zero below 111.6 ft) — both recorded in CFC-DOWN26 |
| Arkema | Plant in 100-/500-yr floodplain; safeguards assumed power | All safeguards failed via flooding; fires Aug 31–Sept 3; 21 sought medical care | CSB: common-mode failure; need to plan for extreme flooding |
| Lake Conroe | Not a flood-control lake; limited freeboard | Record release 79,141 cfs Aug 28 | SJRA: release always below inflow; lawsuits followed (not researched here) |
