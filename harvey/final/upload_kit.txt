DESCRIPTION
On Friday, August 25, 2017, the National Hurricane Center warned of catastrophic, life-threatening flooding: 15 to 30 inches of rain, in places 40. Two days later, Houston's highways were rivers and people were waiting on their roofs. Across Texas, more than 122,000 people had to be rescued.

This film follows the flood from the warning to the aftermath, through U.S. Coast Guard, National Guard and Air Force Reserve archive footage, the official reports, and the people who were there: Coast Guard rescue swimmer Brendan Kiley, Chief Petty Officer Matthew Dusek, whose own house in Dickinson flooded, and the soldiers and pararescuemen who worked the streets.

What was known before: the rain forecast was clear and early. What no forecast could show was which streets would flood, and Houston, remembering the deadly Rita evacuation of 2005, did not order people to leave.
What happened behind the Addicks and Barker dams in west Houston, and why federal courts later ruled that the government must pay for flooding homes there.

In memory of everyone who lost their lives in Harvey's floods, among them Sgt. Steve Perez of the Houston Police Department.
