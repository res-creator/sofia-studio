## Reservoir map: method and what it may claim

**What the shading is.** Land below the record pool elevation (Addicks 109.10 ft, Barker 101.56 ft; HCFCD, Aug 30), connected to each reservoir.
- Elevation comes from the AWS Terrain Tiles: z14, about 9.5 m cells, US source USGS 3DEP bare-earth.
- For Addicks, the shaded area is cut off by me along the dam embankment as it shows in the elevation data. Without that cut, low ground at the south-west end would join the shaded area to the land downstream.

**Why it is not a flood boundary.**
- Bare-earth elevation leaves out houses and floor heights, so it overstates how many homes stood in water.
- The 9.5 m cells and my cut line are approximations.
- No pool levels for the days before Aug 30 are used.

**Check against the photographs.** The Aug 30 photographs show water in the open reservoir land and in the streets of the edge subdivisions. These are roughly where the computed edge runs. They are the only real evidence of where the water was.

**On screen, therefore:**
- The shading is labelled "land below the record pool level (elevation estimate — not a surveyed flood line)".
- It fades out before the aerial photograph appears.
- The numbers of flooded buildings come only from the Governor's Commission (more than 9,000 inside the reservoirs, about 4,000 downstream), never from the shading.
