January fifteenth, nineteen nineteen. Boston, Massachusetts.
A tank holding two point three million gallons of molasses... exploded.
A wave — forty feet high, moving at thirty-five miles per hour — tore through the streets of the North End.
It crushed buildings. It swallowed horses. It trapped people in a thick, suffocating flood.
Twenty-one people died that day. Not from water. Not from fire.
From molasses.
This is the story they don’t teach you in school.
