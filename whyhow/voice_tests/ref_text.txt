Tuesday, November fourteenth, twenty seventeen. A commuter train outside Tokyo pulls out of the station. Twenty seconds early. Nobody misses it. Nobody complains. And the company posts an official apology anyway. Sounds like the perfect railway, right? Well, not quite. On Tokyo's most famous line, the railway hands out official late notes on about fifteen of every twenty weekdays. So how does a city of nearly thirty-seven million people move like this, without falling into chaos? The answer isn't one perfect machine. It's a pile of strange, very human tricks. Like a railway company that makes more money selling houses than selling tickets.
