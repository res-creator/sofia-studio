Walk through Manhattan on a cold morning and you'll see steam pouring out of the street. It isn't the subway, and it isn't the sewers. It's a hidden system more than a hundred years old — and it's only the start of what lies under New York.

We go underground: the largest steam system in the US, water that travels 125 miles from the Catskills by gravity alone, the wooden water towers on the roofs, a water tunnel that has taken more than 50 years to dig, a subway that never sleeps and pumps 13 million gallons a day, the abandoned City Hall station, Hurricane Sandy, Manhattan schist and the men who got "the bends" building the Brooklyn Bridge.
