## Description
```
Picture a city of about half a million people: traffic jams, universities, nightlife. Now try to drive there. You can't. No highway goes in and no highway goes out. Every car on its streets arrived on a boat.

This is Iquitos, in the Peruvian Amazon, the largest city in the world with no road connection. We follow the rubber rush that built it (and its dark side), the river that feeds it, a city that makes its own electricity, thirty thousand mototaxis, the floating neighborhood of Belén where the river rises about twelve meters a year, and the road that still goes nowhere.
```

## Honesty notes
- Population: estimates range from ~380,000 (2017 census districts) to ~490,000 (2023 estimate); we say "about half a million" and compare only with cities clearly smaller (Pittsburgh, St. Louis). "Roughly as many people as Miami" (Miami ~450–490k).
- ~30,000 mototaxis is one student thesis estimate; said as such on screen.
- ~12 m river swing is our calculation from SENAMHI gauge values in different years (record low 2010 vs 2024–2025 highs).
- The Eiffel link to the Iron House is presented as a myth; buyer and year vary by source.
- The Iquitos–Saramiriza route on the map is schematic.
- Archive photos of the Putumayo are shown without close-ups of victims.
