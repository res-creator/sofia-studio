## Description
```
August 9, 1965. At noon, a 41-year-old prime minister stops a press conference because he can't go on. That morning, Singapore had been pushed out of Malaysia: almost no army, no natural resources, barely any land, and its water came from the neighbor that had just let it go.

Since then, Singapore has grown by 28%. It built new land out of the sea. We follow the hills moved into the sea at East Coast and Changi, the sand wars with its neighbors, the seven islands welded into Jurong Island, the oil caverns 130 meters under the seabed, the Dutch-style polder on Pulau Tekong that sits below sea level, the Four National Taps, and the plan to stop the rising sea with a new Long Island.
```

## Honesty notes
- The 1965 press conference was a recorded broadcast, not live TV. The recording was stopped.
- Lee Kuan Yew wept over the end of the merger, not over water. The film says he "raised" the water problem minutes earlier.
- NEWater mostly goes to industry. The film says "clean enough to drink".
- The Tekong polder is for army training, not homes.
