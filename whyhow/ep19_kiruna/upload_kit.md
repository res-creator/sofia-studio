## Description
```
In August 2025, a 113-year-old wooden church rolled down a road in Arctic Sweden on 224 wheels, slower than you walk. And the whole town behind it is packing up too.

This is Kiruna, home of the world's largest underground iron ore mine. The deeper the mine digs, the more the ground cracks and sinks, and the cracks are creeping toward people's homes. So instead of closing the mine, Kiruna is moving its town center about two miles east. We dig into the mountain made of iron, see how you actually move a town (and a house, and a clock tower), ride along with the famous church, and find out why the moving isn't over yet.
```

## Honesty notes
- Distances: new centre ≈ 3 km (≈2 mi) east; the church route ≈ 5 km (≈3 mi).
- "Slower than you walk": the church averaged ~0.5 km/h; walking is ~5 km/h.
- "About a hundred elephants": 672 t ÷ ~6 t per adult African elephant.
- "Deeper than two Empire State Buildings": the 1365 main level is roughly 1,100 m below the surface.
- "More than twenty historic buildings": counts vary (AP: 25 moved before the church; others: 23 including it).
- The rare-earth deposit is a resource estimate, not an approved mine; the Gabna community opposes mining it.
- Generic shots (reindeer, tunnels, steel, northern-lights clips) are labelled ILLUSTRATIVE. The Gabna reindeer photo and the Kiruna aurora photos are real.
