# EP19 — The Ground Under This Town Is Caving In. So They're Moving It. (Kiruna, Sweden)

## COLD OPEN

**A001** August twenty twenty-five. A hundred-year-old church rolls down a road in Sweden. On two hundred and twenty-four wheels. Slower than you walk. [1]

**A002** And it's not alone. The whole town behind it is packing up too. [2]

**A003** So why on earth would you move a town?

**A004** A flood? Nope. A war? No. The problem isn't on the map at all. It's under it. [3]

**A005** This is Kiruna, the northernmost town in Sweden, about ninety miles above the Arctic Circle. Around twenty-three thousand people, long snowy winters, and a sun that doesn't set in early summer. [4][5]

**A006** We'll dig into the mountain that pays for everything, see how you actually move a town, ride along with that church, and find out why the moving isn't over yet. Let's go!

---

## CH1 — A mountain made of iron

**A007** First, a fun little secret about the name. Kiruna comes from the Sámi word Giron. It means ptarmigan, a chubby snow-white bird. The Sámi herded reindeer here long before there was any town. [6]

**A008** But the town is here because of the mountain right next door. Kiirunavaara is basically a giant slab of iron ore, tilted down into the ground. [7]

**A009** In nineteen hundred, a mining boss named Hjalmar Lundbohm drew up a plan for a brand-new town beside it. Soon a railway linked it to the port of Narvik in Norway. Ore out, money in. [6][8]

**A009B** And Kiruna still loves a big project. About twenty-five miles out of town there's even a space center, Esrange, where scientists have launched well over a thousand research rockets and balloons. Iron underground, rockets overhead. [23]

**A010** Lundbohm didn't want a grim mining camp. He wanted a real town, with art, good schools, and a church people would love. Hold that thought, we'll come back to it. [6]

**A011** Fast forward to today. That mountain is now the world's largest underground iron ore mine, run by the state company LKAB. [7]

**A012** And it's enormous. Every single day, Kiruna digs up enough iron ore to build about six and a half Eiffel Towers. Every day! [9]

**A013** The miners work more than half a mile underground. That's deeper than two Empire State Buildings stacked on top of each other. [10]

**A013B** Down there it's a whole hidden city. Tunnels wide enough for trucks, giant elevators that lift forty tonnes of ore at a time, and trains running in the dark. Every summer, tourists can even ride a bus down to a visitor area deep inside the mountain. [9][22]

**A014** Now here's the catch. The method they use is called sublevel caving. Miners blast the ore from below, and the rock above simply caves in to fill the gap. Blasting happens every night. [9][10]

**A015** Deep underground, that's very efficient. Up on the surface, it means the ground slowly cracks and sinks. And this ore body leans toward the town. [10][11]

**A016** So the deeper the mine goes, the closer the cracks creep to people's homes. And you can't live on ground that's slowly breaking apart.

**A017** In two thousand four, LKAB told the town the news. To keep mining, it had to dig right under the old center. So Kiruna had a choice. Close the mine the whole town lives on, or move the town. [11]

---

## CH2 — How do you move a town?

**A018** They chose to move. The new center would be built about two miles to the east, on solid ground. [2][11]

**A019** And the bill goes to the mine. LKAB pays for the move. [12]

**A020** So what happens if your house is in the way? You get a choice. Take the market value of your home, plus twenty-five percent extra, in cash. Or get a brand-new house in the new part of town. [12]

**A021** Sounds like a pretty good deal, right? But imagine leaving the street you grew up on. The window you always looked out of. The corner shop where everybody knew your name.

**A022** In the old center, whole blocks now stand empty behind tall blue fences, waiting to be torn down. The mayor once said locals feel caught between two towns. [13]

**A023** But some buildings are too special to leave behind. So Kiruna does something kind of wild. It picks them up, and drives them away. [14]

**A024** Lundbohm's own house was cut into three pieces, loaded onto trucks, and put back together in the new part of town. Today it's a little museum with a café. [14][15]

**A024B** How do you move a house, anyway? You disconnect the water and power, slide huge steel beams underneath, jack the whole thing up, and roll it onto a trailer. Then you drive very, very slowly, and hope nobody sneezes. [14]

**A025** Old wooden homes rolled down the road on giant trailers, one at a time. More than twenty historic buildings have made the trip so far. [14][1]

**A026** In twenty eighteen, the new town hall opened. They call it the Crystal, because its sharp shape copies the angles of iron crystals. [16]

**A027** And right next to it stands an old friend. The clock tower from the old town hall was taken apart and rebuilt beside the new one. Same clock, new address. [16]

**A028** The new center is still growing. Shops, a library, apartments, a swimming pool. A whole town, built from scratch, while people keep living in it. [2]

## CH3 — The church on wheels

**A029** Now, remember that church Lundbohm wanted people to love? Here it is. Kiruna Church, finished in nineteen twelve. [17]

**A030** Lundbohm told the architect to build something like a Sámi hut. So it looks like a giant dark-red wooden tent, with a roof that almost reaches the snow. [17]

**A031** And people really do love it. In two thousand one, Swedes voted it their favorite building from before nineteen fifty. In the whole country. [17]

**A032** So you can't knock it down. But you also can't leave it sitting on top of a mine.

**A033** So engineers spent more than a thousand hours planning one single trip. About three miles. In one piece. [1]

**A034** The church weighs about six hundred seventy-two tonnes. That's roughly the weight of a hundred elephants. [1]

**A035** It went onto remote-controlled trailers with two hundred twenty-four wheels. And the driver steered the whole thing with a joystick, like a video game. [1][18]

**A036** On August nineteenth, twenty twenty-five, it started rolling. And in its first hour, it moved, wait for it, about thirty meters. [1]

**A037** Nobody cared. Around ten thousand people came to watch. Swedish TV streamed the whole thing live, for hours, with dozens of cameras. The slowest show on television, and people loved it. [18]

**A038** On the second day, the church reached its new spot near the new town center. And the King of Sweden was there to welcome it. [18]

**A039** A hundred and thirteen years old, three miles, two days, and one very patient driver. Then it was lowered onto brand-new foundations. [1]

## CH4 — Not done yet

**A040** So that's it, right? Town moved, church safe, happy ending?

**A041** Not quite. In August twenty twenty-five, LKAB announced that the mine affects even more of Kiruna than they expected. [2]

**A042** Another twenty-seven hundred homes. About six thousand more people. That's roughly a third of the town. [2][19]

**A043** The bill for that new area alone? About twenty-two and a half billion Swedish kronor. More than two billion dollars. And the town asked the Swedish government for help. [2][19]

**A043B** Meanwhile the old center keeps shrinking. Some of the buildings people pass every day are already gone, and one by one the empty blocks come down. [13]

**A044** It's not only houses, either. The Sámi reindeer herders of Gabna say the mine and the town have squeezed their migration route past Kiruna into a narrow strip of land. [20]

**A045** So why keep digging at all? Because Kiruna's iron ends up in steel all over Europe. And in twenty twenty-three, LKAB found something even bigger right next door. [7][21]

**A046** Europe's largest known deposit of rare earth elements. Those are the metals inside phone speakers, electric cars and wind turbines. Europe badly wants its own supply, instead of buying almost all of it from abroad. [21]

**A046B** But mining it would also cut through some of that last reindeer route. So the next chapter of Kiruna is still being argued about, right now. [20]

**A047** So the ground under Kiruna is worth more than ever. Which means this town will probably keep moving, a little bit at a time, for years.
