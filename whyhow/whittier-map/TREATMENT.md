# Whittier, Alaska — the town in one building (test fragment)

Style: **3D Map Explainer** (`map-explainer`, Lemo-Opuscar). English only, voice Kokoro `am_puck` at speed 1.05 (the owner's Buckle Up voice), burned-in English subtitles plus an `.srt`. 1920×1080, 24 fps, ~46 s, −14 LUFS. For How It's Run (US audience).

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **Funnel (the owner's brief)**: globe → North America → Alaska → Anchorage → Whittier; the building pin; the tunnel ignites; rise over Prince William Sound. One continuous camera, from the world to one building and out again.
2. **Journey along one line**: start at Anchorage and fly the real road east along Turnagain Arm into the tunnel and out at Whittier; the building reveal comes at the end. Strong, but the hook ("almost everyone lives in one building") would pay off too late.
3. **Single glowing point on a dark region**: open on one lit window block in a dark region at night, then climb out to reveal the mountains. It's moody, but it can't show the 60-mile distance from Anchorage the brief asks for.

**Chosen: 1, the funnel.** The owner specified the dive, and it is the clearest way to show *why* the town is enclosed: mountains on three sides and the sound on the fourth. To differ from the library demo, it does not end back on the globe. It ends on a low, rising crane over the water, with the closed tunnel glowing cold behind it.

## 2. Logline and arc
Almost everyone in a tiny Alaskan port lives in one 14-floor tower, and the only road in is a one-lane tunnel that closes every night.
- Setup: the dive from space finds a speck 60 road miles from Anchorage.
- Turn: walls of mountain and water; the one building; the one tunnel, shared with trains.
- Ending: the tunnel line dims to cold white as night falls, and the camera rises over the sound with "The only way in closes at night."

## 3. Benchmark
- Learn from: news-organisation 3D terrain explainers (one dive, one point, out again) and low-sun documentary aerials, where shadows stand the relief up.
- Do not take: their colours, icons, fonts or camera paths. No logos and no brand names on screen.

## 6. Cue map
84 bpm, so 1 bar = 2.857 s; 46 s ≈ 16 bars.
- Bars 1–2 (orbit): a low sine pedal on D with 2nd and 3rd harmonics, and thin high air.
- Bars 3–4 (dive): a whoosh into the dissolve, then a pizzicato pulse on each beat.
- Bars 5–6 (enclosure): bowed glass chords and one soft chime per mountain label.
- Bars 7–10 (building): a frame drum on bar downbeats, a chime on the ring pulse, data ticks per card line.
- Bars 11–13 (tunnel): a sub thump and an electric shimmer when the line ignites, cello-like spiccato under the moving train glyph.
- Half a bar of near-silence before W06 at about 37.8 s.
- Bars 14–16 (rise): the pedal returns, a horn-like swell, and the end chime on the end text.

## 9. Facts (see FACTS.md)
- 14 floors and 196 units: Alaska Public Media (2013).
- ~275 residents, almost all in one building: Alaska Public Media / Alaska Beacon (2025).
- 2.5-mile single-lane tunnel shared with trains, about 60 road miles from Anchorage: Alaska Public Media / Alaska Beacon (2025).
- Closes each night: Alaska Public Media (2021).
- The exact closing hour is not stated by any of these sources, so the film says only "closes at night".
- The NPR page (2015) returned HTTP 503 and was not used.
