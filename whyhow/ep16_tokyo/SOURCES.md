# EP16 How Tokyo Moves 40 Million People Without Chaos (the trains): sources

Researched 2026-11-25. Confidence: H = a primary source states it, or several solid sources agree; M = figures vary by source/date, or the source is secondary; L = estimate / single secondary source / blog, say it on screen as "about" / "reportedly".
Wikipedia was used only as a pointer; no number below rests on Wikipedia alone.
Japanese government PDFs (MLIT congestion survey FY2025, MLIT delay "visualisation" FY2024, MLIT congestion trend chart) and JR Central's Integrated Report 2025 were downloaded and text-read directly. jreast.co.jp PDFs and gov-online.go.jp blocked automated fetch; those claims were read through search snapshots and are marked.
**Three warnings before writing the script:**
1. **"Tokyo is the world's biggest city" is now out of date.** The UN's *World Urbanization Prospects 2025* (Nov 2025) ranks Jakarta first (~42 m), Dhaka second (~37-40 m), Tokyo **third (33.4 m)**. The "37 million" is the four-prefecture Greater Tokyo total, a different definition. Say "the biggest metro area in the rich world" or just "37 million people", not "the world's largest city".
2. **Shinjuku's Guinness number changed.** The live Guinness page now says **2,704,703 a day (2022)**; the famous "3.6 million" is the older record (pre-COVID). Use 2.7 million for "the record", or say "before COVID, about 3.5 million".
3. **Tessei is JR East, not the Tokaido line.** The "7-minute miracle" crew cleans JR East Shinkansen (Tohoku/Joetsu/Hokuriku etc.) at Tokyo Station. Do not put it over Tokaido (JR Central) footage or N700 trains.

## Best cold open

**14 November 2017, 9:44 a.m., Minami-Nagareyama station.** A Tsukuba Express train leaves 20 seconds early. No one misses it. No one complains. The company posts an apology anyway: "We deeply apologise for the severe inconvenience." Then the turn: *and yet* the government's own data show that on Tokyo's most famous line, the Yamanote, a delay certificate is issued on about 15 of every 20 weekdays, and at peak hour 64,800 people are squeezed through one stretch of the Tozai line in sixty minutes. So how does a system that is late most days, and full to 160 %, still move 40 million trips without chaos? (#10, #13, #17)
