The famous Las Vegas Strip isn't actually in the city of Las Vegas. The city drinks most of its water twice. In the 1950s its hotels threw parties for nuclear explosions. And under its streets, hundreds of people live in flood tunnels.

How does a city of more than two million people survive in a desert with about four inches of rain a year? We look at the springs that gave Las Vegas its name (and how the town drained them), the casino bosses who escaped the city in 1950, dawn bomb parties, the smallest share of the Colorado River, the war on lawns, the "third straw" under Lake Mead, the ghost town that came back out of the water, the dam workers' town where gambling is still illegal, and the 600 miles of tunnels under the city.
