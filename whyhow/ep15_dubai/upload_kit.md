Tuesday, April 16, 2024. Jets taxi through water at one of the busiest airports on Earth, and more than 1,200 flights are cancelled. Dubai normally gets about half as much rain in a year as Phoenix, Arizona. That day it got about a year and a half of it.

So how does a city of 4.6 million people stay alive in one of the hottest, driest places on the planet? It's not oil: today oil is only about one percent of Dubai's economy. We follow the pearl town that lost everything in 1929, the 1979 day Jebel Ali got a port and a desalination plant, the machines that turn seawater into almost all of Dubai's tap water, the underground pipes that carry cold to whole neighborhoods, the imported food and even imported sand, and what the 2024 flood revealed.

## Honesty notes
- The 254.8 mm figure was measured at Al Ain (Abu Dhabi emirate), not in Dubai. The film says only "about a year and a half of rain" for Dubai, based on 142 mm in 24 hours.
- Cloud seeding did not cause the flood (UAE weather service). The film says so.
- "Almost all" tap water is desalinated, not "100%", because DEWA keeps reserve wells.
- There is no free footage of the flooded airport. The film shows the real CC0 street photos and the NASA and ESA satellite images instead. The real flood images are the CC0 street photos and the NASA and ESA satellite images.
