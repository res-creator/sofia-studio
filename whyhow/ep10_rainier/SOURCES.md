## Popular claims checked

| Claim | Verdict | Notes / source # |
|---|---|---|
| "Rainier last erupted in the 1890s / 1894" | **Unproven** | Newspaper reports only; no deposits found; USGS calls 19th-century evidence "scanty and tenuous". Last magmatic eruption ~1,000 years ago. (Even USGS's own 2002 fact sheet said "19th century" - now superseded.) (#5) |
| "Rainier has 5x the ice of all other Cascade volcanoes" | **Disputed ratio** | USGS FAQ says >5x; NPS says "equivalent to" all others. Safe: "more than all the others combined". (#4) |
| "150,000 people live on top of its last mudflow" | **Mixed up** | 150,000 (USGS 2002) = people on deposits of *all past* lahars. People in today's mapped hazard zone: 78,049 (2009) / 90,000+ (2015). The *last* big flow (Electron) only covered the Puyallup/Orting valley. Osceola is the *largest*, not the last. (#22-24) |
| "Electron Mudflow was in 1502" / "~500 years ago" | **Refined** | Tree rings now give **1507, late summer** (Geology 2026). (#18) |
| "Orting has 40 minutes" | **Half right** | ~1 hour total travel time to Orting in the worst case; 40 min (or as little as 30) is the time left *after detection*. USGS 2024 model: as little as 50 min total. (#27-29) |
| "A 30-foot wall of mud will hit Orting" (AP 2006 etc.) | **Overstated for Orting** | The USGS model front at Orting is ~4 m (13 ft) deep, moving ~9 mph. 30 m (98 ft) was Electron's depth upstream at Electron, not at Orting. (#19, #29) |
| "The Bridge for Kids crosses the Carbon/Puyallup River" | **Wrong for phase 1** | The bridge being built spans **State Route 162**; a Carbon River bridge is a later, unfunded phase 2. Older plans/renderings (and Wikipedia) mix them up. (#41) |
| "The Bridge for Kids opened in 2026" | **Unconfirmed** | Target slipped from March to summer 2026; in July 2026 still "final stretch". Do not say it is open. (#41) |
| "The Osceola Mudflow was 3.8 km³" | **One estimate** | Current USGS page says 2-3 km³. Say "about half a cubic mile" or "billions of cubic meters". (#12) |
| "Osceola lowered the mountain by 2,000 ft" | **Unverified** | Old estimate repeated by HistoryLink; current USGS only says the summit collapsed leaving a 1.8 km crater. Avoid a number. (#11) |
| "Human camps near Enumclaw were found under 75 ft of mud" | **Unverified** | HistoryLink only; no primary source found. Drop it. (#15) |
| "The 2025 swarm meant magma is rising" | **Wrong** | No sulfur gases; cause = hydrothermal fluids; alert stayed Normal. (#45) |
| "Rainier's summit crater lake is the highest lake in North America" | **Shaky** | Lake exists inside the ice caves, but size/elevation come from secondary sources, it may be seasonal, and "lake" is debatable. Say "a small lake hidden under the summit ice". (#8) |
| "The sirens are tested with the real lahar sound every month" | **Wrong** | Monthly test is a Westminster chime + voice; real alert sounds different and longer. (#37) |
| "Lahars only come with eruptions" | **Wrong** | Electron (1507) had no known eruption; ~35 debris flows in Tahoma Creek since 1967 were weather/glacier driven. (#19, #42) |
| "Rainier is the most dangerous volcano in the world/US" | **Framing** | USGS: "Very High" threat (the top NVEWS category; 57 of 161 US volcanoes are rated high or very high) and "potentially the most dangerous in the Cascades". Don't say "world". (#1) |
