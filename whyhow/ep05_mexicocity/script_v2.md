## COLD OPEN

**X001** This is the Angel of Independence, in Mexico City. When it opened in nineteen ten, you climbed nine steps to reach it. Today, there are twenty-three. [12]

**X002** Nobody built it taller. The column stands on thousands of wooden piles, so it sinks too, but far more slowly than the street around it. [12][13]

**X003** In the worst parts of this city, the ground drops up to forty centimeters a year. That's more than a foot. And the height it loses never comes back. [1][41]

**X004** So how do you run a city of more than twenty million people on ground that keeps falling away? And why can't anyone stop it? [46]

---

## CH1 — The lake under the streets

**X005** Here's the short version. Mexico City sits on the bottom of an old lake. Under its streets lies a thick layer of soft clay, and that clay is about seventy percent water. [22]

**X006** For more than a century, the city has been drinking the water underneath it. About seventy percent of its supply is groundwater. [31]

**X007** Pump the water out, and the clay squeezes flat, like a wet sponge under a brick. Everything on top goes down with it. [26]

**X008** It has already gone down a long way. In nineteen ten, the city center stood almost two meters above the level of the old lake. By nineteen fifty-nine, it was five and a half meters below it. The city had sunk below its own lake. And that's why, today, this city has to pump its sewage uphill. [8][35]

---

## CH2 — Killing the lake

**X009** The Aztec capital, Tenochtitlan, stood on an island in that lake. The Spanish built their city on its ruins, and the lake kept flooding it. In sixteen twenty-nine, parts of the city stayed under water for about five years. [24]

**X010** So, to survive, they decided to kill the lake. It took almost three centuries of canals and tunnels. In nineteen hundred, the Gran Canal opened, and most of the lake drained away. [24][25]

**X011** By then, the city was already drinking from hundreds of wells, sunk straight into the old lakebed. [26]

**X012** In nineteen twenty-five, an engineer named Roberto Gayol compared new measurements with ones taken almost fifty years earlier. The city center had dropped. He warned that the city was sinking. Few people listened. [26]

---

## CH3 — The cathedral they sank on purpose

**X013** When the ground sinks unevenly, buildings tilt.

**X014** The Metropolitan Cathedral was already sinking while it was being built. Its builders hid it, wedging in stones and making the cornices uneven, so it would look straight. [14]

**X015** By nineteen eighty-nine, one end of the cathedral was almost two and a half meters lower than the other, and cracks were spreading through it. [14]

**X016** They couldn't lift the cathedral, so they lowered the high side. Workers dug thirty-two shafts, and through narrow pipes they pulled out clay, a little at a time, about one and a half million times. In five years, the high side came down by almost a meter. [15]

**X017** It's still sinking, along with the whole city. But now it sinks almost evenly. [16]

**X018** A few blocks away, the Palace of Fine Arts has sunk so far that its original entrance hall is now the basement. Visitors walk in through what used to be the floor above. [17]

---

## CH4 — Sewage that has to go uphill

**X019** Remember the city sinking below its own lake? That broke the drains.

**X020** Sewage only flows downhill. But by the nineteen fifties, the city's sewers sat four meters below the canal they were supposed to empty into. In the floods of nineteen fifty and fifty-one, people crossed the city center in boats. [35]

**X021** So Mexico City started pumping its sewage uphill. And in nineteen sixty-seven, engineers made a radical decision: dig drains so deep that however far the city sank, they would never stop working. [35][36]

**X022** The first deep tunnel opened in nineteen seventy-five, with some shafts more than two hundred meters deep. [36]

**X023** But a single tunnel can't be shut down for repairs. So in twenty nineteen, a second one opened, sixty-two kilometers long. It's the longest sewage tunnel in the world. [38]

---

## CH5 — The sinking moved out of town

**X024** Back in nineteen forty, the city had banned new wells in the old center. Later, it moved its pumping out to the edges. Downtown, the sinking slowed, from up to half a meter a year to about ten centimeters. [27]

**X025** But the water still had to come from somewhere. So the edges started sinking faster. In Nezahualcoyotl, on the old lakebed east of the city, parts of the ground dropped about three centimeters a month. [2][27]

**X026** Today, about two point seven million people live in sunken bowls, where rainwater collects, and floods are worse. [3]

---

## CH6 — Thirsty and flooded

**X027** And here's the paradox. A city built on a lake is running out of water.

**X028** More than a quarter of its water is pumped from reservoirs in the mountains, lifted more than eleven hundred meters uphill. In twenty twenty-four, after a long drought, those reservoirs fell to about a quarter full, and some homes got water for an hour a week. [31][32][33]

**X029** Sinking ground cracks the pipes, and between leaks and illegal taps, up to forty percent of the water never reaches a home. [34]

---

## CH7 — Can it be stopped?

**X030** So, can Mexico City stop sinking?

**X031** Not completely. The lost height is gone for good. And the clay is still squeezing out water from decades of past pumping. How fast it sinks now depends mostly on how thick the clay is. [28][41]

**X032** But it can stop making things worse, and it has done it before, in the old center. Now it's fixing leaks, and since twenty nineteen, it has put more than seventy thousand rain collectors on people's roofs. [27][42]

---

## ENDING

**X033** Mexico City drained the lake it was built on, and then drank the water out of the mud. So the city fell below its own lake, and now even its sewage has to climb.

**X034** And the Angel of Independence still stands at the heart of it all. Over the years, the city has added step after step at its feet, so people can keep climbing up to it. Fourteen so far. And counting. [12]

**X035** So, would you live in a city that sinks a little every day? And which city should we explain next? Tell me in the comments. This was How It's Run.
