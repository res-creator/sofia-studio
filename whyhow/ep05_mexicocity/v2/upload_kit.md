## Description
```
When the Angel of Independence opened in 1910, you climbed nine steps to reach it. Today there are twenty-three. Nobody built it taller: the street around it sank.

Mexico City sits on the clay bed of an old lake, and for more than a century it has been pumping its drinking water out of that clay. In the worst places the ground drops up to 40 cm a year. By 1959 the city centre had sunk five and a half metres below the level of the lake it was built on, and its sewage now has to be pumped uphill. We look at the engineer who first noticed, the cathedral they sank on purpose to straighten it, the palace whose entrance hall became the basement, the deep drains and the world's longest sewage tunnel, and why a city built on a lake is running out of water.
```
