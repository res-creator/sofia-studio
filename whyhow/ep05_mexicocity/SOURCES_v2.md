## Popular claims checked

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Line 12 collapse (3 May 2021, 26 dead) was caused by subsidence | **Wrong.** DNV's investigation blamed bad welding of studs, missing bolts and design/construction flaws. Subsidence does damage L12, but did not cause the collapse. https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/world/report-blames-poor-welds-for-mexico-city-subway-collapse |
| "Mexico City sinks 50 cm a year" | **Exaggerated as an average.** Only the worst spots (east: Neza, Iztapalapa, Chalco). Downtown is ~8–9 cm/yr (#1); many areas almost zero. AP's "nearly 10 inches a year" is also a hotspot figure. |
| "NISAR shows 2 cm/month = 24 cm/yr" | **Careful.** It is a preliminary dry-season snapshot; NASA says not to extrapolate. |
| Bellas Artes "sank 4 m" / "12 m" | **Unclear.** "4 m" has no primary source; Waltham says ">3 m below street level" and, separately, "12 m below its original altitude" (own + regional sinking). Say "metres". |
| Cathedral "one side 1.5 m lower" (EP05 wording) | Under-states it: 2.42 m max differential by 1989 (#14). |
| Sinking will stop if pumping stops | **Mostly wrong** (#28, #41): clays keep draining for decades; lost height never comes back. |
| ScienceAlert "downtown up to 40 cm/yr" | Not supported; ignore. |
| Airport "sinking 2 cm/month" | Consistent with NISAR and the 2019 AICM study (21–30 cm/yr), but no peer-reviewed runway figure found. |
