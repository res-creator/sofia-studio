## Description
```
February 8, 1912. Starving and freezing on the way back from the South Pole, Robert Falcon Scott's party stopped on the Beardmore Glacier to collect rocks. They dragged 16 kilos of them to the very end. Among them were fossil leaves of Glossopteris: leaves from a forest, in Antarctica.

Antarctica once had forests, rivers and dinosaurs. About 90 million years ago there was even a swampy rainforest about 900 km (560 miles) from the South Pole. So how does a green continent freeze? Not because it drifted to the pole: it was already there. Not because of the asteroid: it got warmer afterwards. We follow the real fossils, from Cryolophosaurus on Mount Kirkpatrick to the palms of Wilkes Land, the slow fall of carbon dioxide, the ocean ring that walled the continent off, what the ice hides today, and what is starting to move at Thwaites Glacier.
```

## Honesty notes
- Dinosaurs and palm trees are 13 million years apart and are never shown together; the THEN image on cover C is an Eocene forest with no animals.
- The main cause of the freeze is falling CO2; the ocean gateways helped, and when Drake Passage opened is still debated.
- Errors in the reference film that we do not repeat:
  - the record cold was in 1983, not 1982;
  - "bacteria in Lake Vostok" was never confirmed;
  - the name is Trinisaura;
  - the lake count is out of date;
  - Gamburtsev peak heights were wrong.
