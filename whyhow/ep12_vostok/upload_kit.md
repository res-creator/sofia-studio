## Description
```
April 12, 1982, 4 a.m. At Vostok Station, in the middle of Antarctica, the power station catches fire. By morning, twenty men have no electricity, no heating and no power to melt snow for water. One man is dead. And no plane can reach them for seven months.

Vostok is where the coldest air temperature ever measured on Earth was recorded: −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F), on July 21, 1983. So how do people survive there at all? Not with technology alone, and not with rescue. We look at why the station was built there, what thin air and −70 °C do to a body and to a machine, the 227 days after the fire, the record morning of 1983, the lake under four kilometres of ice, and the lesson that shaped the new Vostok station of 2024.
```
