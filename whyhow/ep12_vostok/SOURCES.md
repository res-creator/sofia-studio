# EP12 Vostok Station: how people survive the coldest place ever measured: sources

Researched 2026-11-20. Confidence: H = a primary source states it, or several solid sources agree; M = figures vary by source/date or the source is secondary; L = estimate / single secondary source / memoir, say it on screen as "about" / "reportedly".
Wikipedia was used only as a pointer; no number below rests on Wikipedia alone.
Russian sources are cited in Russian and the claim is translated. Two AARI bulletins (2013, 2017) were downloaded as PDFs and text-read directly. Papers Past (1961 article) and nature.com news blocked automated fetch and were read through search snapshots; marked where relevant.
**Warning about the AARI 60th-anniversary bulletin (2017)**: it is the best single source, but it contains two typos: it dates the fire "12 April **1983**" (the 27th Soviet expedition wintered in 1982; every other source says 1982) and the record "**23** July 1983" (WMO and AARI's own 2013 bulletin: 21 July). Use its names and numbers, not those two dates.

## Popular claims checked

| Claim | Verdict | Notes / source # |
|---|---|---|
| "-89.2 °C on 21 July 1983 is the coldest temperature ever measured" | **True (station air temperature)** | WMO-verified (2011). Satellites saw -98 °C *snow surface* elsewhere; different measurement. (#10, #13) |
| "-89.4 °C" / "-89.6 °C" | **Wrong** | Conversion/rounding errors; logbook says -89.2. (#12) |
| "The coldest place on Earth is Vostok" | **Coldest measured by a station; not the coldest spot** | Ridge between Dome A and Dome Fuji is colder by satellite; Dome A typically 5-6 °C colder. (#13) |
| "Fire on 12 April 1982 killed electrician Alexei Karpenko" | **Name right, job wrong-ish** | He was head of the diesel power station / mechanic, not "electrician". (#34) |
| "19 men survived" | **Probably 20** | Sources split 19/20; most Russian sources say 20 survivors. (#35) |
| "Candles from diesel and asbestos" | **Half true** | Asbestos wick, **paraffin** fuel. Stoves burned diesel. (#38-39) |
| "The doctor was A. Maximov" | **True** | Arkady Maksimov, later professor. (#28, #65) |
| "They waited until November for help" | **True** | Il-14 in early Nov 1982, traverse ~2 weeks later; 227 days. (#44) |
| "Lake Vostok has been sealed for 15-25 million years" | **Overstated** | The lake basin may be ~15-20+ Myr old; the water is ~1 Myr old on average. (#47) |
| "Life was found in Lake Vostok" | **Not confirmed** | 2013 "new bacterium" was contamination; the PLoS ONE accretion-ice study is disputed. (#52) |
| "Lake Vostok is 1 km deep under 4 km of ice" | **Roughly right** | >1,000 m deep in the south, 3,750-4,150 m of ice. (#46) |
| "Vostok has its own 300 Club" | **Not found** | Only an unsourced Wikipedia "200 Club"; Vostok does have a banya/sauna. (#30) |
| "Air at Vostok has as little oxygen as Elbrus summit" | **Exaggerated** | Pressure equals ~3,900-4,100 m; Elbrus is 5,642 m. (#8, #27) |
| "The new station cost $100+ million" | **Roughly** | ~7.5 bn roubles (~$100 m at 2020 rates) per Meduza; other split figures exist. (#57) |
| "Drilling fluid was 60 tonnes of kerosene" | **Unverified number** | Kerosene + Freon fluid is confirmed; the tonnage was not found in a primary source. (#49) |
