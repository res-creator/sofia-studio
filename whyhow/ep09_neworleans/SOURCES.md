# EP09 New Orleans below sea level: sources

Researched 2026-11-18. Confidence: H = several solid sources agree or a primary source states it; M = figures vary by source or date; L = estimate / single secondary source, say it as such on screen.
Wikipedia was used only as a pointer; it is not cited as the source of any number below.

| 7 | Natural subsidence before 1900: **~1 mm/yr or less**. After 1900 (human drainage): often **5-25 mm/yr** locally; in parts south of the ridge **~35 mm/yr over the last 70 years**. | Hydrogeology Journal 2024 (#4) citing Kooi et al. 2019, Kolker et al. 2011 | H |

| 9 | Groundwater under the city is today mostly controlled by **leaking underground pipes** (they act as unplanned drains). Groundwater drops to ~1.5 m below surface in dry periods. ## Popular claims checked

| Claim | Verdict | Notes / source # |
|---|---|---|
| "The Netherlands bought / copied Wood's pumps for the Zuiderzee" | **Overstated / unproven** | ASME says his pumps "were built in the Netherlands, Egypt, China and India" but gives no detail. Local histories say the Dutch wanted to buy pumps and Wood would not travel; he sent blueprints. No Dutch record found that Wood pumps drained the Zuiderzee polders (main Dutch stations e.g. Wortman/Lelystad are not described as Wood pumps). Safe wording: "Dutch engineers came to study his pumps." (#13, #15) |
| "New Orleans can pump 1 inch in the first hour, then 0.5 in/hour" | **Misleading** | SWBNO says it is a rule of thumb, not a design standard; real pumping capacity is ~0.5 in/hour; the first half inch just fills pipes. (#18) |
| "Most / 80% of New Orleans is below sea level" | **Overstated** | About half (Campanella) to ~60% (Deltares/Tulane 2020) of the urban area. The ~80% figure usually quoted is how much of the city *flooded* in Katrina (not re-verified here), not its elevation. (#4) |
| "New Orleans was always below sea level / built in a bowl" | **Wrong** | Before the 1890s essentially all of it was at or above sea level; the bowl is man-made by drainage. (#1-3) |
| "Louisiana loses a football field every hour" | **Out of date** | USGS: every ~34 min at the worst, ~100 min in 2010-2016 (#37). |
| "The original Wood pumps are still in use" | **Partly true** | Many Wood-design pumps are still in service (BGR 2023; "almost 50" in 2005), but whether the *very first* 1915 units at DPS 1 still run is unconfirmed. Say "pumps of Wood's design, some about a century old". (#14) |
| "The pumps run on 1915 turbines" | **True for one unit** | Turbine 4 dates from 1915 (The Lens 2023), but it is one of several power sources; new frequency changers are coming online. (#20, #21) |
| "The West Closure Complex is the world's largest pump station" | **Qualified** | It is the Corps' claim for the world's largest *drainage* pump station; say "the Army Corps calls it...". (#35) |
| "The 1927 dynamiting saved New Orleans" | **Wrong** | Upstream breaches soon relieved pressure; 64 Parishes calls the blast unnecessary. (#29) |
| "The Mississippi will switch to the Atchafalaya by 1975 / any day now" | **Unverified date** | The 1975 date appears only in a newspaper column; the Corps' 1953 conclusion (per LUCEC) was ~1990 if uncontrolled. Say "engineers warned it could happen within decades". (#24) |
| "The Mid-Barataria diversion is just paused" | **Outdated** | Officially cancelled July 2025. (#38) |
| "Above-ground tombs are only because of the water table" | **Oversimplified** | Water table + Spanish/French vault tradition + status/cost. (#39) |
