## Description
```
130 years ago, almost none of New Orleans was below sea level. Today about half of it is, and parts of the east sit 12 to 14 feet below the sea. Every time it rains, the city has to pump the water uphill, out of the bowl and into Lake Pontchartrain. And all that draining is a big reason it keeps sinking.

We look at the 1895 map that hid the first sign, the 1915 screw pumps that are still at work, the strange 25-hertz power that runs half the pumps, the river that wants to leave the city for the Atchafalaya and the floodgates that stop it, the levee New Orleans blew up in 1927 to protect itself, the walls that failed in 2005 before the water reached their tops, and why the city can never stop pumping.
```
