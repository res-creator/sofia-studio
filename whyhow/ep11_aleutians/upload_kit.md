On Sunday, June 7, 1942, Japanese soldiers landed on Attu, the last island at the far western end of America. Its villagers were shipped to Japan, and about half never came back. Today Attu has nobody at all. Adak, once a Navy town of thousands, is down to a few dozen people in winter. Why do America's Aleutian Islands keep emptying?

It isn't the war, and it isn't the weather: people lived here for 9,000 years. We follow the line that has kept these islands alive since 1745, from the Russian fur ships to the camps of 1942, the battle for an empty island at Kiska, the Navy town of Adak with its McDonald's and its "national forest", the 17-day blackout of 2025, and the new money that is coming back now.
