## Verdict on the thesis
**Mostly supported, with two corrections.**
1. **Supported**: every emptying since 1745 lines up with an outside link being cut or withdrawn: Attu 1811 ("no ship for five years", #22); the post-1867 "decline in services" that halved Attu (#23); the Komandorski battle that cut Japan's sea supply and forced Kiska's evacuation (#37, #39); four villages never resettled because they were "too costly to travel to" (#35); Adak collapsing 96% when the Navy money left (#46), then losing its fish plant when its cod quota was struck down (#49), and going dark for 17 days in 2025 waiting on a barge and a repair crew (#50). The one place that grows, Dutch Harbor, is plugged into the world's seafood market and imports most of its workforce (#60-61).
2. **Correction 1 (weather)**: weather is not the cause, but do not claim it is irrelevant: fog and wind are what make every supply line expensive (flights twice a week, ferry only to Unalaska, digging seasons of two months). Frame it as "weather sets the price; the supply line decides".
3. **Correction 2 (the first collapse)**: the Russian-era collapse (~80% dead, #19) was caused by violence, forced labor and disease brought *by* the new outside link, not by a link being cut. Before 1745 the Unangan lived ~9,000 years with no outside supply line at all (#13, #16). The accurate line: "For 9,000 years the islands fed their people. Since 1745 the people have lived on a supply line from outside, and every time it was cut, the islands emptied."
4. **War**: the WWII emptying was a direct military decision (evacuation, Japanese deportation), so "not war alone" is fair: what kept Attu and the lost villages empty *after* the war was cost and policy, not the fighting.
