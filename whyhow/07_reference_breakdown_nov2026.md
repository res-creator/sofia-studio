# Step 7 — How the reference channels build their films (19 Nov 2026)

What I looked at:
- **Неприступная Земля**: the full vidIQ visual breakdown of "Курилы: острова, с которых исчезли люди" (135K views, 24:29), the storyboard frames, and the transcripts of four films (Kurils, Chukotka/Alaska, North Canada vs Siberia, Taiga vs Amazon).
- **¿Y si?**: the full vidIQ breakdown of "How Long Would You Last ALONE on a DESERT ISLAND?" (3.1M) and the storyboard of "The Farthest You Can Get From Another Human" (3M).

## 1. Hook: always the same 5 moves (Kurils, Chukotka, Canada)
1. **Dated cold open with one strange, concrete event.** "November 2006. A wave leaves the Kurils. 8.5 hours later it breaks piers in California… on the Kurils the water rose 20 m, and nobody saw it."
2. **The mystery question.** "People lived here 3,500 years ago. Why was nobody on these shores?"
3. **Kill the obvious answer #1 with a fact.** "The first answer suggests itself: a closed border. But tourists go to Iturup."
4. **Kill the obvious answer #2.** "Who would live among 30 volcanoes? But archaeologists find villages thousands of years old among the same volcanoes."
5. **Roadmap of 3 stops, with the last one teased.** "First we'll try to reach the middle of the chain… then go back 200 years to Urup… and at the end we'll find out what really emptied these islands."

The Chukotka film uses the same moves: same gold, 25 t each → 737K vs 48K people → "Chukotka is colder? Anadyr is only 4° colder than Nome" → "more dangerous nature? Alaska had the 9.2 quake".

**Our hooks** give a payoff and a question, but we never *debunk the obvious answers* and never give a *roadmap*. Add both.

## 2. Narration
- **Pace: 119–131 words per minute** (all four films). We run about 160–190 wpm (Kokoro speed 1.08). That is very fast for documentary, and it makes us cram facts. **Proposal: speed 1.0, pause 0.35–0.4 → about 145–150 wpm.** This needs the owner's OK.
- Short declarative sentences + "But…". Every number gets a **scale conversion** ("700 km — like Moscow to Minsk", "20 m — a 7-storey building").
- **Micro-hooks every 3–5 min**: "Now you have to land on shore…", "Worse is what the earth and water do…", "So why did people leave again and again?"
- **Human micro-stories**: one named person per chapter (a hunter's 1874 diary, a marooned 1795 outpost commander).

## 3. Picture (Kurils)
- Average shot is **3.2–4.5 s**: hook 2.5–3.5 s, map and data parts 4–5.5 s. Our 2–4 s cutting is already faster than theirs.
- What fills the screen:
  - maps 30%;
  - drone and landscape 25%;
  - photoreal AI history stills with parallax 20%;
  - big text and number cards 12%;
  - archival photos and documents 13%.
- **We don't copy the AI stills** (our rule, and YouTube's inauthentic-content risk). Instead we use real archival photos, paintings and engravings, framed like museum prints with a slow push. More archive also fills the "history" share honestly.
- **Map style** (this is what makes the film feel premium):
  - dark navy sea, muted relief land, gold for routes and labels, red for danger;
  - snap-zoom from the world to a single island;
  - pulsing rings on a point, routes drawn as animated arcs, wave rings spreading across the ocean.

  We already have a navy and yellow palette. What we lack is *relief land*, *snap-zoom from globe to island* and *pulsing rings / route arcs*. Build these as a reusable `relief map` scene.
- **Text system**:
  - date/place badge bottom-left ("НОЯБРЬ 2006");
  - a full-screen huge gold number on a dark card ("124 ДНЯ", "20 000");
  - a white title card for a person's name ("ИВАН СВЕШНИКОВ");
  - side-by-side comparison cards;
  - small grey source captions under archival images.
  - **Every number said aloud also appears on screen.**

  Our rule of "≤6 number cards over footage" is fine, but their full-screen number cards on a dark background are separate beats. Allow 4–6 of those too.

## 4. Sound
- Dark ambient / cinematic drone, sparse piano and strings for the history parts. ¿Y si? uses a steady mid-tempo synth bed.
- **Silence before the shock**: the music drops out, a dry voice line, then a sub-boom.
- **A foley layer under everything**: wind, surf, gulls, ship creaks, helicopter rotors. Plus a sub-boom on date cards, a whoosh on map moves, and a projector click on archival photos.
- **We have only music + voice.** Add an ambience bed per scene type (sea, wind, rain, city) and 4 SFX: sub-boom, whoosh, click, riser. Free sources: Pixabay sound effects (Pixabay licence) or our own synthesis, the same way we make the music.

## 5. Ending
- **Bookend**: the last image mirrors the first (bottles on the slope → "if a 20 m wave came tomorrow, nobody would know until next summer").
- **A personal question CTA**: "Would you live on an island like this for one year, knowing the next ship might not come?" ¿Y si? asks "How long do you think you'd survive?" Both beat our "Which place should we explain next?" for comments. Use a personal question, then the "next place" line.

## 6. Structure and length
- Kurils (24 min), 9 beats: hook → getting there → hostile nature → disasters → deep history → one-island case study → scientific verdict → modern abandonment → synthesis.
- Comparison films (Canada vs Siberia, Taiga vs Amazon) use a **countdown of N reasons**.
- ¿Y si? 10–20 min, a ladder of needs, with each chapter ending in a bridge line to the next.
- Our films are 6–8 min, so YouTube only allows a mid-roll ad from 8 min. **Target 12–15 min for the Aleutians** (about 1,900–2,200 words at 145 wpm).

## 7. Applied to EP11 — the Aleutian Islands (draft structure)
1. **Cold open, dated**: "June 1942. Japanese troops land on an American island. They find 42 people…" (Attu). Then the mystery: "Today Attu has nobody. Adak was a town of 6,000; now about 150. Why do America's Aleutians keep emptying?"
2. **Kill the obvious answers**:
   - "the war?" — Adak grew *after* the war;
   - "the weather?" — Unangan people lived here for about 9,000 years.

   Then the roadmap: "first we try to get there, then 1942, then the town the Navy left, and at the end, what really empties these islands."
3. Getting there: a 1,900 km chain (a scale conversion: London to …), fog, williwaw winds, flights twice a week, the price.
4. Deep history: 9,000 years of Unangan sea hunters, baidarkas.
5. Russian fur traders, and the population crash.
6. 1942: Attu and Kiska occupied, the Unangan evacuated to camps (the human micro-story), the battle of Attu, Kiska "invaded" with no enemy there.
7. Cold War Adak: 6,000 people, a McDonald's, the "national forest" of ~33 trees; the 1997 closure.
8. Today: Attu has no residents, Adak's empty houses.
9. The verdict, which mirrors the Kurils film: the islands empty when the supply line from outside is cut.
10. Bookend + a personal CTA.

Every footage source must be real and checked before the script: the 1943 US Army film "Report from the Aleutians" (US gov, PD), NARA/Signal Corps photos, USFWS Alaska Maritime NWR, NOAA, US Navy Adak photos, Wikimedia Commons.
