PINNED COMMENT
Sources: Gregor MacGregor called himself "Cazique" (ruler) of Poyais on the Mosquito Coast (today's Honduras), based on a 1820 land grant from a local king "for rum and jewellery". A 355-page guidebook ("Sketch of the Mosquito Shore", 1822, signed by a "Captain Thomas Strangeways", probably written by MacGregor or his helpers) sold the dream. A £200,000 Poyais loan was offered in London in October 1822; land went for about 2 shillings 3 pence an acre (just over £11 for 100 acres); titles and army commissions were sold, and a cobbler was made "Shoemaker to the Princess". Settlers swapped their gold for Bank of Poyais notes printed in Scotland. Two ships (Honduras Packet, Kennersley Castle) carried about 250 people; they found no capital, only jungle. Accounts of the deaths differ (Wikipedia: at least 180 died); fewer than 50 ever came home. A French court tried MacGregor for fraud in 1826 and acquitted him. He died in Caracas in 1845 with a Venezuelan military pension and was buried with full military honours.
Images: MacGregor portrait (c. 1820s) and the "View of the Port of Black River" engraving from the 1822 guidebook and the Bank of Poyais note are archive public-domain images (via Wikimedia Commons). The bond, land certificate, job card and court paper in the video are re-created, not originals; the flag is an illustration.
