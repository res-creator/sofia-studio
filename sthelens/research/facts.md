## 1. Awakening (March 1980)

- **Mar 20, 1980. First strong earthquake.** **[VAR]**
  - **Time:** 15:47 PST (3:47 p.m.).
  - **Magnitude:** 4.2. Source: PP1250 Endo et al., p. 93: "At 1547 PST on March 20 a magnitude-4.2 earthquake beneath Mount St. Helens marked the first strong quake of an earthquake swarm…"
  - **Other values:**
    - PP1250 Christiansen & Peterson, p. 17: "magnitude 4.0+ at 1547 PST".
    - PP1249, p. 16: "3:37 p.m." and "magnitude 4.1".
  - **Suggested wording:** "about 3:45 p.m., magnitude ~4".
- **Earlier small quakes.** FS036 says small earthquakes "began on March 16". PP1250 (Endo et al.) describes "a gradual increase in seismicity starting in mid-March". **[OK]**
- **The swarm.**
  - **Climax:** the late afternoon of Mar 25, "when 24 earthquakes of magnitude 4 and greater occurred during an 8-hr period" (PP1250 p. 17). PP1249 says 22 in one 8-hour period and up to 5 per hour. **[VAR, minor]**
  - **Mar 26:** the 100th quake of M ≥ 3.5 since Mar 20 (PP1249 p. 18).
- **Quakes per day.**
  - After Mar 25: "about 30 events per day having a magnitude of 3 or greater (6/day of magnitude 4 or greater)" (PP1250 p. 17).
  - April: "generally more than 30/day of magnitude 3 or greater" (PP1250 p. 19).
  - Mid-May: "20–40/day" of M3+, "including 5–10 of magnitude 4 or greater" (PP1250 p. 21).
- **Totals.**
  - "Of over 10,000 earthquakes recorded … between March 20 and May 18" (PP1250 Endo et al., p. 93).
  - FS036: "By May 17, more than 10,000 earthquakes had shaken the volcano."
  - About 240 quakes of M ≥ 4 between Mar 20 and Apr 30 (PP1249, Apr 30 entry).
- **Mar 27. First phreatic (steam-blast) explosion.**
  - **Time:** "a loud boom was widely heard at 1236 PST on March 27" (PP1250 p. 18). PP1249 says "about 12:30 p.m.". At 11:20 a.m. a National Guard spotter had already seen "a hole in the icecap" (PP1249 p. 18).
  - **Ash column:** rose to about 2,000 m above the volcano (PP1250 p. 18).
  - **New crater:** "about 60–75 m across … in the northern part of the old 400-m-wide ice-filled summit crater" (PP1250 p. 18).
  - **Fracture:** an east-trending fracture "nearly 1,500 m long" crossed the summit (PP1250 p. 18).
  - **Later growth:** a second crater appeared Mar 29. The craters merged by Apr 7 into one crater about 500 × 300 m (PP1250 p. 19). FS036: "Within a week the crater had grown to about 1,300 feet in diameter."
- **First eruptions, in context.** They were the first in the conterminous U.S. since Lassen Peak (1914–17). Mount St. Helens had not erupted since 1857 (PP1250 p. 17). **[OK]**
- **Who led, and when they arrived.**
  - **Mar 21:** USFS (Gifford Pinchot NF) and UW/USGS seismologists phoned the USGS Volcanic Hazards Project in Denver (PP1250 Miller et al., p. 789).
  - **Mar 24:** David Johnston, a USGS volcanologist from Menlo Park who was vacationing near Seattle, "visited the seismology center and stayed on" (PP1249, Mar 24 entry).
  - **Mar 25:** "that evening Mullineaux arrived in Vancouver" (PP1250 p. 791). PP1249: "Mullineaux will stay on to direct the USGS study of the volcano."
  - **Mar 26:** Mullineaux briefed agencies and gave a joint USFS/USGS press conference. Access was restricted on Mar 26 (PP1250 p. xi, p. 791).
  - **Mar 27:** USGS issued a formal "Hazards Watch" to more than 300 officials (PP1249 p. 18).
  - **Crandell:** later "Mount St. Helens Hazard Coordinator" (PP1249, Apr 23 entry, p. 32). He, Mullineaux and C. Dan Miller served as hazards coordinators from late March to late October (PP1250 p. 789).
  - **Scientist-in-Charge in Vancouver:** Donald W. Peterson (USGS-37 photo caption).

## 2. The 1978 Crandell & Mullineaux report (Bulletin 1383-C)

- **PDF:** https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1383c/report.pdf. It is 26 pages (C1–C26) with 2 plates.
- **Exact quotable sentences** (checked against the scanned text):
  - **p. C1 (Abstract):** "Mount St. Helens has been more active and more explosive during the last 4,500 years than any other volcano in the conterminous United States."
  - **p. C1 (Introduction):** "…we believe it to be an especially dangerous volcano because of its past behavior and the relatively high frequency of its eruptions during the last 4,500 years."
  - **p. C2:** "In the future, Mount St. Helens probably will erupt violently and intermittently just as it has in the recent geologic past, and these future eruptions will affect human life and health, property, agriculture, and general economic welfare over a broad area." The sentence starts at the foot of C1.
  - **p. C22:** "Future eruptions of Mount St. Helens are a near certainty. It will not be possible to prevent them, or to stop them after they have begun."
  - **pp. C24–C25 (the key line):** "The volcano's behavior pattern suggests that the current quiet interval will not last as long as a thousand years; instead, an eruption is more likely to occur within the next hundred years, and perhaps even before the end of this century." The sentence starts at the foot of C24 and ends on C25.
  - **p. C25:** "…the greatest potential danger will exist at or soon after the onset of volcanic activity."
  - **p. C24:** "The present dormant state of Mount St. Helens began in 1856, and no way is now known of determining when the volcano will erupt again."
  - **p. C9, on lateral blasts:** "Explosions can also cause lateral blasts of great force which can carry steam and rock fragments from the dome outward at a high speed to distances of at least 10 km." It ties this risk to dome eruptions, not to a landslide.
  - **p. C4 (Table 1):** a past "Strong laterally directed explosion threw rock debris from side of dome (Sugar Bowl) NE. to distance of 6 km."
- **Earlier version of the line.** The 1975 *Science* paper (Crandell, Mullineaux & Rubin, p. 441) said "…an eruption is likely within the next hundred years, possibly before the end of this century." This is quoted in PP1249 p. 14–15. **[OK, as quoted by USGS]**
- **Distribution.** The report and a hazard letter went out as a "Notice of Potential Hazard" in December 1978 (PP1249 p. 15).
- **USGS verdict after the eruption.**
  - PP1249 p. 15 says the report was "remarkably prophetic, differing from the actual eruptive effects of May 18, 1980, mainly in not anticipating such an extensive and devastating lateral blast."
  - PP1250 p. 798 adds that "the potential for a north-flank landslide" was "not anticipated by Crandell and Mullineaux (1978)".

## 3. The bulge (cryptodome)

- **First recognized on Mar 27.**
  - "The prominent topographic bulge that had been noted on the north flank of the volcano on the first day of eruption…" (PP1250 p. 20). The Mar 27 photo caption already shows "an uplift or bulge on upper north flank" (PP1250 p. 18, fig. 6).
  - USFS spotter David Gibney "reported seeing the large fractures open and close and the uplifted north flank continue to break and rise" (PP1250 p. 18).
  - Austin Post (USGS) suggested on Mar 27 that the north side "might have slumped". After that, "a large landslide on the north slope was identified thereafter as a potential hazard" (PP1250 p. 793).
- **Scale.**
  - Ground ruptures at the 5,400-ft level were first noted Apr 3 (PP1250 p. 20).
  - "By mid-April, the bulge had become ominous" (PP1250 p. 143).
  - Photogrammetry showed up to ~100 m of uplift and outward movement between Aug 1979 and Apr 12, 1980 (PP1250 p. 143).
  - By May 12, the high point stood 150 m above the former slope, and Goat Rocks had moved 106 m north (PP1250 p. 20–21).
- **Growth rate.** **[VAR]**
  - USGS image caption: "growing at a rate of up to five feet (1.5 meters) per day. By May 17, part of the volcano's north side had been pushed upwards and outwards over 450 feet (135 meters)." https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/bulge-north-side-mount-st-helens-developed-magma-push
  - FS036: "the north flank had grown outward at least 450 feet".
  - The scientific report (PP1250 p. 143, abstract) says targets moved "1.5–2.5 m/day" (about 5–8 ft/day). Christiansen & Peterson (p. 21) say "consistent rates of about 2 m/day".
  - Norm Banks says "about six feet a day" in the USGS CoreCast film (see materials.md).
  - **Suggested wording:** "about five feet a day". The rate was nearly constant: "No appreciable change occurred in this rate … even up to 1 1/2 hr before the climactic eruption" (PP1250 p. 21).
- **Volume.** The bulge added about 0.11 km³ by May 18, presumably the volume of the dacite cryptodome (PP1250, Moore & Albee).
- **Measurement methods** (PP1250 Lipman, Moore & Swanson, p. 143–153):
  - **Theodolite and laser ranging:** "Every 1–3 days … horizontal and vertical angles were measured by theodolite to as many as 19 unmanned, fixed targets; slope distances were measured by electronic laser instruments" (EDM / geodimeter). Measurements started in mid-April. Data cover Apr 23 to May 18.
  - **Tilt:** continuously recording platform tiltmeters, plus "the ice-covered surface of Spirit Lake was utilized as a large liquid tiltmeter", plus spirit-level tilt triangles at the Timberline and Spirit Lake parking lots (p. 144).
  - **Coldwater 2 line:** an 8-km line ran from Coldwater 2 to a glass prism at Goat Saddle (p. 152).
  - **Also used:** photogrammetry (Moore & Albee), a time-lapse camera on Dogs Head (PP1249, Apr 30), and thermal infrared surveys.
- **Last measurements, morning of May 18.**
  - Three readings were "radioed in by David Johnston on the morning of May 18, the last at 0653 PDT – only an hour and a half before the catastrophic failure" (PP1250 p. 153). They showed the rate had slowed to about 0.5 m/day on that line.
  - PP1249 says "about 7:00 a.m." **[VAR, minor]**
- **What scientists expected, and what they did not know.**
  - **Before May 18, landslide plus eruption was discussed.** PP1250 Miller, Mullineaux & Crandell, p. 793: "On April 14 … Crandell described the character of a possible massive avalanche from the north side of the volcano … pointing out that it could move into the Spirit Lake area and possibly extend down the North Fork Toutle River valley. Crandell also explained that such a landslide or a large earthquake could trigger a magmatic eruption. The magmatic eruption was expected to be like those of the past, however, the chief dangers being voluminous airborne ash and pyroclastic flows." The same warning went to other groups on Apr 23, and in a letter from Crandell to the Forest Supervisor.
  - **Lipman et al. (p. 143):** the "oversteepening of slopes … was recognized as posing a major hazard … from gravitational failure and landsliding". In late April and early May, landsliding "were likely, and we hoped that changes in deformation rates could be observed as indications of imminent failure. Unfortunately, no such changes took place."
  - **Not expected: the size of the lateral blast.** PP1250 p. 789: "the magnitude of the catastrophic and unprecedented landslide and lateral blast of May 18 greatly exceeded our expectations." p. 798: the blast "extended about three times farther from the volcano than the largest known previous blast at Mount St. Helens, and it devastated an area that probably is 10–15 times larger."
  - **Johnston's post was thought safe.** PP1250 dedication: he "was caught at an observation post that was considered relatively safe."
  - **No short-term warning.** PP1250 p. xi: "the catastrophic eruption on the morning of May 18 commenced without any additional precursory signs that were recognized either in advance or by hindsight."
  - **Crandell to the Seattle Times, Apr 6** (as quoted in PP1249 p. 26): "Mount St. Helens has done so many different things in the past that hardly anything would be a surprise … The only thing it hasn't done is blow itself apart."
  - **[UNC] Was a large landslide-triggered *lateral* blast explicitly forecast before May 18?** The best USGS primary text (PP1250 p. 793) says the eruption a landslide might trigger was expected to resemble past eruptions (ash and pyroclastic flows). Do not claim that a lateral blast of this size was predicted. Some later secondary accounts (e.g. Stepankowsky, below) say a lateral eruption was feared in early May. That is not verified in a primary text.

## 4. Access decisions

- **Early closures.**
  - **Mar 25:** USFS closed the forest above timberline (less than 2 to nearly 4 miles from the summit). The Skamania County Sheriff closed SR 504 about 5 miles NW of the peak (PP1249, Mar 25 entry).
  - **Mar 26:** closures were extended (PP1250 p. 791).
  - **Mar 27:** Truman "refused to leave in spite of strong urging" (PP1249, Mar 27 entry). About 300 loggers left three Weyerhaeuser camps on Mar 27–28 (PP1249).
- **Apr 3.** Governor Dixy Lee Ray "declared a state of emergency and set up a 'Mount St. Helens Watch Group'" (PP1249, Apr 3 entry). **[OK]**
- **Apr 30: the Red and Blue Zones.** **[OK, with a caveat]**
  - PP1249 (Apr 30 entry): "Governor Ray and the Forest Service's Forest Supervisor Robert Tokarczyk … closed additional areas near the volcano to entry. A 'Red Zone' was established around the peak to distances ranging from about 3 to 8 miles and included the Spirit Lake area. Only scientists, law-enforcement officers and other officials, and search-and-rescue personnel are permitted in this zone. In a 'Blue Zone,' which extends beyond the Red Zone, logging operations are allowed to continue, and property owners holding special permits may enter, but no overnight stays are allowed."
  - PP1250 fig. 454 caption, p. 795: in the Blue Zone, "access was allowed from 0800 to 1700 by landowners or controllers with a permit". The figure shows "road blocks effective April 30".
  - The WA Secretary of State blog (https://blogs.sos.wa.gov/fromourcorner/?p=22187) says Ray's first executive order, dated April 30, 1980, limited entry into the red zone. It is one of six eruption-related orders that year. Copy from search results only: the page and the state archive (digitalarchives.wa.gov) returned 404/502 from this session. **The order number is not verified [UNC].**
  - **Penalty:** "$500 or a 6-month jail sentence" (PP1249, May 16 entry).
  - **Zone lines followed property, not hazard.** "land-access restrictions often did not closely match forecast volcanic-hazard zones" (PP1250 p. 791).
- **May 25.** Ray "by executive order, extended the Red Zone … out to 20 miles in all directions". The penalty became $1,000 or a year in jail (PP1249, May 25 entry).
- **Harry R. Truman.**
  - Proprietor of the Mount St. Helens Lodge at the SW end of Spirit Lake (PP1249).
  - Age 83, born Oct 1896, at Spirit Lake since 1926 (HistoryLink essay 22801, https://www.historylink.org/File/22801).
  - His quote, as reported: "the mountain will never hurt me" (HistoryLink, quoting press).
  - He stayed "in defiance of government authorities … and has become something of a folk hero" (PP1249, May 17 entry).
  - He was presumably buried under hundreds of feet of avalanche debris (PP1249 fig. 19 caption).
  - On May 17 visitors pleaded with him to leave. The last people to see him left after 6 p.m. (HistoryLink). **[secondary, but well sourced]**
- **Cabin owners, May 17 and May 18.**
  - "law-enforcement officers today escorted about 50 carloads of property owners to their property in the Red Zone to retrieve some of their possessions. Those who entered … were required to sign liability releases … and to be back out by nightfall. Authorities have agreed to allow a second caravan of property owners to go in at 10 a.m. tomorrow, if the mountain remains quiet." (PP1249, May 17 entry, p. 41)
  - PP1249 p. 1 (abstract): "Limited entry was, in fact, granted on May 17 and scheduled also for the morning of the cataclysmic eruption."
  - Sheriff Closner (Columbian, quoted in PP1249): "No one would listen … People were going around, through, and over the barricades."
- **Loggers and the Sunday.**
  - PP1249 (Apr 1): "about 300 loggers have returned to their jobs on and near the lower flanks … and at Camp Baker". PP1249 (May 18): "a few loggers … had long been on the move".
  - **"Hundreds would have died on a weekday" [UNC, secondary].**
    - Steve Olson (National Geographic, 2016): "If it had happened during a weekday, hundreds of loggers would have died." https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/160320-mount-st-helens-eruption-logging-volcano-olson-ngbooktalk
    - A. Stepankowsky (ex-Columbian reporter, opinion piece): "Hundreds of loggers were working within a few miles of the bulging north flank … the previous week."
    - Both are informed opinions, not counts. Frame it as "hundreds of loggers worked there on weekdays."
- **How many of the dead were inside the zones?** **[UNC: secondary, with a contradiction]**
  - Steve Olson (MPR, 2016): "Only three of the victims had violated the danger zones … Two of them had permission to be there. The third, Harry R. Truman, refused to leave." https://mprnews.org/story/2016/03/18/mount-saint-helens
  - Stepankowsky: "Only three of the dead are known to have been in the zones, and all three had permission to be there." https://andrestepankowsky.substack.com/p/jimmy-carters-death-is-occasion-to
  - **Partial confirmation in USGS text.** PP1249 (May 17): Johnston was "legally" in the Red Zone. Reid Blackburn at Coldwater I was "not inside the restricted zone", 2.4 mi west of the Red Zone line. Gerry Martin was "just outside the northern boundary".
  - **Safe wording:** "most of the 57 were outside the restricted zones."

## 5. May 18 sequence

- **08:32 PDT earthquake, M 5.1.**
  - FS036: "Within 15 to 20 seconds of a magnitude 5.1 earthquake at 8:32 a.m., the volcano's bulge and summit slid away."
  - Voight (PP1250 p. 69): quake at 08:32:11.4 PDT, rockslide motion began at about 08:32:21. **[OK]**
  - PP1250 overview (p. 4): "The avalanche began 10–20 seconds after the 0832 earthquake."
- **Collapse as successive slide blocks** (PP1250 Voight et al., p. 347): "a 2.3-km³ rockslide detached … Within 26 s, the first of a retrogressive series of blocks had moved 700 m – velocity of the first slide block was about 50 m/s and increasing – and a second block had moved 100 m." The Stoffels saw it from the air: "the whole north side of the summit crater began to move instantaneously … like nothing we had ever seen before" (PP1249 p. 45).
- **Debris avalanche volume.** **[VAR: 2.5 km³ and 0.67 mi³ do NOT match]**
  - 0.67 cubic miles = 2.8 km³.
  - FAQ-H and FS036: "2.8 cubic kilometers (0.67 cubic miles)".
  - Voight et al. (PP1250 p. 347 and p. 371): 2.5 km³ for the main mapped part; "about 2.8 km³ for the entire deposit"; 2.3 km³ in place on the cone before failure.
  - **Suggested wording:** "2.8 km³ / two-thirds of a cubic mile".
  - "the largest on Earth in recorded history" (FS036) / "largest subaerial landslide in recorded history" (USGS FAQ).
- **Avalanche travel.**
  - About 22.5–23 km (14 mi) down the North Fork Toutle (FAQ-H "22.5 km (14 miles)"; PP1250 p. 23 "as far as 23 km").
  - Average deposit depth 150 ft (46 m), maximum 600 ft (FS036). It covered about 60 km² (23 sq mi).
  - Speed 70–150 mph (FS036 table).
- **Spirit Lake.** Raised by about 60 m (more than 200 ft) (PP1250 p. 22, 786; PP1249 p. 44).
- **Lateral blast.**
  - **Speed** **[VAR]**:
    - FS036: "accelerated to at least 300 miles per hour".
    - Eyewitness timing gives 140–150 m/s (310–335 mph) to the north (PP1250 Rosenbaum & Waitt p. 60).
    - A seismic station 3.5 mi out went dead 77 s after the quake, implying 220–250 mph (PP1249 p. 44).
    - Voight: near-source 50 to more than 200 m/s.
    - NPS: "up to 670 mph" (https://www.nps.gov/subjects/volcanoes/blasts.htm). This is an outlier; avoid it.
    - **Suggested wording:** "at least 300 mph".
  - **Area** **[VAR]**:
    - 230 sq mi (FS036, USGS-37). NPS: "230 square miles (600 square km)".
    - Moore & Sisson (PP1250 p. 421): "affected an area of 600 km²".
    - Kieffer (p. 379): "about 500 km²". PP1249 abstract: "more than 150 square miles".
    - **Use 230 sq mi / ~600 km².**
  - **Reach:**
    - "as far as 17 miles northward" (FS036 text). The FS036 table says "reached 17 miles northwest of the crater".
    - Trees were leveled up to 28 km from the crater (Moore & Sisson).
    - The arc measured 23 miles east–west (PP1249 p. 44).
  - **Zones:**
    - *Tree-removal* or "direct blast zone": "in an inner zone extending nearly 10 km from the summit … virtually no trees remained".
    - *Tree blowdown* or "channelized blast zone": "all standing trees were blown to the ground".
    - *Seared (singed) zone*: "at the blast's outer limit the trees were left standing but thoroughly seared".
    - Sources: PP1250 p. 22; Kieffer p. 379 (zone names); Moore & Sisson p. 421.
  - **Temperature:** inner zone above 350 ± 50 °C, outer limit 50–200 °C (Moore & Sisson). FS036: "As high as 660 °F (350 °C)".
  - **Timber:** 4 billion board feet blown down (FS036).
- **Coldwater II and Johnston Ridge.**
  - Coldwater II was "5.7 miles from the mountain's summit" (PP1249 dedication) and "5.7 miles north-northwest" (PP1249 p. 42). Media usually round this to "about 6 miles". **[VAR, minor]**
  - "The lateral blast obliterated Coldwater II observation station" (PP1249 dedication).
  - Coldwater I was 13.5 km (8.4 mi) NW (USGS image page, https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/coldwater-observation-post-later-called-coldwater-i-135-km-8; PP1249 p. 42).
- **Summit height.** **[VAR, ±2 ft]**
  - 9,677 ft before. 8,363 ft after; 1,314 ft removed (FS036).
  - PP1250 Jordan & Kieffer, p. 134: 2,950 m (9,677 ft) to 2,549 m (8,364 ft).
  - FAQ-H: rim high point 8,365 ft, about 1,300 ft lost.
- **Crater.** 1.2 mi E–W × 1.8 mi N–S, 2,084 ft deep (FS036).
- **Plinian column.**
  - "within less than 10 min had risen to a height of more than 20 km" (PP1250 p. 23).
  - FS036 table: "about 80,000 feet in less than 15 minutes".
  - "The Plinian phase … continued vigorously for 9 hr" (PP1250 p. 24). It began to wane at about 17:30 (p. 24).
  - About 540 million tons of ash fell over more than 22,000 sq mi (FAQ-H). FS036 says 520 million tons. **[VAR, minor]**
- **Pyroclastic flows.**
  - "beginning just after noon, swift avalanches of hot ash, pumice, and gas … poured out of the crater at 50 to 80 miles per hour and spread as far as 5 miles to the north" (FS036).
  - At least 700 °C. Deposits up to 120 ft cumulative (FS036).
  - The peak of the eruption was between 3:00 and 5:00 p.m. (FS036).
- **Lahars.**
  - Largest: the North Fork Toutle, fed by water seeping from the avalanche deposit "through most of the day". It reached its maximum "at about midnight in the Cowlitz River about 50 miles downstream" (FS036).
  - It swept up "thousands of logs from timbering operations … destroying most bridges across the river" (PP1250 p. 23).
  - Totals: "27 bridges, nearly 200 homes" damaged (FS036).
  - The Columbia River channel went from 40 to 14 ft deep, stranding 31 ships (FS036).
  - Smaller lahars ran down the Muddy River and Pine Creek into Swift Reservoir. The reservoir had been lowered in advance. The largest, about 14 million m³, "was easily contained" (PP1250 p. 798).
  - Witness on the South Fork Toutle (Dergan/Reitan): a "railroad trestle moving at about 25 mi/hr appeared … with an enormous log jam behind it" (PP1250 p. 63, site 40NW).
- **Ashfall east.**
  - Spokane: "complete darkness in Spokane, Washington, 250 miles from the volcano" (FS036).
  - Yakima: two runners near Cowiche said that "by this time (10:30 a.m.), it was black as a dark night" (PP1249 p. 59–60).
  - Ash causing "darkness eastward for more than 200 km" (PP1250 p. 23).
  - Ash crossed the U.S. in 3 days and circled Earth in 15 days (FS036).

## 6. People

- **Death toll: 57.** **[VAR in early USGS texts]**
  - FS036 table: "Human 57". USGS-37: "Fifty-seven people … died". The USGS CoreCast page: "David A. Johnston and 56 others".
  - PP1250 Foreword (1981) says "about 60 lives". PP1249 says "about 60 persons dead or missing".
  - Official counts kept climbing: 14 dead and 90 missing on May 21; 24 dead and 45 missing on June 17 (PP1249).
  - **Use 57.**
- **Causes of death.** Of 22 autopsies: "Sixteen deaths resulted from inhalation of hot ash, three from burns, and three from head injuries (two of these caused by falling trees)" (PP1249, June 17 entry, p. 98–99).
- **David A. Johnston.**
  - "30-year-old volcanologist", 1949–1980, from Illinois, PhD at the University of Washington in 1978. His specialty was volcanic gas (PP1250 dedication).
  - On May 17 he relieved Harry Glicken at Coldwater II (PP1249 p. 42). Photo "David Johnston at Coldwater II, 1900 hours, May 17, 1980", photographer Harry Glicken: https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/david-johnston-coldwater-ii-1900-hours-may-17-1980-dave-did
  - **Last words, USGS wording** **[VAR: punctuation]**:
    - PP1249 dedication (front matter): **"Vancouver, Vancouver, this is it!,"** he shouted into his radio.
    - PP1249 p. 43–44: "Vancouver, Vancouver, this is it," he shouted.
    - USGS texts use commas, not "Vancouver! Vancouver!". The punctuation with exclamation marks comes from later media.
    - The message did not reach Vancouver. It "was received instead on the radio of a private citizen and recorded on an attached tape recorder." A second transmission was "too garbled to be understood" (PP1249 p. 44–45).
    - So a recording reportedly existed in 1980. Its present whereabouts are unknown to us.
  - First USGS employee to die in a volcanic eruption (PP1249 dedication). His body was never found. [Secondary sources add that trailer parts were found in 1993: UNC]
- **Gerry (Gerald) Martin.**
  - Retired Navy radioman and volunteer state ham operator, on the ridge north of Coldwater II, "just outside the northern boundary of the Red Zone".

  - He died. Good, documented material for the film.

  - **Gary Rosenquist** (site 17NEc, Bear Meadow, 17 km / ~11 mi NE).

  - **Mike and Lu Moore** (site 22N, "north side of Green River", 2 interviewed).

## 7. After

- **Landscape.**
  - About 600 km² devastated, with an inner zone "nearly 10 km" of near-total tree removal (PP1250 p. 22).
  - The North Fork Toutle valley was filled for 14 mi, 150 ft deep on average (FS036).
  - Spirit Lake: "more extensive and shallower, and its shoreline is about 200 feet higher … The lake now is choked with thousands of logs blown into it by the initial blast" (PP1249 p. 104 caption).
  - Steam blasts burst through the hot deposits, with craters up to 20 m and columns up to 2,000 m (PP1250 p. 24).
  - Wildlife: about 7,000 big-game animals and about 12 million hatchery salmon fingerlings died (FS036).
- **Later 1980 eruptions.**
  - **Dates:** May 25 (0230 PDT; ash fell on western WA/OR), June 12, July 22, Aug 7, and Oct 16–18. Lava domes formed in June, August, October and December (PP1250 p. xi, p. 24–25).
  - **Size:** each produced columns 8–9 miles above sea level and pyroclastic flows (FS036).
  - **Domes:** the June and August domes were destroyed. Seventeen episodes from Oct 1980 to Oct 1986 built a dome 876 ft high (FS036).
- **Forecasts from 1980 on.**
  - "the eruptions of June 12 and August 7 were preceded by increases in level of harmonic tremor, and eruptions of July 22 and October 16 were preceded by increased numbers of shallow volcanic earthquakes. For each of these, the precursors were recognized and warnings issued at least 2 hr before the eruption began" (PP1250 p. 786).
  - "Eight significant eruptions in 1980 were preceded by multiple phenomena over periods of months to hours" (PP1250 p. 786, Decker).
  - **Caveat:** the kind, size and timing were not predicted (PP1250 p. 791, note 1).
- **David A. Johnston Cascades Volcano Observatory.** **[VAR: founding year]**
  - In 1980 there was "no formal Cascades Volcano Observatory and no dedicated office space". Scientists worked in an operations center at USFS headquarters in Vancouver (USGS-37).
  - The permanent office "was officially dedicated on May 18, 1982 … The Cascades Volcano Observatory (or CVO), as it was named, was dedicated in David Johnston's memory" (USGS-37). USGS-37 also says: "Two years later, USGS founded the Cascades Volcano Observatory."
  - **Say:** "set up in 1980, formally dedicated in Johnston's name on May 18, 1982."
- **VDAP (Volcano Disaster Assistance Program).**
  - "The two agencies [USAID/OFDA and USGS] founded a partnership known as the Volcano Disaster Assistance Program in 1986, headquartered at the USGS Cascades Volcano Observatory." https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/news/volcano-watch-us-volcano-disaster-assistance-program-26th-year
  - It was founded after Nevado del Ruiz (Nov 13, 1985), whose lahars "killed at least 25,000 people" according to that USGS page. **[VAR: commonly "more than 23,000"]**
- **Pinatubo 1991** (FS115).
  - PHIVOLCS declared a 10-km danger zone in April. VDAP scientists joined "in a few weeks". The joint team "issued urgent warnings" before the June 15 climax.
  - "The USGS and PHIVOLCS estimate that their forecasts saved at least 5,000 lives and perhaps as many as 20,000."
  - Property: "prevented property losses of at least $250 million". Aircraft and equipment at the U.S. bases: "$200 to 275 million" averted; airlines another $50–100 million.
  - "more than 15,000 American servicemen and their dependents were evacuated from Clark Air Base". "only about 250 lowland residents were killed". Of 20,000 Aeta, "all but about 20 were safely evacuated".
  - Cost: less than $1.5 million for PHIVOLCS + USGS.
- **Johnston Ridge and the Johnston Ridge Observatory.**
  - "Coldwater II would eventually be re-named 'Johnston Ridge' in honor of Dave" (USGS image page above).
  - JRO "opened in May [1997] – the 17th anniversary" (Seattle Times, Nov 5, 1997, https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19971105/2570377/humble-observatory-frames-the-awesome-power-of-nature).
  - **[UNC]** The USFS page could not be reached. The exact day is unverified; avoid it.
  - Currently closed after the May 2023 SR 504 landslide. Check status before saying "today you can visit". **[UNC]**
- **National Volcanic Monument.**
  - Public Law 97-243, **Aug 26, 1982**, "approximately one hundred and ten thousand acres". It is managed by the USFS within the Gifford Pinchot NF.
  - Statute: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-96/pdf/STATUTE-96-Pg301.pdf
  - USGS page: "established in 1982 to designate 445 km² (110,000 acres)": https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/mount-st.-helens/mount-st-helens-national-volcanic-monument
  - Some sources say Aug 27. The statute says Aug 26.

## Uncertain or contested: summary list

1. **Johnston's words.** USGS wording is "Vancouver, Vancouver, this is it!" (commas). "Vancouver! Vancouver!" is not the USGS form.
2. **Avalanche volume.** 2.5 km³ ≠ 0.67 mi³. USGS uses 2.8 km³ = 0.67 mi³ for the whole deposit (2.3 km³ in place).
3. **Bulge rate.** "up to 5 ft/day" (USGS caption) vs 1.5–2.5 m/day (PP1250).
4. **Mar 20 quake.** M4.0 / 4.1 / 4.2; 3:37 or 3:47 p.m.
5. **Blast speed.** From 220–250 mph up to "at least 300 mph". NPS gives 670 mph (outlier). Blast area: 500–600 km² (230 sq mi).
6. **Deaths.** "57" (later USGS) vs "about 60" (1981–82 USGS).
7. **Dead inside the zones.** "only 3" comes from Olson and Stepankowsky (secondary). They disagree on whether all three had permission.
8. **CVO.** Founded 1980 or 1982? It was formally dedicated May 18, 1982.
9. **304th ARRS "61 lives"** and the JRO opening (May 1997): secondary or snippet only.
10. **Lateral blast.** A landslide-triggered eruption was discussed before May 18 (Crandell, Apr 14). A lateral blast of this size was not forecast.
11. **Secondary claims to avoid.** The weekday-loggers estimate and the claims that the governor's expanded-zone order was unsigned are secondary or opinion only.
