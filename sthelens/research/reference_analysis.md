### 2:00–3:20 — explaining the territory
- A satellite map with labels (Lake Pontchartrain, river) and a red line drawn along the levees.
- A **cross-section** of the city between the river and the lake, showing the city below water level. This is the one fact a map can't show.
- The same map again, now with blue district outlines. It **returns to the same base** and adds one layer of information each time.
- Short archive (old city, the river) and a hand writing, for "studies".
- The USACE logo, then a real elevation map (red/green), then a "2004" stamp on aerial.
- Pattern: **one consistent base map**, with one new layer per return. A schematic appears only where height or below-sea-level must be understood.

### 7:25–7:45 — the document
- The real NWS bulletin text, full screen, with a slow drift.
- Everything is **blurred except one paragraph** (focus pull, then another paragraph). The viewer reads the actual words: "most of the area will be uninhabitable for weeks".
- The document is shown in context first (the whole page), then one line is isolated.

## Principles to adopt (not copy)
1. Every block answers "what did people know / do at that moment", in time order. The disaster is approached, not summarised.
2. Material is chosen per type of claim:
   - place → establishing archive;
   - threat → measurement (radar or data);
   - who knew → a real person at work;
   - what was said → original sound;
   - mechanism → map or schematic;
   - payoff → event footage.
3. Maps: one consistent base, and each return adds a layer. Schematics only for what archive can't show; reconstructions are stylised so they can't be mistaken for archive.
4. Documents: show the real page, then isolate the one line that matters.
5. Original voices get room: the narrator yields, and music dips under them.
6. Human stories are named and specific, and arrive after the mechanism. Numbers come after people.

## What we do differently
- The reference reuses copyrighted TV news, and in places shows reconstructions without a label. We use only public-domain/CC0 USGS material, and we label schematics on screen.
