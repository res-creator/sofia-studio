# Final pass after the critic review (structure unchanged)

## Facts and wording (checked against primary sources)
- **Bulge rate.** Was "about five feet a day". Now "five to eight feet a day", matching USGS PP1250 p. 143: targets moved 1.5–2.5 m/day. Norm Banks's "about six feet" falls inside that range. The USGS photo caption "up to 5 ft/day" is the lower estimate.
- **The 6:53 a.m. readings.** Was "Nothing in the data looked unusual". Now "No sign that the slope was about to fail was recognized, then or in hindsight." PP1250 p. xi: "commenced without any additional precursory signs that were recognized either in advance or by hindsight". That line had actually slowed to about 0.5 m/day (PP1250 p. 153), so "nothing unusual" was inaccurate.
- **May 17 caravan.** Was "deputies". Now "law-enforcement officers", as in PP1249 p. 41.
- **Re-recorded lines.** Only these three (b3_03, b4_03, b5_03) were re-recorded. They were checked by speech-to-text and their loudness is within 1 dB of the neighbouring lines.

## Rights status (what could and could not be confirmed)
- **Confirmed.**
  - USGS film data release: CC0.
  - USGS media pages: "Public Domain".
  - USGS CoreCast: the USGS podcasts page says CoreCast products "are considered public domain".
  - NARA USAF and USFS items: catalogued "Unrestricted".
  - Mixkit music: Mixkit Free License.
- **Not confirmed.**
  - **Music inside the USGS CoreCast film.** It is credited "Alon Levitan". No separate licence statement was found, so it is covered only by the general CoreCast public-domain statement. A Content ID claim is possible.
  - **NARA USFS prints 529556 and 529555 (search, May 28).** They have no photographer credit, look like halftone reproductions, and could originally be newspaper photos. The risk is medium.
  - **USGS pages with no "Sources/Usage" line:** the Johnston photo (USGS photographer Harry Glicken), the full-resolution Pinatubo image (a sibling page with the same caption says "Public Domain") and the Armero photos ("Photograph credit: USGS/VDAP"). These are treated as US government works.
  - **Mixkit tracks.** The licence allows monetized use, but Content ID claims on Mixkit tracks do happen and must be disputed.
