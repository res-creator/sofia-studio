TITLE
The Morning Mount St. Helens Exploded Sideways

ALTERNATIVE TITLES
- Mount St. Helens 1980: The Mountain That Exploded Sideways
- They Measured the Bulge Every Day. Then Mount St. Helens Exploded Sideways

DESCRIPTION
For two months in 1980, scientists watched the north side of Mount St. Helens swell outward, measuring it every few days. On the morning of May 18, the bulge gave way, and the eruption that followed blasted sideways across 230 square miles.

This documentary follows the event in order: the first earthquakes, the 1978 hazard report, the bulge, the Red Zone, the last evening at Coldwater II, the collapse and lateral blast, the people caught in it, the rescues, and what changed afterward. It is told through USGS archive film, photographs, documents, and the voices of the USGS scientists who were there.

What was known: the volcano was dangerous, and its north flank was unstable.
What was not known: when it would fail, and how violent the sideways blast would be.
