PINNED COMMENT
Sources: Daily Maverick (2021, drawing on Edward Jay Epstein, The Atlantic 1982) — in September 1938 Harry Oppenheimer of De Beers met the head of the New York agency N.W. Ayer to give diamonds a new image in America; US diamond sales (by carats) had fallen about 50% since 1919. Sotheby's — Ayer copywriter Frances Gerety wrote "A Diamond Is Forever" (1947 per Sotheby's; some sources say 1948); the line also discouraged people from reselling. Al Jazeera (2025) — about 10% of brides had a diamond ring in 1940, about 80% by 1980; the "salary rule" started at one month, later two ("How can you make two months' salary last forever?"). Epstein's 1982 text — a dealer said his firm would pay about $600 for a ring bought for $2,000. "3 months" in our question is for discussion, not a quote from US ads. All people in the video are generic drawings (re-enactment), not portraits; company names as plain text only, no logos.
Before you buy: ask the jeweler what they'd pay to buy that ring back — and decide the budget yourself, not by a salary rule.

Title: Your Engagement Ring Is an Ad Campaign (A Diamond Is Forever)
Description: The diamond engagement ring isn't an ancient tradition. In the late 1930s, diamond sales in America were falling, so the diamond giant De Beers hired the ad agency N.W. Ayer with one job: make a diamond mean love. After the war, copywriter Frances Gerety wrote four words: "A diamond is forever." Brides with a diamond ring went from about 10% in 1940 to about 80% by 1980, and the ads even put a price on love: one month's salary, later two. The money part: a ring bought for $2,000 might get an offer of about $600 from a dealer. "Forever" meant: never sell it. (Illustrated re-enactment; generic characters.)
