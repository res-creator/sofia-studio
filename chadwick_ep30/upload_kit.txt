PINNED COMMENT
Sources: Cassie L. Chadwick (born Elizabeth "Betsy" Bigley, Canada, 1857) claimed to be Andrew Carnegie's secret daughter. After a visit to Carnegie's New York house (she spoke only to his staff), she let a man from Ohio see a $2 million promissory note "signed" by Carnegie — a forgery. Banks lent her money, some at 25% interest; by one estimate about $2 million in all (≈ $72 million today; estimates vary). In November 1904 lender Herbert B. Newton asked for his $190,000 back and sued. Carnegie's office said: "Mr. Carnegie does not know Mrs. Chadwick of Cleveland… Mr. Carnegie has not signed a note for more than thirty years." The Citizens National Bank of Oberlin, Ohio, which had lent her money, faced a run and failed (the amount it lent differs between sources). She was arrested in December 1904, convicted in 1905 and died in the Ohio Penitentiary in 1907.
Images: Chadwick's 1904 cabinet-card portrait, Andrew Carnegie (c. 1913), the "Cassie L. Chadwick Residence, Cleveland" postcard and Carnegie's signature are archive public-domain images (Wikimedia Commons); the newspaper is the Pacific Commercial Advertiser, Dec 8, 1904 (Library of Congress, Chronicling America). The promissory note, loan papers, lawsuit and press statement in the video are re-created, not originals; the bank, the crowd and the vault are illustrations.
