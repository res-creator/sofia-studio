# Ep 30 — "Carnegie's Daughter" (Cassie Chadwick, 1897–1904) — verified facts

| Fact | Source (opened) |
|---|---|
| Born Elizabeth "Betsy" Bigley, Canada, 1857; earlier forgeries; fortune-teller "Madame Lydia DeVere" | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassie_L._Chadwick |
| 1897 married Dr. Leroy Chadwick, Cleveland; lived on Euclid Avenue (mansion photo on Wikipedia) | Wikipedia |
| Rode with a man from Ohio (Dillon — "lawyer" per Wikipedia, "banker" per Encyclopedia.com) to Carnegie's New York mansion, went in ~30 min (talked to staff: housekeeper/butler), came out and dropped a $2 million promissory note "signed" by Carnegie | Wikipedia; https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/chadwick-cassie-l-1859-1907 |
| Claimed to be Carnegie's illegitimate daughter; claimed $7m in notes at home, $400m inheritance (Wikipedia) / $10m securities at Wade Park bank (Encyclopedia.com) | both |
| Banks lent her large sums, some at 25% interest; total about $2 million (≈ $72m today) — estimates vary | Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com |
| Nov 1904: Herbert B. Newton lent $190,000, called it in and sued → scheme unravelled | Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com |
| Carnegie statement: "Mr. Carnegie does not know Mrs. Chadwick of Cleveland. … Mr. Carnegie has not signed a note for more than thirty years." (press release) — "I have never heard of Mrs. Chadwick" (hoaxes.org) | Encyclopedia.com; https://hoaxes.org/archive/permalink/cassie_chadwick |
| Citizens National Bank of Oberlin had lent her money (amount differs: $200k / $240k / $800k) → run on the bank, failed | Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com |
| Arrested 7 Dec 1904; convicted March 1905; sentence 10 yrs (Enc.) / 14 yrs (Wiki) — don't state the number | both |
| Died in Ohio Penitentiary, 10 Oct 1907 | Wikipedia |

Careful: no Oberlin amount, no sentence length; "about $2 million" as "by one estimate"; Dillon = "a man from Ohio".
