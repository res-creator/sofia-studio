PINNED COMMENT
Sources: the story of the Eiffel Tower con is known mainly from one book — "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower" (Doubleday, 1961), the memoir of ex-Secret Service agent James F. Johnson, as told to Floyd Miller. No 1925 French police file or newspaper report has been found, so treat the Paris part as the agent's account. In the standard version Lustig met scrap dealers at the Hôtel de Crillon; the amount is disputed (one account: $20,000 + $50,000 = $70,000; others say about 70,000 francs). His second attempt was reported to the police and he fled to the U.S. The rest is on the record: he made fake $100 bills (a judge said he was "like some other government, issuing money in rivalry with the United States Treasury"), was arrested in New York on May 10, 1935, escaped on a bedsheet rope on Sept. 1, 1935, the day before his trial, was caught in Pittsburgh, and was sent to Alcatraz (prisoner #300, 1936). He died on March 11, 1947, in the federal prison medical center in Springfield, Missouri — not on Alcatraz. His death certificate listed his job as "apprentice salesman".
Documents shown in the video (the letter, the death certificate) are re-created, not originals.
Photos: Eiffel Tower — Library of Congress, stereograph "Eiffel Tower, Paris, France" (1927). Victor Lustig (center) — Philadelphia newspaper photo, 1935.
If an "official" offers you a secret deal and asks for a bribe — it's not a deal.

Title: The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower (Victor Lustig, 1925)
Description: According to the Secret Service agent who later caught him, in 1925 con man Victor Lustig posed as a French government official, told scrap dealers the Eiffel Tower was rusting and would be secretly scrapped, and took a dealer's money — plus a bribe. When he tried again, someone told the police and he fled to America, where he made fake $100 bills, escaped jail on a bedsheet rope and still ended up on Alcatraz. His death certificate listed his job as "apprentice salesman". The Paris con is known mainly from the agent's 1961 memoir; documents in the video are re-created. Genius con man... or just a liar?
