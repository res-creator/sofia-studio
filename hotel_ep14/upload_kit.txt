PINNED COMMENT
Source: NBC Bay Area, "Family faces 'rude' overbooked hotel, a $1,400 lesson for all travelers" (Mar 23, 2026). The Dworkins' one-night Courtyard stay in Nassau was prepaid; they say they were turned away because the hotel had overbooked, and they found a $1,400 suite at Margaritaville around midnight. NBC says Marriott later agreed to repay the $1,400 plus the original stay; Marriott HQ and the hotel's owner did not reply to NBC. Why this hotel overbooked wasn't reported.
If it happens to you: ask the hotel to "walk" you — book a nearby room, cover the first night and get you there. A hospitality professor told NBC that's what major hotels usually do (usual practice, not a law). The report's tips: check in online early, and tell the hotel if you'll arrive late.

Title: The $1,400 Hotel Room: prepaid, then "sold out"
Description: A California family prepaid one night at a Courtyard by Marriott in Nassau, the Bahamas. At check-in they were told the hotel was sold out. Around midnight they walked the streets with their luggage and found one room left: a $1,400 suite at Margaritaville. After they contacted NBC Bay Area, Marriott agreed to repay the $1,400 plus the original stay. In hotel slang, being sent to another hotel is called getting "walked" — usually the hotel arranges it.
