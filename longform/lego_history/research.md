## 7. What actually caused the crisis (several causes, not one)
1. **The market changed** — kids moved to video games/electronics earlier; big-box retailers gained power; cheaper copies after the patents expired; a strong kroner and Danish production made LEGO expensive. (≈, Robertson/Wharton)
2. **Growth without control** — too many new toys, pieces and colours; most new pieces used once; costs rose while sales stalled since ~1993. (≈/✔ CFO.com, Robertson)
3. **Diversification away from the core** — parks, clothes, games, TV, toys that needed little building (Explore, Jack Stone, Galidor). (✔/≈)
4. **Dependence on blockbusters** — Star Wars/Harry Potter sales hid the weakness; in 2003, without new films, sales fell 26 %. (✔ annual report)
5. **A fixed-cost business** — factories, molds and stock cost money whether sets sell or not; retailers cut stock at the same time. (✔/≈)
The film says this explicitly and never claims a single cause.

## 8. Myths / risky claims to avoid
- "LEGO was weeks/months from bankruptcy" — the company itself never said that; use "on the edge", "running out of time", "close to collapse" only as framing; no day count.
- "Losing $1 million a day", specific debt totals, a bank cutting off loans — unverified.
- "The Star Wars license caused the crisis" — it was one of the few things selling; dependence was the problem.
- "Knudstorp saved LEGO alone" — owner Kjeld Kirk Kristiansen, CFO team (Jesper Ovesen, Søren Lindgaard), retailers and fans all played parts.
- Exact counts of pieces/colours — always "about"/"more than".
