# Script v2: hooks, key changes vs v1, fact check

## 1. Three hooks: written, scored, one chosen

All three use only documented facts (#22, #28, #9). Scores are 1–10 for **curiosity** (does it raise a question the viewer needs answered?) and **retention** (does the next line pull them forward, and is the first image instantly readable?).

| | Hook (first ~10 s) | Curiosity | Retention | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Money + cause** | "In 2004, the company behind the world's most famous toy lost almost two billion kroner. And part of the reason was hiding in its own warehouses." | 6 | 7 | Clear and big, but it names the place of the answer (warehouses) in the second sentence. The "why" is half-solved before the viewer asks it. Starts like every "company in crisis" video. |
| **B · The single-use piece** ✅ | "By 2004, LEGO had invented thousands of new pieces. And most of them were used in exactly one set." → "That same year … lost almost two billion Danish kroner." → "Those two facts are connected." | 9 | 8 | A concrete, visual, slightly absurd fact that every LEGO owner understands, then the stakes, then an open link between the two. The viewer gets the *what* (pieces, money) but not the *how*. That gap carries the film to 5:15. |
| **C · The 1958 brick** | "Every LEGO brick made today still fits a brick made in 1958. In 2004, almost nothing else about the company fit together." | 7 | 5 | Elegant, but abstract in the first 5 s: there's no money, no danger and no image of trouble. It also spends the ending. Better kept as the payoff (a5). |

**Chosen: B.** Only B is in `script_v2.md`. Its parts pay off later: "used in one set" → b6–b7; the money → y4–y6; "those two facts are connected" → b9 + y3; "thirty-five, joined three years earlier" → b4 and o1; the word "System" → c3, b7, a4.
