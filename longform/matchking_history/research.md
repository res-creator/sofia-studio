# Ivar Kreuger, "the Match King": verified fact sheet

Research brief for a ~10-minute How It's Run long-form film (English). Compiled 2026-11-08.

**Tags.** VERIFIED means stated by a primary/contemporary or reliable reference source, ideally two of them. DISPUTED means sources conflict (the conflict is explained). UNVERIFIED means a single secondary source only, or a claim that could not be checked.

**Source notes.**
- Britannica (britannica.com/money/Ivar-Kreuger) and ne.se returned 403/paywall, so their text could not be read. Only the Britannica search snippet was seen.
- The 1933 Fortune 3-part series (fortune.com/1933/05/01/a-3-part-series-on-the-life-and-death-of-ivar-kreuger) is the richest contemporary source, written within 14 months of his death.
- Time archive pages were read directly.
- Flesher & Flesher (The Accounting Review, 1986) was not reachable online, so nothing below is cited to it.
- Partnoy's book was not read directly. Partnoy claims come via interviews and reviews.

## 1. Early life and engineering

[1] Born 2 March 1880 in Kalmar, Sweden. Son of match manufacturer Ernst August Kreuger, who owned match factories around Kalmar. Sources: Store norske leksikon, "Ivar Kreuger" (Trond Gram, updated 4 Jan 2026), https://snl.no/Ivar_Kreuger ; EBSCO Research Starter "Ivar Kreuger" (Steven Pressman, 2024), https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/ivar-kreuger/ . **VERIFIED**

[2] Studied at the Royal Institute of Technology (KTH), Stockholm, from age 16 and qualified as a civil engineer at about 20 (combined mechanical and civil/road-and-water engineering). Sources: SNL (above); Swedish Wikipedia, citing Nordisk familjebok 3rd ed. (1929), https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivar_Kreuger . **VERIFIED** (exact graduation year: about 1900)

[3] Spent about seven years abroad as an engineer, mostly in the USA, plus Mexico, South Africa, Britain and Germany. He returned to Sweden around Christmas 1907. Sources: en/sv Wikipedia; Fortune, 1 May 1933. **VERIFIED** (the duration is approximate)

[4] US employer: Purdy & Henderson, a New York structural engineering consultancy, in 1902–03. Source: SNL (above); sv.wikipedia. **VERIFIED** (two reference works agree)

[5] US buildings. Fortune (1933) says he worked on "the Flatiron Building, Macy's, the Metropolitan Life Building, the St. Regis, and the Plaza", plus "the Syracuse Stadium". EBSCO (2024) names Macy's, the Flatiron and the Plaza Hotel. Sources: Fortune, 1 May 1933 (URL above); EBSCO (above). **DISPUTED.** These are contemporary claims, largely sourced to Kreuger's own accounts, and no building records name him. On screen, say "worked on New York skyscrapers such as the Flatiron, according to Fortune (1933)". Do not say he "built" them. He was a junior engineer at a consultancy.

[6] Syracuse: worked there as a young engineer when Archbold Stadium was built in 1907. Syracuse University gave him an honorary Doctor of Business Administration in June 1930. Source: en.wikipedia "Ivar Kreuger" (Other business). Fortune 1933 also names "Syracuse Stadium". **VERIFIED** (the stadium work rests on two sources; the honorary degree on one: **UNVERIFIED**, check with Syracuse University archives)

[7] Kreuger & Toll was founded in May 1908 (sv.wikipedia gives 18 May 1908) with Paul Toll (and cousin Henrik Kreüger). It specialised in the Kahn reinforced-concrete system. Sources: SNL; sv/en Wikipedia; Commons photo "Paul Toll, Henrik and Ivar Kreuger 1908". **DISPUTED.** Ericsson's history page gives 1910 for "Byggnadsaktiebolaget Kreuger & Toll", and the firm was reorganised as AB Kreuger & Toll in 1911 (https://www.ericsson.com/en/about-us/history/people/industrial-leaders/ivar-kreuger--the-hero-who-became-a-crook). Safe wording: "In 1908 he and Paul Toll started a building firm."

[8] Projects included the Stockholm Olympic Stadium (1911–12, for the 1912 Games), foundation work for Stockholm City Hall (1912–13) and the NK department store (1913–14). Sources: SNL (stadium); en.wikipedia (all three). **VERIFIED** for the stadium. **UNVERIFIED** for City Hall foundations and NK (Wikipedia only).

[9] Lateness-penalty and bonus contract: about $1,200 per day, for a six-storey department store. EBSCO says it finished early and earned a $70,000 bonus. Sources: EBSCO; en.wikipedia. **UNVERIFIED** (an anecdote whose figures differ between sources)

## 2. The match business

[10] From 1911 he consolidated the Swedish match industry, taking over his father's factories with bank help. In 1913 he headed AB Förenade Svenska Tändsticksfabriker ("United Match Factories", 9 factories). Sources: SNL; en.wikipedia. **VERIFIED**

[11] Autumn 1917: Förenade merged with Jönköpings & Vulcans Tändsticksfabriks AB to form Svenska Tändsticks AB (STAB, "Swedish Match"), with Kreuger as President. Source: Swedish Match company history, https://swedishmatch.com/Our-company/Company-history/Svenska-Tandsticks-AB ; SNL. **VERIFIED.** Correction: the Jönköping & Vulcan merger is **1917**, not 1913. 1913 is the year Förenade was formed.

[12] Share of world match production. The company's own history says it was "sole or dominant owner of match companies in 33 countries" and "controlled a total of 60 percent of the global production" in 1930, falling to about 20% by 1938. Source: Swedish Match history (above). **VERIFIED** (as the company's own figure)

[13] Other figures. Time (21 Mar 1932) says Swedish Match made "66% of the world's matches" in 250 plants in 43 nations. SNL says 70–80% by 1930. EBSCO says "two-thirds". Sources: Time, "Business: Poor Kreuger", 21 Mar 1932, https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,743409-2,00.html ; SNL; EBSCO. **DISPUTED** (range 60–80%). Safe wording: "about two-thirds of the world's matches".

[14] Earnings in 1930: Swedish Match $13M, International Match $20M. Swedish Match held 30% of British Match Corp. Source: Time, 21 Mar 1932 (p.2, above). **VERIFIED** (contemporary)

## 3. Monopolies for loans

[15] Model: dollar loans to cash-poor governments in exchange for national match monopolies. "The first loan was to Poland in 1925 and consisted of $6,000,000." Source: Time, 21 Mar 1932, p.2 (above). **VERIFIED**

[16] France: a $75M loan (1,875,000,000 francs, to Premier Poincaré), partly funded by $50M of International Match bonds sold in the US. Source: Time, 21 Mar 1932, p.2. **VERIFIED** for the amount. The year 1927 comes from en.wikipedia/Thunholm (**VERIFIED** as the standard date). Strictly, France granted no formal monopoly: Fortune and Wikipedia describe it as a de facto arrangement.

[17] Germany: "the biggest loan of all", $125,000,000 in 1929, secured by a ban on Russian matches. Swedish matches already supplied "a good 70%" of German matches. Sources: Time, 21 Mar 1932, p.3, https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,743409-3,00.html ; Straumann, Kugler & Weber, "How the German crisis of 1931 swept across Europe", Economic History Review 70(1), 2016, doi:10.1111/ehr.12334 ("late 1929"). **VERIFIED.** Note that some sources date it 1930 (ratification). The German match monopoly law lasted until 1983 (UNVERIFIED secondary source).

[18] Loan list 1925–31 (from Thunholm 1995, via en.wikipedia):
- Poland: 1925 $6M; 1930 $32.4M. Danzig: 1930 $1M.
- Greece: 1926 £1M; 1931 £1M.
- Ecuador: 1927 $2M; 1929 $1M.
- France: 1927 $75M.
- 1928: Yugoslavia $22M; Hungary $36M; Latvia $6M; Estonia SEK 7.6M.
- Germany: 1929 $125M.
- 1930: Romania $30M; Lithuania $6M; Bolivia $2M; Guatemala $2.5M; Turkey $10M; part of the Young Loan $15M.

Source: en.wikipedia "Ivar Kreuger", table citing Lars Thunholm, *Ivar Kreuger* (1995). **VERIFIED** for Poland, France and Germany (cross-checked with Time). **UNVERIFIED** for the rest (single secondary table).

[19] Total lent to governments: about $387M (Thunholm via Wikipedia). SNL says "over 1 billion kronor". Sources: en.wikipedia; SNL. **VERIFIED** as an order of magnitude. Safe wording: "nearly $400 million to some 15 governments".

## 4. IMCO, US securities and investors

[20] International Match Corporation (IMCO) was founded in the US in 1923 with Lee, Higginson & Co. of Boston/New York. Sources: Princeton University Library, "Kreuger & Toll Company Records" finding aid (MC078), https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC078 ; Fortune 1933. **VERIFIED**

[21] About $250,000,000 was invested in Kreuger securities in the US, three-fifths of it in International Match issues. Lee, Higginson marketed all of it directly or indirectly. Source: Fortune, 1 May 1933 (above). **VERIFIED.** Time (5 Nov 1945) also gives $250M from the US, out of $560M taken from the public.

[22] "Most widely held" claim: Kreuger & Toll stocks and bonds "were the most-widely held securities in the United States and throughout the world during the 1920s". Source: Princeton finding aid (above). **DISPUTED.** It is an archival description with no data behind it. Say "among the most widely held".

[23] Dividends. By 1924 they were "largely paid out of capital instead of from profit". A later investigation found "dividends of only 1.5% were justified by the profits of the match industry". Source: Princeton finding aid. **VERIFIED.** The often-quoted "about 25% dividends" comes only from a getAbstract summary of Partnoy: **UNVERIFIED.**

[24] Control: Class A voting shares capitalised at $50,000, majority-held by Kreuger, controlled properties capitalised at "over a billion dollars". B shares carried 1/1000 of a vote. Source: Time, 21 Mar 1932, p.3; en.wikipedia. **VERIFIED**

[25] Convertible gold debentures: $15M issued through Lee, Higginson at $94.50, 6.5%, 20 years. American Certificates: $28M pledged days after Black Monday, 1929. Source: en.wikipedia. **UNVERIFIED** (single secondary source)

[26] Lee, Higginson accepted a Swedish audit instead of a US one in 1928, "to accept a Swedish audit of the greatest Swede". Source: Time, "Business: Kreuger's Books", 18 Apr 1932, https://time.com/archive/6749148/business-kreugers-books . **VERIFIED**

## 5. Lifestyle and image

[27] Tändstickspalatset ("Match Palace"), Västra Trädgårdsgatan 15, Stockholm: commissioned 1926, designed by Ivar Tengbom, completed November 1928. It was the group's HQ until 1932. Source: sv.wikipedia; Commons category "Tändstickspalatset" (inception 1928). **VERIFIED**

[28] Homes: a penthouse at 791 Park Avenue, New York, "with trees on the roof"; a 20-room flat atop the Banque de Suède building, Place Vendôme, Paris; apartments in Berlin (Pariser Platz) and Stockholm (Villagatan 13). Also the island retreat Ängsholmen, two Rolls-Royces and three motorboats. Source: Fortune, 1 May 1933. **VERIFIED** (contemporary). Note that he died at a different Paris flat, see [36].

[29] Habits: secretive, slept little, champagne drinker. He once posed in a false beard as a fictitious "Professor Bollitov" to answer US bankers' questions. Source: Time, "Business & Finance: Greatest Crook", 23 Jan 1933, https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,744987-2,00.html . **UNVERIFIED** (an anecdote, even though it is reported by Time)

[30] Time cover: Ivar Kreuger, issue of 28 October 1929, the week of the Wall Street crash. Sources: Wikimedia Commons "Ivar Kreuger-TIME-1929.jpg" (date 28 Oct 1929, from Time's cover archive); 45worlds Time 14/18 listing. **VERIFIED**

[31] He visited the White House and had talks with President Hoover. Source: Fortune 1933. **UNVERIFIED** (single source; it is widely repeated)

## 6. Methods

[32] Off-balance-sheet and offshore entities. US capital raised in 1922 was moved to a Liechtenstein subsidiary. Others included Dutch Kreuger & Toll, Garanta (capital $200,000, owing International $18,020,000) and Continental Investment ($88M debtor to International). Sources: Partnoy interview, Newsweek (Daniel Gross, 2009), https://www.newsweek.com/ivar-kreuger-grandfather-financial-scams-79797 ; Fortune 1933. **VERIFIED**

[33] Forged Italian bonds. Kreuger forged the signatures of Italian officials (Mosconi, Boselli) on bonds of the "Italian Match Monopoly". The bonds were printed in Stockholm from plates he delivered himself.
- **Face value per Fortune (1933): £28,668,500.**
- Princeton finding aid: "a face value of 21 million pounds".
- EBSCO: 42 bonds of £500,000 each, which is £21M.
- Time (Jan 1933, Nov 1945): "$100,000,000" / "$102,000,000".

Sources: Fortune 1933; Princeton finding aid; Time 23 Jan 1933 (p.1), https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,744987-1,00.html ; Time 5 Nov 1945, https://time.com/archive/6789918/high-finance-the-house-of-matches/ . **DISPUTED on the amount.**
- Forgery: **VERIFIED.**
- The "$142M" figure was not found in any source.
- Safe wording: "about $100 million of fake Italian bonds".
- Mussolini's government confirmed the signatures were forged (Fortune).

[34] Accounting: the Price Waterhouse fraud certificate (28 Nov 1932) said the manipulations "were so childish that anyone with but a rudimentary knowledge of bookkeeping" could have seen them. One-sided entries across the group totalled SEK 2,738,361,820. Source: Fortune 1933. **VERIFIED**

## 7. Collapse and death

[35] After the 1929 crash he faced a liquidity squeeze. In 1931/32 he borrowed SEK 800M from Swedish commercial banks ("10 per cent of Sweden's GDP") and turned to the Riksbank. PM C. G. Ekman persuaded the Riksbank to lend. Source: Sveriges Riksbank, "1932 – The Kreuger Crash", https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1900-1999/the-kreuger-crash/ . **VERIFIED**

[36] ITT/Ericsson. Kreuger bought a majority of L. M. Ericsson in 1930 and sold the shares to ITT in 1931. In early 1932 ITT found he had misrepresented Ericsson's finances and demanded the deal be cancelled with repayment of USD 11M, which he could not pay. Source: Ericsson history (URL in [7]). **VERIFIED**

[37] Death: Saturday 12 March 1932, his flat at 5 Avenue Victor-Emmanuel III, Paris (third floor; now Avenue Franklin D. Roosevelt). He died of a 9 mm Browning pistol he had bought days earlier at Gastinne-Renette. He was found around 1 p.m. and left letters, one ending "Goodby now and thanks. I.K." Sources: Fortune 1933; Time 21 Mar 1932 p.1, https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,743409-1,00.html ; Riksbank. **VERIFIED.** Paris police withheld the news until markets closed (Time).

[38] Cause of death. French police ruled suicide, and that is the established record. His brother Torsten Kreuger claimed murder (*Sanningen om Ivar Kreuger*, 1965). Lars-Jonas Ångström (*Därför mördades Ivar Kreuger*, 1990) and others argue there was a murder motive. sv.wikipedia notes the investigation had serious shortcomings. Sources: en/sv Wikipedia; Dagens PS, https://www.dagensps.se/varlden/historia/blev-ivar-kreuger-mordad-vilka-tjanade-pa-det/ . **DISPUTED.** Say "police ruled it suicide; his family always insisted on murder".

[39] He was cremated in Stockholm on 22 March 1932. Source: Fortune 1933. **VERIFIED.** (A 17 March 1932 funeral procession in Paris is photographed by Agence Rol, see [53].)

## 8. Aftermath

[40] The Price Waterhouse preliminary report was read in the Riksdag on 5 April 1932. It found the 31 Dec 1930 Kreuger & Toll balance sheet "grossly misrepresented" the true position and 1930 profits "grossly overstated by means of fictitious entries". Sources: Time 18 Apr 1932 (URL in [26]); Fortune 1933. **VERIFIED**

[41] Final findings, as reported by Time on 23 Jan 1933:
- Manipulations went back to 1917 and "assumed large proportions in 1923 and 1924".
- Reported profits of more than $300M over 14 years compared with only about $40M of genuine operating profit.
- $560M was taken from the public and $164M from banks.

Source: Time, 23 Jan 1933, p.1 (URL in [33]). **VERIFIED** (contemporary)

[42] About $250M of reported assets had never existed. Source: Princeton finding aid. **VERIFIED** (this is the source of the common "$250M overstated" line)

[43] Bankruptcy: Kreuger & Toll was declared bankrupt in Sweden on 24 May 1932 and in the US on 6 Aug 1932, "the largest bankruptcy on record at that time". Irving Trust was appointed receiver for International Match on 13 Apr 1932. Sources: Princeton finding aid; Fortune 1933. **VERIFIED**

[44] Recoveries: Irving Trust found $9,881 in cash. About 24,000 creditor claims totalled $1,168,000,000 and allowed US debenture claims came to $98M. Creditors were paid about 32 cents on the dollar. Source: Time, 5 Nov 1945 (URL in [33]). **VERIFIED**

[45] Loss estimates: about $300M worldwide, about $200M in the US (Time, 18 Apr 1932, an early estimate). Bankrupt-estate debts of nearly SEK 1.1bn (SNL). **DISPUTED** (estimates vary widely, as Partnoy himself notes, via Kirkus https://kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/frank-partnoy/the-match-king). Give ranges, not a single number.

[46] Sweden: the government passed a moratorium and appointed an investigation commission (Swedish Match history). The National Debt Office rescued Skandinaviska Banken (Riksbank). PM Ekman resigned in August 1932 after first denying, then admitting, that his party had taken Kreuger money (Riksbank; en.wikipedia "Carl Gustaf Ekman"). **VERIFIED**

[47] The Stockholm stock exchange closed temporarily after the death (SNL). The exact number of days was not found. **UNVERIFIED** as to duration.

[48] US Senate: Banking and Currency Committee, "Stock Exchange Practices", Part 4 "Kreuger & Toll", hearings of 11–12 Jan 1933, pp. 1145–1396 (the pre-Pecora phase under Sen. Norbeck). Source: FRASER, St. Louis Fed, https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/stock-exchange-practices-87/hearings-s-res-84-s-res-239-33976 . **VERIFIED.** Correction: this is not "the Pecora hearings". Pecora became counsel later, in Jan 1933.

[49] Role in the 1933/34 Acts. A textbook chapter (Clikeman, *Called to Account*) says the fraud "helped build political support" for the 1933 and 1934 acts. Partnoy argues the 1930s rules were created "almost entirely" because of Kreuger (PopMatters review, https://popmatters.com/94118-the-sucker-king-2496027137.html). Partnoy also says: Price Waterhouse investigated, the Senate held hearings, and "we end up with the 1930s Security laws" (Newsweek, 2009). **DISPUTED** for sole causation. **VERIFIED** for "one of the scandals that shaped" the Acts. Do not say "Kreuger caused the SEC". The Pecora/Wall Street-wide abuses and the 1929 crash were the main drivers. The NYSE's requirement for independent audits of listed companies is also often credited to the Kreuger case (PCAOB speech, page blocked: UNVERIFIED).

## 9. Legacy

[50] Swedish Match survived the collapse. Philip Morris International took control (85.87%) on 11 Nov 2022 in a deal of about $16bn. Source: PMI Form 10-K FY2022, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1413329/000141332923000025/pm-20221231.htm . **VERIFIED.** (Today it is mainly snus and ZYN nicotine pouches; matches are a minor line.)

[51] Partnoy calls Kreuger "the father of today's financial market", "a dubious honor". He says Kreuger, unlike Madoff, had a real business: Swedish Match survived, and the German loan kept paying interest. Source: Newsweek/Partnoy (URL in [32]). **VERIFIED** (as Partnoy's opinion)

## 10. Money conversions

[52] Few sources give inflation-adjusted figures.
- en.wikipedia converts $1,200 (1908) to about $18,000.
- EBSCO claims his fortune of "several hundred million dollars" equals "$75 billion in 2010 dollars". This is implausible (it looks like a GDP-share method) and should not be used.

## Myths to avoid

- **"He built the Flatiron Building."** At most he was a junior engineer at the consultancy Purdy & Henderson that worked on it. The source is Fortune 1933, which relied on Kreuger lore [4][5].
- **"Kreuger & Toll took over Jönköping & Vulcan in 1913."** The merger that created Swedish Match was in 1917 [11].
- **"He controlled three-quarters of the world's matches."** His own company said 60% (1930); contemporaries said about 66%. Use "about two-thirds" [12][13].
- **"$142 million of forged Italian bonds."** Not found. Sources give £21M–£28.7M face value, or about $100M [33].
- **"Price Waterhouse found he overstated assets by $250M."** The Princeton finding aid says about $250M of assets "had never existed". Fine to use, but credit the archive, not a PW quote [42].
- **"The Pecora hearings exposed Kreuger."** The Kreuger & Toll hearings (11–12 Jan 1933) were the Norbeck-era Stock Exchange Practices hearings [48].
- **"Kreuger's fraud created the SEC / the Securities Act."** It was one influential case among many. Say "helped push" or "helped shape" [49].
- **"He died in a hotel room."** He died in his own Paris flat, 5 Avenue Victor-Emmanuel III. SNL and Dagens PS get this wrong [37].
- **"He was definitely murdered" / "he faked his death."** The official finding was suicide, and murder is a family/author theory [38].
- **Keynes quote ("the greatest constructive business intelligence of his age").** No source found attributing it to Keynes. Fortune 1933 attributes "a man of great constructive intelligence and wide vision" to *The Economist*. Do not use it as Keynes.
- **"I've built my enterprise on the firmest ground ... the foolishness of people."** No contemporary source found. Treat it as apocryphal.
- **"The Leonardo of their craft" (Galbraith).** Not verified in the text of *The Great Crash, 1929*. Use only after checking the book.
- **"Paid 20–25% dividends every year."** Only a book summary gives this. The Princeton archive says dividends were paid out of capital after 1924 [23].
- **"$75 billion fortune in today's money."** It comes from a single source with an unclear method [52].
