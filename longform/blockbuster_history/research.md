## 4. Late fees
[11] Extended viewing ("late") fees: $583.9M in 1998 (15.0% of revenue), $692.6M in 1999 (15.5%). — Blockbuster 10-K FY2000 (above), MD&A — VERIFIED (primary).
[12] 2000: "nearly $800 million in late fees, 16 percent of its revenue"; 2009: $134M (3%). — NBC News/AP "Hubris — and late fees — doomed Blockbuster", Sept 2010 https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna39332696 ; UPI/Washington Post 2003 https://www.upi.com/Business_News/2003/08/30/Late-fees-key-to-movie-rental-business/16201062257151/ — VERIFIED-secondary (consistent with [11] trend; 2000 was last year disclosed).

## 5. Netflix founding and the Apollo 13 story
[13] Netflix incorporated Aug 29, 1997 (Delaware), began operations (website) April 14, 1998; subscription launched Sept 1999; no late fees. — Netflix 10-K FY2002, SEC https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000095016803001155/d10k.htm — VERIFIED.
[14] Hastings told the "$40 late fee for Apollo 13" origin story (e.g. "The genesis of Netflix came in 1997 when I got this late fee, about $40, for Apollo 13"). Randolph's book calls it "a convenient fiction"; the idea came from months of commute brainstorming (first test: mailing a CD to Hastings' house). — NPR excerpt of "Netflixed"/coverage https://www.npr.org/2013/01/30/170673232/excerpt-netflixed ; WBUR On Point w/ Randolph, Nov 7 2019 https://www.wbur.org/onpoint/2019/11/07/netflix-marc-rudolph-that-will-never-work ; Quartz Aug 2017 https://qz.com/1062888/ — DISPUTED: Hastings maintains the fee happened; Randolph says it was a story simplification, not the origin.

## 6. The 2000 meeting in Dallas
[15] Hastings (No Rules Rules): early 2000, Dallas; "Blockbuster is a thousand times our size"; Netflix had ~100 employees and ~300,000 subscribers; proposed that Blockbuster buy Netflix and Netflix run Blockbuster's online arm (Blockbuster.com); price $50M; Antioco "flatly declined." "That year alone, our losses would total $57 million." — Marketplace excerpt of No Rules Rules, Sept 8, 2020 https://www.marketplace.org/story/2020/09/08/ceo-reed-hastings-on-how-netflix-beat-blockbuster — VERIFIED (as Hastings' account).
[16] Randolph (That Will Never Work, 2019): Hastings: "We should join forces... We will run the online part of the combined business. You will focus on the stores." Ed Stead: "If we were to buy you, what are you thinking? ... a number." Hastings: "Fifty million." "John Antioco was struggling not to laugh." Antioco: "The dot-com hysteria is completely overblown." "A long, quiet ride back to the airport." Attendees per Randolph: Hastings, Randolph, CFO Barry McCarthy; Antioco, Stead. — The Independent (via Yahoo), Nathan Place, Feb 7, 2022 https://www.yahoo.com/news/blockbuster-ceo-struggled-not-laugh-191553438.html ; excerpt in Vanity Fair, Sept 2019; Inc. (dates meeting Sept 2000) — VERIFIED as Randolph's account. Month DISPUTED: Hastings "early 2000" vs Inc./Randolph "September 2000".
[17] Netflix 2000 financials: net loss $57.363M; operating loss $57.557M; subscription revenue $35.9M; marketing = 72% of revenue. — Netflix 10-K FY2002 (above) — VERIFIED.
[18] Antioco's rebuttal (LinkedIn, 2022): he "was not a part of that meeting, although I did stop by to greet our guests"; "there were no serious conversations about our buying the nascent Netflix business"; no one "thought their company was worth anything close to that valuation." — Next TV, Daniel Frankel, June 3, 2022 https://www.nexttv.com/news/blockbusters-famous-netflix-2002-rebuff-that-didnt-happen-says-video-chains-former-ceo ; Newsweek fact check (rates the $50M offer "True") https://newsweek.com/fact-check-did-blockbuster-turn-downchance-buy-netflix-50-million-1575557 — DISPUTED (who was in the room; how serious it was).

## 9. Blockbuster Online and End of Late Fees
[23] BLOCKBUSTER Online (US) launched Aug 2004 (UK May 2004); >750,000 subscribers by Mar 9, 2005; goal >2M by Q1 2006. — Blockbuster 10-K FY2004 (above) — VERIFIED.
[24] Announced Dec 2004: "end of late fees" from Jan 1, 2005 at >4,500 company stores + ~550 franchise stores (Canada from Jan 29, 2005). Rentals still had due dates; after a week the item was sold to the customer, then a $1.25 restocking fee if returned. — Blockbuster 10-K FY2004; CNNMoney Feb 18, 2005 https://money.cnn.com/2005/02/18/news/midcaps/blockbuster_suit/ — VERIFIED.
[25] Cost: Antioco wanted "$200 million to launch Blockbuster Online and another $200 million to eliminate late fees" (HBR, Apr 2011, p.42, as quoted); a 2005 press estimate put lost late-fee operating income at $250–300M. In 2005 game late-fee elimination alone cut game rental revenue by $62.8M. — HuffPost review quoting HBR https://www.huffpost.com/entry/the-fall-of-blockbuster-v_b_921031 ; Home Media Magazine https://www.homemediamagazine.com/blockbuster/icahn-antioco-opine-blockbuster-fall-23394 ; Blockbuster 10-K FY2005 (above) — DISPUTED ($200M vs $250–300M; use "around $200 million a year in fees").
[26] Lawsuits: New Jersey AG sued Feb 2005 (Mercer County) calling the "No More Late Fees" ads deceptive; settled Jan 2006 (> $90,000 customer credits + $50,000 to state). Separate March 2005 settlement with 47 AGs (CA + 46 states + DC): $630,000 and ad changes. — NJ AG press release Jan 6, 2006 https://www.nj.gov/oag/newsreleases06/pr20060106a.html ; California AG https://oag.ca.gov/node/29594 — VERIFIED.

## Myths to avoid
- "Blockbuster could have bought Netflix outright and laughed in their faces": the offer was Netflix selling itself for $50M AND running Blockbuster's online arm (Hastings, Randolph). The laughter was suppressed ("struggling not to laugh"), and Antioco says he only greeted the guests and no serious talks happened. Present as "according to Netflix's founders".
- Apollo 13 $40 late fee as the literal origin: disputed by co-founder Randolph ("a convenient fiction").
- "Blockbuster was a $6bn company in 2000": 2000 revenue was $4.96bn; $6.05bn was the 2004 peak.
- "60,000 employees": 10-Ks say ~95,800 (2000) and ~84,300 (2004).
- "Blockbuster ignored the internet": it launched Online (2004), dropped late fees (2005), and Total Access (2006) briefly beat Netflix's growth; the reversal came after the Icahn fight and the CEO change in 2007.
- Keyes quote is real but was about digital rivals (Walmart/Apple), Dec 2008.
- Debt at bankruptcy: "nearly $1 billion", not a precise $900M.
- DISH "paid $320M": that was the bid value; cash paid was ~$226M.
