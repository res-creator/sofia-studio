# Long-form "Why Airlines Sue People for Buying Cheap Tickets" — research (all pages opened)

## Pricing
- Airlines price by market (origin–destination), not distance or cost: "airlines don't price based on cost". Hubs mix high-fare nonstop and low-fare connecting passengers; hidden-city use could make flights unprofitable → fewer frequencies, smaller planes, dropped routes. Example (July 10 search, 2015): LAX→Washington Dulles nonstop $588.10; LAX→Boston via Dulles $215.60 (skip Boston); LAX→Morgantown WV via Dulles $677.10. — Cranky Flier (Brett Snyder), Jan 5 2015, https://crankyflier.com/?p=14184
- Works only one-way, no checked bags (bags go to the final destination). — Fortune 2014 (below); academic: "only one-way passengers are eligible" (search)

## Skiplagged vs United/Orbitz
- Skiplagged started 2013 by Aktarer Zaman, aged 20. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skiplagged
- Nov 2014 United + Orbitz sued him (unfair competition), sought $75,000 lost revenue; Zaman 22; "As a 22-year-old with a startup being bullied by these large corporations, your support means so much to me"; "[Hidden city ticketing] has been around for a while… It just hasn't been very accessible to consumers" (to CNN); GoFundMe $16,718 of $20,000 goal at Dec 30 2014. — https://fortune.com/2014/12/30/united-airlines-orbitz-in-legal-brawl-with-22-year-old-travel-entrepreneur
- $33,000 from 1,600+ donors by Dec 31 2014. — https://www.wtvr.com/2014/12/31/aktarer-zaman-skiplagged-raises-33000-in-united-airlines-fight ; later reports "almost $50,000 / 2,300+" (search snippets only — say "tens of thousands")
- ~Apr 30 2015: federal judge dismissed United's suit — Illinois not the proper venue (procedural, no merits). — https://yro.slashdot.org/story/15/05/01/2240255/judge-tosses-united-airlines-lawsuit-over-hidden-city-tickets ; Orbitz settled (site stopped linking to Orbitz) — Wikipedia
- 2021 Southwest sued, settled. — Wikipedia

## Airlines vs passengers
- United Corporate Security letter (published Oct 14 2018): "Notice of Claim Pertaining to Point Beyond Ticketing and Demand for Reimbursement"; 38 instances since Jan 2016; called it "fraud", Rule 6; demanded $3,236.76 within 10 business days; threatened "an outside collection agency", MileagePlus termination, refusal of carriage (Rule 21). — https://viewfromthewing.com/2018/10/14/united-airlines-threatens-to-trash-the-credit-of-passengers-who-skip-purchased-flights/
- Lufthansa: passenger booked Oslo–Frankfurt–Seattle–Frankfurt–Oslo (~$716) instead of Berlin–Frankfurt–Seattle–Frankfurt–Berlin-type trip (~$3,000), skipped Frankfurt→Oslo on return, flew to Berlin; Lufthansa sued for €2,112; Berlin district court ruled against Lufthansa (Dec 2018; unclear calculation, skeptical of charging for a paid service not fully used); Lufthansa withdrew appeal → ruling binding (Oct 2019). Lawyer Dr. Matthias Böse: Lufthansa "has realized that the version of its conditions of carriage used is incompatible with German law." Not a precedent. — https://liveandletsfly.com/2019/10/07/lufthansa-loses-lawsuit/ ; https://www.advant-nctm.com/en/news/hidden-city-ticketing-risks-and-possible-forms-of-protection-for-airlines-in-light-of-the-recent-lufthansa-case
- July 2023: 17-year-old, Gainesville FL → NYC via Charlotte (his home); stopped/questioned at check-in (NC licence); $150 ticket cancelled; family paid $400 direct; father Hunter Parsons: "banned from AA for three years" (AA didn't confirm length); AA policy: prohibits "purchasing a ticket without intending to fly all flights to gain lower fares". — https://www.fox29.com/news/american-airlines-bans-north-carolina-teen-who-tried-popular-travel-hack (Jul 20 2023) + search
- Skiplagging is not illegal; it violates contracts of carriage. — Fox29/search; Cranky Flier

## American v. Skiplagged
- AA sued 2023; Oct 2024 Fort Worth federal jury: $9.4M = $4.7M copyright (scraping flight schedules against terms) + $4.7M disgorgement of Skiplagged revenue (based on lost-fare estimate); trademark/logo claim rejected; ~half what AA's lawyers asked; judgment finalized May 2025. — https://gizmodo.com/american-airlines-wins-9-4-million-from-skiplagged-site-that-exploits-airlines-business-model-2000514406 ; Wikipedia; search (Dallas News, Law360)
- After the verdict the site still showed AA flights (without logos) — search snippet (Skift) — say "reportedly".
