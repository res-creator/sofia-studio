The hunt ran from about April to July 1902. Only the tail had to be handed in; the bounty is reported as 1 cent per tail (accounts differ — "a few cents" elsewhere). Peak day: more than 20,000 rats. Tailless rats were seen, and rat farms were found on the outskirts. Re-enactment with generic characters, not portraits of real people.
Lesson: pay for the result you want (fewer rats), not for something people can simply make more of (tails).

YOUTUBE SHORTS
Title: Hanoi Paid People for Rat Tails. It Backfired (1902)
Description: In 1902, the French colonial administration in Hanoi had a rat problem: the brand-new sewers under the French quarter had become rat highways, rats climbed out of toilets, and plague was spreading across Asia. The city offered a bounty for every rat killed — but you only had to hand in the tail (reportedly one cent each). At the peak, more than 20,000 tails came in a single day. Then officials spotted rats running around with no tails: people cut off the tail, let the rat go and let it breed more tails. Inspectors even found rat farms outside the city. The hunt failed, and the plague came anyway. Not hunters — money: pay for tails, and you get tails, not fewer rats. (Illustrated re-enactment; generic characters.)
