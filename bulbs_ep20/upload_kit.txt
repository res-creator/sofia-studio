PINNED COMMENT
Sources: IEEE Spectrum, "The Great Lightbulb Conspiracy" (2014) — the Phoebus cartel was set up in Geneva on Dec 23, 1924 by Osram (Germany), Philips (Netherlands), Compagnie des Lampes (France), Tungsram (Hungary), AEI (Britain) and Tokyo Electric (Japan), with General Electric (USA) taking part through its overseas affiliates; the standard life was set at 1,000 hours (1,500–2,000 hours had been common); factories sent sample bulbs to a testing lab in Switzerland and were fined if bulbs burned longer (or shorter) than allowed; average life fell from about 1,800 hours (1926) to 1,205 hours (1933–34); costs fell while prices barely moved; the deal was meant to run until 1955 but ended in 1940 in the war (some sources say 1939). EC&M, "Lightbulb Cartel: A Dark Spot in Lighting History" — same facts. A British commission (1951) said the aim to shorten bulb life was alleged but not proven. All people in the video are generic drawings (re-enactment), not portraits.

YOUTUBE SHORTS
Title: The Secret Deal to Make Lightbulbs Worse (Geneva, 1924)
Description: In 1924, the world's biggest lightbulb makers — from Germany, the Netherlands, France, Britain and Japan, plus an American giant working through its overseas arms — made a secret deal in Geneva. Good bulbs back then could burn up to 2,000 hours; the new rule was 1,000, max. Every factory mailed sample bulbs to a lab in Switzerland, and if a bulb lasted too long, the factory paid a fine. Real bulb life dropped by about a third. Bulbs got cheaper to make, but prices barely moved — because every bulb that burns out is a bulb you buy again. The deal only died in 1940, when war tore it apart. (Illustrated re-enactment; generic characters.)
