TIKTOK CAPTION
Same $5 coffee. Same day. So how could it end up costing $40? ☕ Under Wells Fargo's old system in California (2000s), card purchases were processed biggest first — so one overdraft could turn into three fees. The $35 fee is an illustrative example. Did you know banks could pick the order?
#overdraft #banking #wellsfargo #personalfinance #money #didyouknow

PINNED COMMENT
Sources: Gutierrez v. Wells Fargo Bank — Ninth Circuit opinion (2012), which describes the high-to-low posting order and a customer who was charged $506 in overdraft fees for going $120 over; Ninth Circuit memorandum (Oct 29, 2014) affirming the ~$203 million restitution award. Note: the appeals court said federal law governs the posting order itself; the award was upheld on the claims that customers were misled about it. This was a historical practice in California, not how the bank works today. Today, US banks need your opt-in before charging overdraft fees on ATM and one-time debit card purchases (Regulation E, since 2010) — worth checking your overdraft settings.
