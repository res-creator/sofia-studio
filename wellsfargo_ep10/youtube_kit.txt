TITLE (63 characters)
How a $5 Coffee Turned Into $40 in Bank Fees (Wells Fargo)

ALTERNATIVE TITLE (for an A/B test)
Same Purchases, 3x the Overdraft Fees: Wells Fargo's Old System

DESCRIPTION
Coffee $5, lunch $12, gas $28, groceries $90 — and only $100 in the account. Process the purchases in order and you get one overdraft fee. Process the biggest first and you get three.

That's what Wells Fargo did with debit card purchases in California in the 2000s. In the court case Gutierrez v. Wells Fargo, one customer went $120 over and was charged $506 in fees. After years of appeals, a $203 million restitution award was upheld.

The $35 fee in the video is an illustrative example. This was a historical practice, not how the bank works today. Today US banks need your opt-in before charging overdraft fees on ATM and one-time debit card purchases.

Sources: Ninth Circuit opinion in Gutierrez v. Wells Fargo Bank (2012) and Ninth Circuit memorandum (2014).
