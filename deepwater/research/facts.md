## 1. Place and setup

- **Location:** Mississippi Canyon Block 252 (MC 252), Macondo prospect, at 28°44′N, 88°21′W (JIT1 PDF p. 4). **[CONTESTED, distance]** The figures differ by source:
  - "approximately 48 miles from the nearest shoreline, 114 miles from … Port Fourchon … 154 miles from the Houma … helicopter base" (JIT2 PDF p. 24). **Best single figure.**
  - "approximately 50 miles from Louisiana, the nearest shoreline" (CSB1 PDF p. 12, §1.3).
  - "approximately 41 nautical miles off the Southeast coast of Louisiana" (≈47 statute miles) (JIT1 App. E, PDF p. 163).
  - "49 miles off the Louisiana coast" (COMM p. viii, foreword). The same report's photo caption on p. 1 says "40 miles", and JIT1 PDF p. 9 also says "approximately 40 miles".
  - The Coast Guard's 22:05 broadcast gave "45NM ESE of South Pass, LA" (JIT1 App. H-17).
  - Recommended narration: "about 50 miles off Louisiana".
- **Water depth:** 4,992 ft (COMM p. 3). CSB says "just short of 5,000 feet" (CSB1 PDF p. 13).
- **Well depth:** drilling stopped April 9, 2010, at **18,360 ft below sea level**, short of the planned 19,650 ft (CSB1 PDF p. 25). That is roughly 13,400 ft below the seafloor (my arithmetic). COMM rounds it to an "18,000-foot-deep Macondo well" (p. 1 caption) and a "two-and-a-half-mile-deep" well (p. 2). The open hole ran from 17,168 to 18,360 ft (JIT2 PDF p. 182).
- **Rig:** Deepwater Horizon was a mobile offshore drilling unit and a semisubmersible "owned by Transocean" (COMM p. 2). It was "dynamically positioned", using "global satellite technology and thrusters to maintain position … rather than cables and anchors" (CSB1 PDF p. 14). Other particulars:
  - Size 396 × 256 ft, with housing for 146 people (CSB1 p. 14).
  - Built by Hyundai Heavy Industries in Ulsan, South Korea, and delivered Feb 23, 2001. Flagged in the Marshall Islands. Rated for 8,000 ft of water (JIT1 PDF p. 163).
  - Leased to BP through 2013 at $496,800 a day bareboat, or about $1M a day all-in (JIT1 PDF p. 163).
  - **[CONTESTED, value]** COMM says "built for $350 million" (p. 2), but its own caption on p. 1 calls it "The $560-million-dollar rig". The second figure is probably a replacement value; that is not stated.
- **Companies:** BP was the operator and held the lease with Anadarko and MOEX (JIT2 PDF p. 24). Transocean owned and crewed the rig. Halliburton did the cementing, and its Sperry-Sun unit did mudlogging. Cameron designed the BOP. M-I SWACO supplied the mud (JIT2 PDF p. 7; COMM p. 3).
- **History:** the Transocean rig Marianas spudded the well on Oct 7, 2009. Hurricane Ida damaged the Marianas on Nov 9, 2009, and Deepwater Horizon arrived on Jan 31, 2010 (JIT2 PDF p. 24; COMM pp. 2–3).
- **People on board:** **126**, employed by 13 companies (CSB1 PDF p. 14). JIT1 App. H-20 (22:35) calls this "the first accurate accounting of the POB". An earlier Coast Guard broadcast wrongly said "approximately 144 POB" (H-17).
  - **Died: 11** ("missing and presumed deceased"). **Survived: 115** (JIT1 PDF p. 9).
  - **[CONTESTED]** JIT1 and COMM (p. 18 caption) give **16** injured. The CSB report titles say "11 Fatalities, 17 Injured".

## 2. Barriers, in plain language

- **The three barriers.** COMM p. 115: "Three things could have contained those pressures: the cement at the bottom of the well, the mud in the well and in the riser, and the blowout preventer. But mistakes and failures to appreciate risk compromised each of those potential barriers."
- **Drilling mud:** heavy fluid whose weight balances the pressure of the reservoir (COMM p. 1). Temporary abandonment meant removing it. BP's plan replaced mud with seawater down to 8,367 ft below sea level, so the bottom-hole pressure would end up about 2,350 psi lower than before (CSB1 Fig. 2-4; COMM p. 107).
  - The well became **underbalanced at about 8:50 p.m.** (Transocean model) or **8:52 p.m.** (BP model). From then on, mud no longer held back the reservoir (CCR p. 176). **[APPROX]**
- **Cement at the bottom:**
  - Halliburton pumped nitrogen-foamed cement, finishing at 12:40 a.m. on April 20 (COMM p. 102).
  - JIT2 PDF p. 6: "a central cause of the blowout was failure of a cement barrier in the production casing string".
  - BP sent home the Schlumberger crew booked to run cement evaluation logs, saving the $128,000 fee (COMM p. 4). The decision rested on a "decision tree" whose main criterion was lost returns during cementing (COMM pp. 102–103).
- **Negative pressure test:**
  - Purpose: "reduce the pressure inside the well in order to simulate its state after the Deepwater Horizon had packed up and moved on. If pressure increased … or if fluids flowed up from the well, that would indicate a well integrity problem" (COMM p. 4).
  - Pass criterion: "the pressure must remain at 0 psi when the pipe is closed after the pressure is bled off" (COMM p. 107), with "no flow or pressure buildup for a sustained period of time" (COMM p. 108).
  - No standard existed. COMM p. 119: "there was no standard procedure for running or interpreting the test in either MMS regulations or written industry protocols … the regulations and standards did not require BP to run a negative-pressure test at all", and "BP and Transocean had no internal procedures … and had not formally trained their personnel".
- **Blowout preventer (BOP):**
  - A "massive, 360-ton device installed at the top of the well" on the seafloor (JIT2 PDF p. 9), so about 5,000 ft below the rig. It was connected to the rig by the riser.
  - It held annular preventers and pipe rams (variable bore rams for 3½–6⅝ in pipe), a casing shear ram, and the **blind shear ram (BSR)**, "specially designed blades" meant to cut the drill pipe and seal the well (CSB2 PDF p. 19; CSB-ES p. 12 n.10).
  - **AMF/deadman:** an automatic mode function meant to close the BSR when power, communications and hydraulics to the rig are lost. It runs through two redundant control pods, yellow and blue (CSB-ES p. 12; COMM p. 115).
  - **Autoshear:** closes the BSR if the lower marine riser package separates from the BOP stack. An ROV triggered it on April 22 (JIT2 PDF p. 10).
  - **EDS (emergency disconnect):** the button on the bridge that should close the BSR and unlatch the rig (COMM p. 114).
  - The CSB adds that the BSR "could not reliably shear the 6⅝" drillpipe used during all of the DWH drilling operations except on April 20" (CSB2 PDF p. 34; CSB-ES p. 13 #12).
- **Diverter:** the crew could route flow to the mud-gas separator or overboard. Its default route was to the mud-gas separator (MGS) (CSB-ES p. 14 #17).

**"Bladder effect" box.** Witness statements from both well site leaders say Anderson attributed the 1,400 psi to a "bladder effect": heavy mud above the annular "exerting pressure on the annular preventer, which in turn transmitted pressure to the drill pipe" (COMM p. 107, per trainee Lee Lambert). Lambert "did not recall anyone agreeing or disagreeing".
- The court found it "not known to exist in the context of negative pressure tests" (PH1 ¶257). "Not a single witness had ever heard of" it (¶284).
- **[CONTESTED]** Who originated it. PH1 ¶289 says "there are reasons to question whether the 'bladder effect' originated with Jason Anderson". CSB3 PDF p. 54 says it is "in dispute whether the entire on-duty well operations crew and both Well Site Leaders … accepted this rationale".
- Alternative explanation: the unusual lost-circulation-material spacer "possibly plugged the kill line" (JIT2 PDF pp. 93, 115; COMM p. 119).

## 4. Why the BOP failed to stop the blowout

- **CSB finding (2014/2016):**
  - "The AMF/deadman system most likely functioned on the day of the incident, but buckled off-center drillpipe in the BOP prohibited the BSR from fully closing and sealing the well." (CSB-ES p. 13 #4)
  - "The drillpipe buckled due to effective compression, a phenomenon which occurs when large pressure differences between the inside and outside of the drillpipe are established." (CSB-ES p. 13 #5)
  - The pipe-ram closure at about 9:47 p.m. "created the pressure difference necessary" (CSB2 PDF p. 33).
  - "The BSR punctured and partially severed the pressurized drillpipe, causing flow to resume rapidly." (CSB2 PDF p. 34)
  - CSB calls this "a buckling mechanism not yet identified by other investigative reports" (CSB2 PDF p. 34).
- **Control pods (CSB):**
  - Blue pod "miswired, causing a critical battery to drain and rendering the pod inoperable".
  - In the yellow pod, solenoid valve Y103 was miswired so its two coils "oppose one another". A drained battery "likely rendered one of these coils inoperable, allowing the other coil to activate alone and initiate closure of the BSR" (CSB-ES p. 12 #2a–b).
  - These faults "could not have passed the manufacturer's factory acceptance testing" (CSB-ES p. 14 #14).
- **DNV forensic examination for BOEMRE (Report EP030842, March 2011), as summarized in JIT2:**
  - The pipe was "positioned such that the outside corner of the upper BSR blade contacted the drill pipe slightly off center … trapped between the ram block faces", leaving an estimated **2.8-inch gap** (JIT2 PDF pp. 143–144).
  - Cause: "elastic buckling". JIT2 says it was "caused by the force of the hydrocarbons blowing out of the well; by the weight of the 5,000 feet of drill pipe located in the riser … or by a combination of both" (JIT2 PDF pp. 10, 145).
  - DNV "concluded that the most likely scenario for the activation of the blind shear ram was from the autoshear circuit" on April 22 (JIT2 PDF p. 144).
- **[CONTESTED] When the BSR closed and why the pipe was off-center.**
  - CSB: the AMF fired on April 20. JIT/DNV: most likely the autoshear on April 22.
  - CSB: effective compression. DNV/JIT: blowout forces or pipe weight. CSB finds the weight theory "unlikely but cannot definitively rule it out" (CSB2 PDF p. 32).
  - CSB2 PDF p. 33: "Video evidence supports the activation of the autoshear function on April 22, but it does not preclude previous closure of the BSR … on April 20."
  - **[CONTESTED] Deadman.** COMM (written before DNV) said "the deadman failed too", citing "low battery charges in one pod and defective solenoid valves in the other". COMM explicitly declined to determine whether the BOP failed to operate as designed (COMM p. 115 and note).
- **Why the crew's own BOP actions came late:** "Flow rates at this point may have been too high for either the annular preventer or a variable bore ram to seal the well. (Earlier kick detection would have improved the odds of success.)" (COMM p. 114). The CSB says the riser erupted "only 2-3 minutes after hydrocarbons entered it" (CSB-ES p. 14 #20a).
- **No single cause.**
  - COMM p. vii: "The immediate causes of the Macondo well blowout can be traced to a series of identifiable mistakes made by BP, Halliburton, and Transocean that reveal such systematic failures in risk management that they place in doubt the safety culture of the entire industry."
  - COMM p. 122: "the root causes are systemic … The most significant failure at Macondo—and the clear root cause of the blowout—was a failure of industry management."
  - COMM p. 115: "We may never know the precise extent to which each of these missteps and oversights in fact caused the accident."
  - CSB-ES p. 12: "a complex, closely connected interplay of technical, human, organizational, and regulatory factors."
  - CSB2 PDF p. 31: "If either the bottom hole cement job had been successful or the BOP had functioned that day, the blowout could have been avoided."

## 5. Rescue

- **Damon B. Bankston**, a 260-ft Tidewater supply vessel captained by **Alwin J. Landry**, was alongside taking mud. About 3,100 bbl had been transferred by 17:17 (JIT1 H-12).
  - The crew cut loose the hoses, backed off to 500 m, and launched their fast rescue craft. It pulled jumpers from the water, including Gregory Meche, before either lifeboat launched (COMM p. 12; JIT1 H-17).
  - All 115 survivors ended up on the Bankston (JIT1 PDF p. 76). Lifeboat 1 was the last to leave; its last occupant stepped off at 23:14 (JIT1 H-17, H-21).
  - The recreational boat *Ramblin' Wreck* also searched (COMM p. 10). JIT recommends awards for the Bankston master and crew, the Ramblin' Wreck crew, and the DWH chief engineer (JIT1 PDF p. 147).

- **Search:** active search and rescue ran "until approximately 1900 on April 23, 2010 when the active Search and Rescue (SAR) efforts were suspended" (JIT1 PDF p. 66). That is about 69 hours, spread over four calendar days (Apr 20–23). The 11 "were never found" (JIT1 PDF p. 16).

## 6. Aftermath

- **Sinking:** "At 1026 on April 22, DEEPWATER HORIZON sank" (JIT1 PDF p. 9), about 36 hours after the explosion (COMM p. 18).
- **Flow:**
  - Capped at about 2:25 p.m. on **July 15, 2010**: "For the first time in 87 days, no oil flowed into the Gulf" (COMM p. 165).
  - Static kill succeeded Aug 3–4 and cementing followed (COMM pp. 166–167).
  - "permanently sealed on September 19, 2010" (JIT2 PDF p. 29) after the relief well. Relief wells began May 2 and May 17 (COMM p. 132).
- **Volume [CONTESTED]:**
  - Government "Oil Budget" (Aug 4, 2010): about **4.9 million barrels**, ±10%, from flow rates of 62,200 bbl/day falling to 52,700 bbl/day (COMM p. 167 and note).
  - COMM also notes BP suggested the estimate was "20 to 50 percent too high".
  - **Court:** "4.0 million barrels of oil released from the reservoir". After deducting 810,000 bbl collected, **"3.19 million barrels of oil discharged into the Gulf of Mexico"** for the Clean Water Act penalty (PH2 pp. 39, 43–44, ¶¶255, 273, 277).
  - EPA uses "Four million barrels … flowed" (EPA-DWH). NOAA uses "approximately 134 million gallons" (= 3.19M bbl) (NOAA).
- **Area:** "oil was detected on more than 43,300 square miles of the ocean, an area about the size of the state of Virginia" (cumulative), and "more than 1,300 miles of shoreline" from eastern Texas to the Florida Panhandle (NOAA).
  - At the June 2 peak, federal fishing closures covered "nearly 37 percent of the Gulf zone" (COMM p. 140).
  - The response peaked at "over 45,000 people" (COMM p. 129 caption).
- **Criminal and civil outcomes:**
  - **BP Exploration & Production:** pleaded guilty to **14 counts**: 11 felony seaman's manslaughter, 1 felony obstruction of Congress, plus Clean Water Act and Migratory Bird Treaty Act counts. **$4 billion** and 5 years' probation; plea accepted Jan 29, 2013 by Judge Sarah Vance (DOJ-VNS). The plea agreement is dated Nov 15, 2012 (EPA-DWH).
  - **Transocean Deepwater Inc.:** pleaded guilty to one misdemeanor Clean Water Act count. **$400M criminal + $1B civil** (Jan 3, 2013). It admitted that crew "acting at the direction of BP's 'Well Site Leaders' … were negligent in failing fully to investigate clear indications that the Macondo well was not secure" (EPA-TO).
  - **Phase One ruling (Sept 4, 2014):** BP's conduct "reckless"/grossly negligent; Transocean and Halliburton negligent; fault **BP 67%, Transocean 30%, Halliburton 3%** (PH1 ¶¶545, 613).
  - **BP civil consent decree** (lodged Oct 5, 2015):
    - $5.5B Clean Water Act penalty, 80% to the Gulf under the RESTORE Act.
    - $8.1B natural resource damages, including $1B already committed, plus up to $700M for unknown conditions.
    - $250M for response costs, lost royalties and a False Claims Act matter (DOJ-CD; EPA-DWH: "up to $8.8 billion in natural resource damages").
    - **[UNVERIFIED]** The "$20.8 billion" total (with state economic claims) and court entry on Apr 4, 2016 are widely reported but were not confirmed in a document I opened.
  - **Others:** MOEX $90M (Feb 17, 2012); Anadarko $159.5M penalty ruling (Nov 30, 2015) (EPA-DWH).
  - **[UNVERIFIED, press only]**
    - Halliburton pleaded guilty (2013) to one misdemeanor for destroying post-spill cement review records: $200,000 fine and 3 years' probation.
    - Well site leaders Kaluza and Vidrine were charged with 11 counts of seaman's manslaughter (DOJ-VNS confirms the charges). The manslaughter counts were dismissed (Dec 2015). Vidrine pleaded to a misdemeanor Clean Water Act count (10 months' probation). Kaluza was acquitted (Feb 2016).
    - BP executive David Rainey was acquitted (2015).
- **Regulatory changes:**
  - MMS was renamed BOEMRE "two months after the Macondo incident" (June 2010). Revenue collection moved to the **Office of Natural Resources Revenue (ONRR) on Oct 1, 2010**. **BOEM** (leasing) and **BSEE** (safety and environment) were created from BOEMRE in **October 2011** (CSB4 PDF p. 130; CSB1 PDF p. 11).
    - Note: the CSB glossary misprints the BOEMRE dates as 2011.
  - **Well Control Rule:** 81 FR 25888, published Apr 29, 2016, "effective on July 28, 2016". It covers BOP systems, well design, real-time monitoring and containment (FR2016).
  - **[UNVERIFIED]** BSEE revised the rule in 2019. Its May 2018 proposal is documented; the 2019 final rule was not checked.

## Summary

**Best quotable lines**
- "The explosive loss of the Macondo well could have been prevented." (COMM p. vii)
- "a series of identifiable mistakes … that reveal such systematic failures in risk management that they place in doubt the safety culture of the entire industry." (COMM p. vii)
- "The pressure data were not ambiguous." (COMM p. 119)
- "But, who cares, it's done, end of story, [we] will probably be fine…" (BP engineer Brett Cocales' email, COMM p. 116)
- "this has been [a] nightmare well" (Morel email, COMM p. 2)
- "We have mud going to the crown." (Curtis, via Ezell, COMM p. 8)
- "I saw fire from derrick leg to derrick leg and as high as I could see." (Bertone, COMM p. 10)
- "kind of like a black rain." (Capt. Landry, COMM p. 12)
- "a 550-ton freight train hitting the rig floor" (Transocean representative, COMM p. 114)
- "for BP, the accident happened at 9:49 p.m. … the blowout began in early 2009" (Sears, COMM p. 223)
- "If either the bottom hole cement job had been successful or the BOP had functioned that day, the blowout could have been avoided." (CSB2)

**Uncertain or contested**
- Distance offshore (40–50 mi).
- Days behind schedule: 38 days or about six weeks. "43" has no primary source.
- Injured: 16 or 17.
- First explosion: 9:48, 9:49 or 9:50.
- Test declared good at 7:45 or 8:00.
- Who proposed the "bladder effect".
- EDS authorization sequence, and the general-alarm delay.
- BSR closure: April 20 (AMF) or April 22 (autoshear). Buckling: effective compression or flow forces/weight. Deadman: worked (CSB) or failed (COMM).
- Muster timing; first-helicopter time (11:10 vs 11:22 p.m.).
- Volume: 4.9M (government) vs 4.0M released / 3.19M discharged (court).
- Search area (5,200 sq mi, press only); the individual prosecutions (press only).
- The rig's value: $350M build cost vs the $560M in COMM's caption.
