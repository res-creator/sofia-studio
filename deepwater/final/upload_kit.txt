DESCRIPTION
On April 20, 2010, the crew of the Deepwater Horizon tested whether the Macondo well was sealed. One line showed no flow. The drill pipe showed 1,400 psi. Around 8 p.m., the test was declared a success. Less than two hours later, the well blew out, the rig exploded, and eleven men were killed.

Why was the well judged safe? And why didn't the blowout preventer on the seafloor stop it?

This documentary follows the evening in order: the rig and the well, the three barriers, the negative pressure test, the missed signs, the blowout, the escape and rescue, why the blowout preventer did not stop the flow, and what followed. It is told through Coast Guard archive footage, official investigation reports, and the testimony of Mike Williams, the rig's chief electronics technician, at the joint investigation hearing in July 2010.

What was known: the well had been difficult, Halliburton's own lab tests had shown the cement mix unstable, the cement had not been independently evaluated, and the test readings did not agree.
What investigators concluded later: the pressure data were not ambiguous, and the warning signs were missed.

In memory of Jason Anderson, Aaron Dale Burkeen, Donald Clark, Stephen Curtis, Gordon Jones, Roy Wyatt Kemp, Karl Dale Kleppinger Jr., Blair Manuel, Dewey Revette, Shane Roshto and Adam Weise.
