DESCRIPTION
Two hours before Deepwater Horizon exploded, the crew ran a test to prove the Macondo well was sealed. If it was, the drill pipe should have read zero. It read 1,400 psi. The test was declared a success.

What went wrong that evening, why the well was judged safe, and why the blowout preventer on the seafloor failed to stop the blowout, told through U.S. Coast Guard archive footage, the official investigation reports, and the testimony of survivor Mike Williams, the rig's chief electronics technician.

What was known before: the well had been difficult, Halliburton's own lab tests had shown the cement mix unstable, the cement had not been independently evaluated, and the test readings did not agree.
What investigators concluded later: the pressure data were not ambiguous, the warning signs were missed, and there was no single cause.

In memory of the eleven men who were killed on April 20, 2010: Jason Anderson, Aaron Dale Burkeen, Donald Clark, Stephen Curtis, Gordon Jones, Roy Wyatt Kemp, Karl Dale Kleppinger Jr., Blair Manuel, Dewey Revette, Shane Roshto and Adam Weise.
